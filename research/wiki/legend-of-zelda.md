The Legend of Zelda (Nintendo, February 1986), designed and directed by Shigeru Miyamoto
and Takashi Tezuka, launched as a Family Computer Disk System title in Japan — more than a
year before its iconic gold-cartridge North American NES release.

## In this game

The Legend of Zelda is the **Level 10 cabinet**, teaching Q and P and completing Act 2's top
row (the "TOP ROW UNLOCKED" milestone). Its key-and-locked-door dungeon structure is a
deliberate metaphor: the player has been unlocking keys the whole game. See
`.claude/rules/levels.md` Level 10.

## The facts

- Designed and directed by Shigeru Miyamoto and Takashi Tezuka (Miyamoto produced; Tezuka
  wrote story/script). Released in Japan February 1986 as a Family Computer Disk System
  launch title; the North American NES cartridge release followed more than a year later
  (1987).
- **Correction:** the gold-colored cartridge and cutaway box design were specific to the
  **North American NES cartridge release**, not the original 1986 Japanese Famicom Disk
  System version. If any Keystroke Kingdom copy implies the gold cartridge was part of the
  original 1986 release, that's an NA-specific detail from the later cartridge version —
  flagged for awareness.
- The North American cartridge was one of the first NES games with an internal battery for
  saving game data — genuinely significant and confirmed.
