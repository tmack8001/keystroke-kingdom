Warren Robinett is the programmer who, in June 1979, hid his name inside a secret room
in [[atari-adventure|Adventure]] for the Atari 2600 — because Atari's policy at the time
denied developers credit or royalties — creating what's most widely cited as the first
video game easter egg.

## In this game

`adventure` typed on the title screen triggers `WARREN ROBINETT WAS HERE. 1979.` — this
is described in the hub doc as "the most thematically perfect egg in the game." The date
is correct as written because it names the year Robinett *hid* the message inside the
code (June 1979), not the year the game shipped (March 1980) — see
[[atari-adventure]] for the release-date detail. See [[hacker-culture]] and
[[easter-egg-culture]] for the wider design lineage this egg founded.

## The facts

- **June 1979:** Robinett submits the Adventure source code, containing the hidden
  room, to Atari management.
- **March 1980:** the game itself is released.
- Robinett's own account (Forbes interview) explains the motivation: Atari stripped
  developer credit from its games specifically to stop competitors from poaching
  programmers and to deny developers negotiating leverage — hiding his name was a
  direct response to that policy, not just a prank.
- This is the reference most widely cited as the origin point of the "video game easter
  egg" as a cultural practice — see [[easter-egg-culture]] for the synthesis of how this
  lineage runs forward to modern eggs.
