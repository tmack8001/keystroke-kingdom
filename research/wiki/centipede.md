Centipede (Atari, August 1981), designed by Dona Bailey and Ed Logg, used a deliberately
pastel color palette — one of the earliest arcade games designed by a woman — to feel
"visually arresting" and different from the dark, neon-heavy games of its era.

## In this game

Centipede is not an assigned Keystroke Kingdom cabinet, but its trackball input and pastel
palette are a notable counterpoint within the [[discrete-logic-era]]/early-sprite-hardware
period alongside [[tempest]] and [[q-bert]].

## The facts

- Developer: Atari — designed by Dona Bailey and Ed Logg. Development began July 1980; the
  arcade street release was August 1981.
- Visual style: pastel palette (greens, pinks, purples, soft yellows) — a deliberate choice
  by Bailey, in her own words, "to make the game compelling and look appealing in a dark
  arcade" and "visually arresting."
- Input: trackball (primary, distinctive for the era) plus fire button — one of few golden
  age games to use a trackball.
- Iconic: shooting a middle centipede segment splits it into two independent centipedes;
  scorpions poison mushrooms, causing centipedes to dive straight down.
- Dona Bailey is documented as one of the first female game designers at Atari.
