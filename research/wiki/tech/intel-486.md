The Intel i486, announced April 10, 1989, was the direct successor to the
[[intel-386|Intel 386]] and the first x86 processor to pack more than one million
transistors on a single chip — roughly 50 times the performance of the original IBM
PC's 8088.

## In this game

**Level 18 history card, word `486`.** Digits 4, 8, 6 are all available the instant 3
and 8 land at Level 18 (see `.claude/rules/levels/act4.md` Level 18) — 8 is the
bottleneck (4@17, 6@16 already unlocked). A deliberate pairing with the existing
[[intel-386]] card at the same level — the chip and its direct successor, side by side.

## The facts

- Intel announced the i486 at Spring Comdex on April 10, 1989. It integrated roughly
  1.2 million transistors — the first x86 chip to exceed one million — and ran at
  15–20 million instructions per second.
- It was the first tightly-pipelined x86 design and the first x86 chip with an
  integrated on-chip floating-point unit and cache, built as a direct performance
  successor to the 386 (this game's existing Level 18 card).
- The 486 remained Intel's flagship x86 processor until the Pentium's 1993 debut, and
  continued to be manufactured for embedded applications until 2007.
