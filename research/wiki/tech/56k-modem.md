56K modems, standardized as ITU-T V.90 in September 1998, were the fastest and last
generation of dial-up modems before broadband — capping a decade-long climb from 14.4k
(1993) through 28.8k (1994) and 33.6k (1996), and ending with the same screeching,
warbling handshake sound documented on [[dialup-modem]].

## The facts

- Before 56k, dial-up speeds climbed steadily: 14.4 kbit/s modems were standard in
  early 1993, 28.8k modems launched in 1994, and 33.6k modems followed in 1996.
- 56k modems arrived in 1998, but two competing, incompatible proprietary protocols
  (US Robotics' X2 and Rockwell/Lucent's K56Flex) caused real market confusion until
  the ITU published a unifying V.90 standard, approved in September 1998.
- V.90's specified maximum was 56,000 bit/s downstream and 33,600 bit/s upstream — but
  real-world phone-line conditions and FCC power limits meant the advertised 56k
  ceiling was never actually achieved in practice; most connections topped out lower.
