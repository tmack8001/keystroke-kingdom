Electric cars are not new — they are older than the traffic light and nearly as old as the
car itself. Robert Anderson built a crude electric carriage in the first half of the 1800s;
William Morrison's six-passenger electric wagon (Des Moines, ~1890) did 14 mph; by 1900
roughly **one in three** cars on American roads was electric; and the first vehicle of any
kind to pass 100 km/h was an electric one, *La Jamais Contente*, in April 1899. Gasoline
then won for most of a century, and electric came back through the 1997 Toyota Prius hybrid
and the 2010 Nissan LEAF / Chevy Volt.

## The facts

- **Robert Anderson** (Scottish) built a crude electric carriage in the first half of the
  1800s, powered by non-rechargeable primary cells. The US Department of Energy's own
  history calls him "a British inventor" who "developed the first crude electric carriage"
  and gives **no specific year** — dates circulating online (1832, 1839) are not supported by
  the DOE page. Treat the decade as approximate.
- **~1890 — William Morrison**, a chemist in Des Moines, Iowa, built "the first successful
  electric car" in the US per DOE: a six-passenger vehicle with a top speed of **14 mph**,
  "little more than an electrified wagon."
- **1898 — Ferdinand Porsche** built an electric car called the **P1**, and later the world's
  first hybrid electric car (DOE).
- **29 April 1899 — *La Jamais Contente*** ("The Never Satisfied"), a torpedo-shaped
  aluminium-alloy-bodied electric racer driven by Belgian Camille Jenatzy, reached
  **105.88 km/h** at Achères near Paris — the first land-speed record over 100 km/h, and it
  stood until 1902. The car survives in the museum collection at the Château de Compiègne.
- **~1900 — the heyday.** DOE: "By 1900, electric cars were at their heyday, accounting for
  around a third of all vehicles on the road." Thomas Edison believed electric was the
  superior technology and worked on better EV batteries.
- **1997 — Toyota Prius**, released in Japan, "became the world's first mass-produced hybrid
  electric vehicle" (DOE).
- **Late 2010 — the modern restart** in the US market: the Chevy Volt and the Nissan LEAF
  were released (DOE).
- **2025 — the crown changes hands.** BYD passed Tesla as the world's largest seller of
  all-electric vehicles for the first time: ~2.26 million BEVs (up ~28%) against Tesla's
  1.64 million (down ~8%).
