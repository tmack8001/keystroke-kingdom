Tim Berners-Lee proposed the World Wide Web at CERN in March 1989 and, in April 1993,
CERN made its underlying code available royalty-free to the world — the act that let it
become the free, open web.

## In this game

Level 9's history card spells `www` — three of the same brand-new letter, since W
unlocks at Level 9. Card text: *"Tim Berners-Lee invented the World Wide Web and gave it
away for free — to everyone, forever."* See [[computing-history-moments]] for the full
17-card table.

## The facts

- **March 1989:** Berners-Lee's original written proposal at CERN (then internally
  called "Mesh") — nothing built yet.
- **Late 1990:** first working browser/server built.
- **April 30, 1993:** CERN formally places WWW source code in the public domain,
  "relinquish[ing] all intellectual property rights to this code... permission is
  granted for anyone to use, duplicate, modify and redistribute it" — the specific act
  the card's "gave it away for free" clause describes.
- Verdict: the card compresses two real dates (1989 invention, 1993 giveaway) into one
  year. CERN's own materials headline 1989 as the invention date, so the card's choice
  is defensible as a simplification for a children's card — no change required.
