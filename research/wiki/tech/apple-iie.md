The Apple IIe (January 1983, US$1,395) is the machine that sat in a den or a school
computer room for a whole decade — Apple sold it until **15 November 1993**, making it
*"the longest-lived computer in Apple's history."* For a typing game its distinguishing
feature is unusually on-the-nose: **the IIe is the first Apple II you could type
lowercase letters on without buying an extra card**, and the first with a full ASCII
keyboard, an up arrow and a down arrow, Delete, Tab and built-in auto-repeat. Everything
its predecessor typed came out in capitals.

## The facts

- **Introduced January 1983**, price **US$1,395** for the main unit; successor to the
  Apple II Plus. Discontinued **15 November 1993** — *"the longest-lived computer in
  Apple's history"* (Wikipedia). The original (non-Enhanced) IIe was in production
  **January 1983 – February 1985**, with the Enhanced and Platinum revisions carrying the
  line to 1993 (apple2history.org). *Precision point: "the IIe" as a product line ran
  eleven years; "the IIe as first shipped" ran about two.*
- **The typing story, which is what makes this the right machine for this game.** It was
  *"the first Apple II with built-in lowercase"* and added *"the ability to input and
  display lower-case letters"* along with *"four-way cursor control and standard editing
  keys (Del and Tab)"* and an **auto-repeat that no longer needed a dedicated REPT key**
  (Wikipedia). apple2history.org states the design goal in the same terms — *"One of the
  original plans was to give the Apple II an 80-column text display and a full
  upper/lowercase keyboard"* — and confirms the firmware work *"to make the ESC cursor
  moves work with the left and right arrow keys, and the new up and down arrow keys."*
  **Before the IIe, the Apple II keyboard could only move the cursor left and right.**
- **80-column text and 64K RAM standard**, with the two Apple modifier keys
  (open-apple and solid-apple) and the Reset key moved safely off to the side.
- **A dramatic simplification.** Wikipedia: *"reducing total chip count to 31 (previous
  models used 120 chips)"* — roughly a 75% reduction. apple2history.org, more granularly:
  engineers *"managed to decrease the number of components on the motherboard from over
  one hundred to thirty-one."*
- **It sold hard, immediately.** apple2history.org: *"by May of 1983 the Apple IIe was
  selling sixty to seventy thousand units a month."* Wikipedia notes the IIe *"sold well
  in 1983 despite the IBM PC's great success"* and that Apple claimed *"85 to 90% of
  Apple II software worked with it"* at launch.
- **How it got into schools — "Kids Can't Wait," and the dates matter.** California's
  **AB 3194**, signed by Governor Jerry Brown in **September 1982**, created *"a 25% tax
  credit against the state corporate income tax for computer equipment donated to
  schools."* Apple then donated systems to roughly **9,000 eligible California elementary
  and secondary schools** (schools with fewer than 100 students did not qualify), each
  package an **Apple IIe** — *"a 64K computer, monitor, disk drive, and Apple Logo
  software"* — valued at **$2,364 retail**. Steve Jobs personally championed it and
  lobbied Congress for a federal version (**HR 5573**, the Computer Equipment Contribution
  Act), which *"passed the House 323-62"* but **failed in the Senate**. The tax credit cut
  Apple's net cost from about **$5.2 million to roughly $1 million**.
  **⚠️ Created-vs-shipped: the law is 1982, the machines are 1983, and the machine donated
  was the IIe — which had only just been introduced.** Do not date the donation 1982.
- **It is in the national collection.** Computer History Museum catalogue **102633586**:
  *"Apple IIe computer"*, made **1983**, Apple Computer, Inc., Cupertino; model
  **A2S2128**; *"Object consists of IIe system unit, no peripherals. Inside is memory,
  super-serial, and diskette cards."* Weight 10 lb.

## Unsourceable / weakly-sourced items

- **Total Apple IIe units sold.** Widely-quoted figures (e.g. "400,000 in 1983, a million
  more in 1984") appeared in search summaries this session but were **not** confirmed on
  any page fetched. Not asserted above; a future pass should pin them or drop them.
- **"The most successful Apple II model."** Plausible and commonly stated; the fetched
  sources support *longest-lived* and *sold well*, which is what this page claims. Do not
  upgrade the superlative without a source.
- **The Apple IIe's own keyboard homing markers.** A community claim exists that Apple
  used **D and K** rather than F and J for tactile home keys on some models; it could not
  be verified this session and is recorded as an open question on [[home-row-bumps]], not
  as a fact here.
