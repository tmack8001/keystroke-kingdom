The 16-bit era (roughly 1991–1996) spans the Genesis/SNES console war and its
contemporaneous arcade/CD-ROM titles — Genesis FM synthesis, SNES Mode 7, dramatically
larger sprites than the NES generation, the first pre-rendered-3D sprite technique
([[donkey-kong-country]]), and the first CD-ROM photographic-still adventure games
([[myst]]).

## In this game

The 16-bit era is Keystroke Kingdom's **Act 3 ("The Basement")** visual target (Levels
11–15): "16-bit wars: Genesis FM + SNES Mode 7 era; big sprites (SF2's scale was the
shock), pre-rendered 3D (DKC's SGI trick), CD-ROM photographic stills." Canvas grammar:
gradients allowed, 2-3 depth parallax, 80-112px sprites with shading maps, god-rays,
screen-flash battle drama. Act 3's cabinets: [[sonic-the-hedgehog]] (Level 11, Genesis),
[[street-fighter-ii]] (Level 12, moved here from an earlier draft's Level 4 — SF2 belongs
in the 90s Basement, not the home row), [[myst]] (Level 13), [[donkey-kong-country]] (Level
14), [[pokemon-red-blue]] (Level 15, Game Boy — a deliberate 4-shade contrast within the
same act). See `research/video-game-history.md`, Era fidelity targets table, Act 3 row.

## The facts

- Sonic the Hedgehog (1991): 16-bit Genesis FM, speed-blur effects, multi-layer parallax —
  a dramatic jump from Act 2's NES palette.
- Street Fighter II (1991): CPS-1 arcade hardware, large detailed 2D sprites at 384x224 —
  "SF2's scale was the shock."
- Myst (1993): pre-rendered photographic CD-ROM stills, **rendered on Apple Macintosh
  Quadra with StrataVision 3D, not SGI** — see [[myst]]'s correction, distinguishing it
  from DKC's genuinely SGI-based pipeline in the same era.
- Donkey Kong Country (1994): genuinely SGI-rendered pre-rendered-3D sprites — see
  [[pre-rendered-3d]].
- Pokémon Red/Blue (1996): Game Boy 4-shade dot-matrix display, contrasted with a dramatic
  visual-energy spike on battle-screen entry.
