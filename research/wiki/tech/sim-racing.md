"Sim racing" is a technical claim, not a marketing adjective. A racing game is a **simulation**
when it models tyre behaviour as physics rather than friction (temperature, wear, flat spots,
graining), treats fuel and wear as live strategic variables, models aerodynamics as a live
system, exports **telemetry** an engineer could read, builds its circuits from **surveyed
(laser/LIDAR-scanned) geometry** rather than by eye, and speaks to the player through **force
feedback**. Everything else is an arcade racer, however realistic it looks.

## In this game

This page exists to settle the terminal point of **Act 7 — Speed / Racing**: Trevor wants the act
to "end on an F1 simulation," and this is the definition that decides which titles qualify. Full
arc: `research/racing-games-by-era.md` §11. The happy finding for a kids' game is that the modern
official F1 titles are **ESRB E for Everyone** — a genuine simulation is the most
age-appropriate terminal station in the whole arc. The One Rule caveat lives in the same place:
sim racing's characteristic mechanics (incident points, licence demotion, failable graded tests)
are punishment systems and must be inverted, not imported.

## The facts

**The six technical markers.**

1. **Tyre model.** The clearest dividing line. Kunos Simulazioni's own product description for
   [[assetto-corsa]]-era physics advertises "**tyre flat spots, heat cycles including graining
   and blistering**" — tyre *chemistry*, not a grip coefficient. Geoff Crammond's
   [[formula-one-grand-prix]] (1991) already modelled tyre wear and damage.
2. **Fuel, wear and strategy as live variables**, including modern hybrid systems: "hybrid
   systems with KERS and energy recovery simulation."
3. **Aerodynamics as a live system** — "very advanced aerodynamic simulation with active movable
   aerodynamics parts controlled in real time by telemetry input channels."
4. **Telemetry** — "advanced setup options and telemetry for data analysis," plus in-game
   telemetry apps. Its ancestor is [[indianapolis-500-the-simulation]]'s (1989) live car setup
   and six-angle replay.
5. **Surveyed geometry.** Modern sims **laser/LIDAR-scan** real circuits: [[iracing]] is built on
   LIDAR-scanned cars and tracks; Kunos scanned circuits with a survey truck to millimetre
   accuracy. Assetto Corsa's own copy: circuits "developed using Laser scan technology, in order
   to guarantee the highest level of accuracy possible."
6. **Force feedback.** Consumer force feedback arrived at the end of the 1990s: Logitech's
   **WingMan Formula** (1998) was its first racing wheel and had none; the single-motor
   gear-driven **WingMan Formula Force** followed in **1999**, using Immersion Corporation's
   I-Force technology over the then-new USB. *(Chronology from PCGamingWiki plus a Logitech
   manual surfaced in search but not fetched — directionally safe, not card-grade.)*

**Which titles actually qualify.**
- **Simulation:** the Papyrus lineage — [[indianapolis-500-the-simulation]] (1989) →
  [[nascar-racing-1994]] → [[grand-prix-legends]] (1998) → [[iracing]] (2008), all traceable to
  designer Dave Kaemmer; Geoff Crammond's [[formula-one-grand-prix]] series; [[gran-turismo]]
  ("The Real Driving Simulator"); Assetto Corsa (Kunos Simulazioni, **December 19 2014**,
  developed at an R&D office inside the Vallelunga circuit in cooperation with real drivers and
  teams).
- **Arcade, whatever the box says:** [[daytona-usa]], [[ridge-racer]],
  [[sega-rally-championship]], [[need-for-speed]] after 1994, [[f-zero]], [[mario-kart]].

**The official Formula 1 games.** Codemasters has produced the official F1 game **since 2009**,
under a licence running to 2025; **Electronic Arts acquired Codemasters for ~$1.2 billion,
completed February 2021**. The **F1 Esports Series launched in 2017** (109,000 entrants from 156
countries by the 2019 qualifiers). **F1 23 and F1 24 are both ESRB E for Everyone** (Mild
Language; Users Interact; In-Game Purchases).

*See also (added 2026-09-13):* **[[f1-25]]** — the current entry (Codemasters / EA Sports,
May 30 2025), with its own ESRB record, LiDAR-scanned circuits, first-ever reverse layouts and
PC path tracing, documented at Act 7 cabinet-build depth. **Note the ratings drift:** F1 25 is
still **E**, but carries a **second** descriptor, **Use of Alcohol** (podium champagne),
alongside Mild Language — so "the official F1 games are E (Mild Language)" is accurate for
23 and 24 and **out of date for 25**. Always re-check per year.
