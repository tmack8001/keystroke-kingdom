The flat LCD screen that replaced the deep, heavy CRT tube monitor on an ordinary
home desk arrived later than intuition suggests: the first standalone LCD monitors
appeared in the mid-1990s at high prices, but LCD monitors did not actually **outsell**
CRT monitors until **2003** — and CRTs stayed a common sight well into the later 2000s.

## In this game

**Not yet built.** Candidate component for the proposed "THE HARDWARE" wing — a good

sourced date is notably later, which makes it a genuinely useful, non-obvious fact
rather than a repeated assumption.

## The facts

- **First standalone desktop LCD monitors: mid-1990s, expensive.** Wikipedia's
  Computer monitor article: *"The first standalone LCDs appeared in the mid-1990s
  selling for high prices."*
- **Cost-competitive with CRT by 1997.** The same article dates LCD monitors as
  starting to compete with CRTs on price by 1997 — six-plus years before they
  actually overtook CRT in sales.
- **Early notable desktop LCD models: 1998**, from Eizo, SGI, Apple, and ViewSonic —
  named specifically on the same page as among the earliest.
- **The crossover point: 2003.** Wikipedia's Computer monitor article states directly:
  *"In 2003, LCDs outsold CRTs for the first time, becoming the primary technology
  used for computer monitors."* This is the precise, sourced answer to "when did this
  actually happen for ordinary households" — not the mid-1990s a first-appearance date
  might imply, and later than many adults may remember it.
- **CRTs lingered for years after that.** The same page notes 4:3 CRT monitors had
  become rare by 2011 — implying an extended, gradual replacement rather than a sudden
  switch, consistent with Wikipedia's Liquid-crystal display article, which frames
  LCDs as having replaced CRTs "in nearly all applications" only by "the late 2000s to
  the early 2010s," and separately notes that LCD *televisions* (a related but
  distinct product category) first outsold CRT TVs in worldwide sales in Q4 2007.
