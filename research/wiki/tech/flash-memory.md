Flash memory — the storage technology behind every USB stick, SD card, and phone —
was invented by Toshiba engineer Fujio Masuoka, who presented NOR flash publicly in
1984 (building on a patent filing begun in 1980) and named it for the way its
bulk-erase process reminded a colleague of a camera flash.

## The facts

- Fujio Masuoka, an engineer at Toshiba, is credited with inventing flash memory. The
  Computer History Museum dates the invention to 1984, the year he publicly presented
  what's now called NOR flash; other sources note his underlying patent work (with
  Hisakazu Iizuka) began as early as 1980. Masuoka went on to demonstrate NAND flash
  at the 1987 IEEE International Electron Devices Meeting; Toshiba commercially
  launched NAND flash that same year.
- The name "flash" was suggested by Masuoka's Toshiba colleague Shōji Ariizumi,
  because erasing a block of the memory's contents reminded him of a camera's flash
  going off.
- Masuoka later felt Toshiba had not adequately credited or compensated him for the
  invention; he left to teach at Tohoku University and won a lawsuit against his
  former employer, settling for roughly $758,000 in 2006.
