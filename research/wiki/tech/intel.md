Robert Noyce and Gordon Moore, already Silicon Valley veterans from co-founding
Fairchild Semiconductor, incorporated Intel on July 18, 1968 — the company whose chips
(the 386 and 486, this game's Level 18 cards) power decades of the computers this game
walks through.

## The facts

- Robert Noyce and Gordon Moore incorporated Intel on July 18, 1968, after both had
  been founding members of Fairchild Semiconductor in 1957. Noyce is credited with
  co-inventing the integrated circuit; Moore is known for "Moore's Law."
- The company's name is a portmanteau of "integrated electronics." Noyce was Intel's
  first CEO; Moore succeeded him in 1975.
- Intel began operations on August 1, 1968 with about a dozen engineers, working from a
  conference room in Mountain View, California.
