Before the machines took the name, **"computer" was a job — a person who computed.** At NACA
(which became NASA in 1958), rooms full of people, overwhelmingly women, worked out flight
trajectories by hand on paper and mechanical calculating machines. Several of them then became
the programmers of the electronic machines that replaced their job title.
**[[katherine-johnson]]** checked John Glenn's orbital numbers by hand *because he asked her to*;
**Dorothy Vaughan** taught herself FORTRAN and became an expert in it. It is a true story about a
word changing meaning, and it is exactly this game's subject.

## The facts

- **The job title.** Before electronic computers, *computer* referred to a person. NACA — the
  National Advisory Committee for Aeronautics, NASA's predecessor — hired **"human computers,"**
  largely women, in significant numbers during and after the Second World War, filling vacancies
  left by men at war. At Langley these included the segregated **West Area Computing** section,
  staffed by Black women mathematicians.
- **[[katherine-johnson]]** joined NACA at **Langley in the summer of 1953**, initially in the
  West Area Computing section, then the Maneuver Loads Branch of the Flight Research Division.
- **The John Glenn episode (1962) is real and is NASA's own telling.** Ahead of Glenn's orbital
  flight, the IBM computers producing the trajectory were not fully trusted; NASA's biography
  records Glenn asking engineers to *"get the girl to run the same numbers through the same
  equations… by hand, on her desktop mechanical calculating machine"* — and *"If she says they're
  good, then I'm ready to go."* Johnson's own assessment of her greatest contribution was the
  *"calculations that helped synch Project Apollo's Lunar Module with the lunar-orbiting Command
  and Service Module."* She authored or co-authored **26 research reports** and retired in 1986;
  Barack Obama awarded her the **Presidential Medal of Freedom in 2015**.
- **The transition into programming.** **Dorothy Vaughan**, a West Area computer and NACA's first
  Black supervisor, became proficient with the new IBM machines and an expert in **FORTRAN**,
  working on the SCOUT launch-vehicle programme. More broadly, the West Computers *"became
  engineers, (electronic) computer programmers, the first black managers at Langley and trajectory
  whizzes."*
- **Precision notes.** (a) The date on Johnson's Medal of Freedom is **2015** — Hamilton's is 2016;
  do not swap them. (b) "Hidden Figures" is the title of Margot Lee Shetterly's book and the film;
  it is not NASA's term — cite NASA for the facts and the book for the popularisation.
  (c) Vaughan's FORTRAN expertise is supported by museum/journalistic sources here, not by a NASA
  primary page fetched this pass — see the reference labels.
