The Intel 80386, released October 17, 1985, was the first 32-bit processor in Intel's
x86 line, packing 275,000 transistors at a 16MHz clock speed — the chip credited with
making computers fast enough for the games and software of the following decade.

## In this game

Level 18's history card spells `386` — 3 and 8 are Level 18's new keys. Card text: *"The
chip that made computers fast enough for real games. Thirty-two bits of pure speed,
smaller than a cracker."* See [[computing-history-moments]] for the full 17-card table.

## The facts

- **October 17, 1985:** Intel releases the 386, with a simultaneous 5-city global
  launch, 275,000 transistors, 16MHz clock.
- Confirmed as the first 32-bit processor in the x86 line.
- The "smaller than a cracker" comparison is inferred from pin-count/pin-pitch geometry
  (a 132-pin PGA package of that era works out to roughly 1.3–1.7 inches per side,
  smaller than a ~2-inch saltine or Ritz-style cracker) rather than a directly-quoted
  spec-sheet dimension — a geometric inference, not exaggerated, but the one detail in
  this card that isn't a directly cited number.
- Verdict: accurate. Release date and "first 32-bit x86 processor" claim both
  confirmed.
