In 1959 Volvo engineer **Nils Bohlin** — hired the year before as the company's first chief
safety engineer, after designing ejector seats for Saab fighter aircraft — produced the
V-shaped three-point seat belt that secures the upper and lower body and buckles at hip
level. Volvo then did the unusual thing: it made the patent freely available to every other
carmaker, reasoning that the design did more good as a shared standard than as a private
advantage. It is credited with saving at least a million lives.

## The facts

- **Who and when.** Nils Bohlin, a Volvo engineer, developed the V-type three-point safety
  belt in 1959. Volvo's own group heritage page: "the three-point safety belt was perfected
  by Volvo engineer Nils Bohlin in 1959."
- **His background.** Bohlin had designed ejection seats for Saab fighter aircraft in the
  1950s; Volvo hired him in 1958 as its first chief safety engineer, and he had the belt
  within about a year.
- **The gift.** Volvo made the patent available to all manufacturers — in its own words the
  design "was effectively gifted to the rest of the industry, and therefore the rest of the
  world to use, without payment or claims." By 1963 all Volvos had front seat belts.
- **Impact.** Volvo credits the invention with saving at least one million lives worldwide;
  its use "is enshrined in law around the globe."
- **Precision note.** Seat belts existed before 1959 (two-point lap belts); what Bohlin
  produced was the *three-point* belt that restrains both the shoulder and the hips with a
  single buckle a driver can fasten one-handed. "Invented the seat belt" is the wrong verb —
  "designed the three-point belt we all still use" is right.
