The MITS Altair 8800, on the cover of the January 1975 issue of *Popular Electronics*,
was the first commercially successful personal computer — a mail-order kit powered by
the Intel 8080 that hooked hobbyists by the thousands and drew a young Bill Gates and
Paul Allen to write its first BASIC interpreter, founding what became Microsoft.

## The facts

- MITS (Micro Instrumentation and Telemetry Systems), led by H. Edward Roberts,
  introduced the Altair 8800 via the January 1975 cover of *Popular Electronics*. It
  sold as a build-it-yourself kit for $397 (assembled versions cost more), built around
  the Intel 8080 microprocessor.
- Demand overwhelmed MITS: Roberts had hoped to sell 200 units to break even; by
  February 1975 the company had 1,000 orders, and by August 1975 more than 5,000 had
  shipped.
- The Altair had no built-in screen or keyboard — it was operated via front-panel
  switches and lights and connected to an external terminal.
- Bill Gates and Paul Allen wrote a BASIC interpreter for the Altair after contacting
  MITS in 1975 — the founding product of what became Microsoft.
