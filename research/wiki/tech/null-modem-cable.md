A null-modem cable is a real, correctly-named piece of hardware: an RS-232 serial
cable with its transmit and receive wires deliberately crossed, letting two computers
talk directly to each other with no modem and no phone line — a documented way
Amiga owners played multiplayer games together in the 1980s–early 1990s. It is **not**,
on the sourcing gathered this session, confirmed as the specific method DOS PC gamers
used for games like Doom — that era's dominant multiplayer path was a different
technology, IPX networking, and this page says so rather than blurring the two.

## The facts

- **What it is, precisely.** Wikipedia's own article title and definition: *"Null
  modem is a communication method to directly connect two DTEs (computer, terminal,
  printer, etc.) using an RS-232 serial cable"* — the trick is that *"the transmit and
  receive lines are crosslinked,"* so each machine's "talk" wire plugs into the
  other's "listen" wire. No modem, no telephone line, no dial tone — just a cable
  between two serial ports.
- **It IS documented as a multiplayer-gaming method — on the Amiga.** The same
  Wikipedia article states directly: *"On the Amiga computer, a null modem connection
  was a common way of playing multiplayer games between two machines."* It further
  frames null-modem cables generally as *"a popular method for transferring data
  between the early personal computers from the 1980s to the early 1990s."*
- **For DOS/PC gaming specifically (the "1995 PC gamer" case the brief asks about),
  the better-sourced dominant technology is IPX, not null-modem.** Wikipedia's
  IPX/SPX article states IPX was *"the de facto standard for DOS-era multi-user
  network games,"* naming *Doom* and *Warcraft III* as examples of games built on it,
  and describes third-party tunneling tools (Kali) that let IPX-based games reach
  players over the early internet rather than only a physical LAN. This is a
  **different kind of connection** from a null-modem cable — IPX is a network
  protocol running over LAN cabling (coax or twisted-pair Ethernet-style wiring, often
  many machines at once), while null-modem is a single direct point-to-point serial
  link between exactly two machines.
- **So: is "null modem" the wrong term?** Not wrong — it's a real, correctly-used,
  well-documented technology. It is very likely **imprecise** as *the* term for a
  1990s LAN-party scene with several PCs in one room, which is much more likely to
  have been IPX over Ethernet or coax cabling. It is **accurate** for a specific,
  smaller scene: two machines, one cable, direct — genuinely how some early home
  computers (confirmed: Amiga) connected for two-player play, and DOS-era PCs
  supported the same RS-232 serial-link option in many games' setup menus as a
  documented general-purpose alternative to modem or network play (a widely
  repeated claim in retro-computing writing, but one this session's available
  sources — Wikipedia's Doom article, and two attempts at DoomWiki/Doom Fandom that
  both failed to load, 403 and 402 respectively — did not directly confirm for any
  named game).
