IBM began life on June 16, 1911 as the Computing-Tabulating-Recording Company (CTR) —
a holding company for scale, clock, and punch-card-machine makers — and only became
"International Business Machines" on February 14, 1924, under general manager (later
chairman) Thomas J. Watson.

## The facts

- On June 16, 1911, financier Charles Ranlett Flint merged the Hollerith Tabulating
  Machine Company with two other data-processing/measuring-equipment manufacturers to
  form the Computing-Tabulating-Recording Company (CTR) in New York State.
- Thomas J. Watson became CTR's general manager in 1914 and had gained full control of
  the company by 1924. Disliking the clumsy "Computing-Tabulating-Recording" name, he
  renamed the company "International Business Machines" on February 14, 1924 — a name
  previously used only by CTR's Canadian division.
- CTR/IBM's early business was tabulating machines, time clocks, and scales — not yet
  anything resembling a modern computer; that came decades later.
