RFC 791, published by Jon Postel in September 1981, formally specified the Internet
Protocol (IPv4) — the addressing scheme that gives every device on the internet a
unique numeric address, still the foundation of most internet traffic more than four
decades later.

## The facts

- RFC 791, "Internet Protocol," was published in September 1981 by Jon Postel at
  USC/Information Sciences Institute under the DARPA Internet Program, replacing an
  earlier January 1980 draft (RFC 760).
- It defines the 32-bit addressing scheme, packet (datagram) format, and
  fragmentation/reassembly rules that let data find its way between "interconnected
  systems of packet-switched computer communication networks."
- In March 1982, the U.S. Department of Defense adopted the Internet Protocol Suite
  (TCP/IP, of which IPv4 is a core piece) as its standard for military networking,
  cementing its path to becoming the global internet's addressing scheme.
