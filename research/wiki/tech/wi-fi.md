"Wi-Fi" was invented as a brand name in 1999 by the agency Interbrand, hired by the
newly formed Wireless Ethernet Compatibility Alliance (WECA) to find something catchier
than "IEEE 802.11b" — a playful cousin of "hi-fi" that, despite the persistent myth, was
never actually an acronym for "wireless fidelity."

## The facts

- The Wireless Ethernet Compatibility Alliance (WECA) was formed August 23, 1999 by
  six companies (3Com, Aironet, Intersil, Lucent Technologies, Nokia, and Symbol
  Technologies) to certify interoperability between early 802.11a/802.11b wireless
  products.
- WECA hired the branding agency Interbrand, which proposed "Wi-Fi" — a deliberate pun
  on "hi-fi" — from a shortlist of ten candidate names; founding member Phil Belanger
  has said Interbrand also designed the logo.
- Wi-Fi was never officially an acronym. Some WECA members, worried the name alone
  wouldn't convey meaning, added the tagline "The Standard for Wireless Fidelity" —
  which the alliance later dropped, but which planted the enduring "wireless fidelity"
  myth.
- WECA renamed itself the Wi-Fi Alliance in 2002.
