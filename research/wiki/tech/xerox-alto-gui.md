The Xerox Alto, unveiled by Xerox PARC in 1973, was the first computer to demonstrate a
graphical user interface (GUI) — windows, icons, and a mouse-driven desktop metaphor —
work led by Alan Kay, Larry Tesler, Dan Ingalls, and others, which went on to directly
inspire Apple's Lisa and Macintosh interfaces.

## The facts

- Xerox PARC (Palo Alto Research Center) unveiled the Alto computer in 1973 (introduced
  around March–April of that year). It featured a bitmapped, high-resolution screen and
  was the first computer to demonstrate the desktop metaphor and a graphical user
  interface.
- The GUI's design — overlapping windows, icons, and mouse-driven navigation ("WIMP":
  windows, icons, menus, pointer) — was developed by a PARC team including Alan Kay,
  Larry Tesler, Dan Ingalls, David Smith, and Clarence Ellis, among others.
- The Alto's windowing concept was itself influenced by Douglas Engelbart's earlier
  "Display NLS" overlapping-window system (operational by early 1973 at ARPA sites) —
  the same Engelbart whose 1968 wooden mouse is [[engelbart-mouse|this game's Level 11
  card]].
- The Alto's interface directly influenced Apple's subsequent Lisa and Macintosh
  designs, following a famous 1979 Xerox PARC demo to Apple engineers including Steve
  Jobs.
