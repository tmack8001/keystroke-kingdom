The IBM 350 disk storage unit, part of the IBM 305 RAMAC system shipped to Zellerbach
Paper in San Francisco in 1956, was the first commercial hard disk drive — fifty
24-inch spinning platters, the size of two refrigerators, holding about 3.75 megabytes.

## The facts

- IBM shipped the Model 350 disk storage unit to Zellerbach Paper in San Francisco in
  June 1956, as part of the IBM 305 RAMAC (Random Access Method of Accounting and
  Control) computer system.
- Developed by a team led by Reynold Johnson at IBM's San Jose research lab, building on
  earlier concepts from Jacob Rabinow at the National Bureau of Standards.
- Fifty 24-inch-diameter magnetic disks spinning at 1,200 rpm, holding roughly 5 million
  6-bit characters — about 3.75 megabytes. The whole unit occupied the footprint of two
  refrigerators and weighed roughly a ton; the system leased for $3,200/month (about
  $36,700/month in 2025 dollars).
- More than 1,000 RAMAC systems were built before production ended in 1961.
