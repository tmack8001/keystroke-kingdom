Unix was begun in the summer of 1969 at Bell Telephone Laboratories by Ken Thompson,
with Dennis Ritchie's collaboration (and, soon after, his creation of the C language to
support it) — an operating system whose design lineage still underlies macOS, Linux,
Android, and iOS.

## The facts

- Development began in the summer of 1969 at Bell Telephone Laboratories, driven
  primarily by Ken Thompson, after Bell Labs withdrew from the earlier Multics
  project. Thompson wrote the first version in assembly for a DEC PDP-7 minicomputer.
- Dennis Ritchie collaborated closely with Thompson from early on and later created
  the C programming language specifically to make Unix portable across different
  hardware — a first complete version of Unix, with a hierarchical file system, was
  released in 1971.
- The name evolved from an early pun: the team originally called their project "Unics"
  (Uniplexed Information and Computing Service), a play on "Multics" (Multiplexed
  Information and Computer Services), the project it succeeded.
- Unix's design lineage runs directly to macOS (built on a Unix-derived kernel), Linux
  (a Unix-like reimplementation), and, through Linux, Android — meaning most phones
  and computers made today still carry ideas from this 1969 project.
