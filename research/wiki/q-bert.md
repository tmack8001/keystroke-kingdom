Q*bert (Gottlieb, October 1982) is the isometric pseudo-3D hopping game whose orange,
big-nosed protagonist and "@!#?@!" grawlix speech balloon made it one of the most visually
distinct cabinets of the golden age.

## In this game

Q*bert is the **Level 22 cabinet** in Keystroke Kingdom's parked Act 5/Corner Cabinets plan
— "one of the most visually distinct cabinets ever; instantly readable to a child." See
`research/video-game-history.md` "Cabinet assignments — history notes for the newer picks."

## The facts

- Developer: Gottlieb — programmed and designed by **Warren Davis**, from an original
  character and cube-pattern visual concept by artist **Jeff Lee**. Released October 1982.
- **Correction (design credit):** the flat "designed by Warren Davis and Jeff Lee"
  co-design credit has been refined. Davis wrote all the code and held Gottlieb's official
  "designer" credit per the company's own internal convention (programmers were credited as
  designers, artists were not). Lee originated the visual character and cube-pattern
  concept as the game's artist. Both contributions are real; the credit split was
  previously imprecise.
- Visual style: isometric pseudo-3D perspective — a 2D game rendered in axonometric
  projection. Black background, multicolored 28-cube pyramid, cubes change color when
  landed on.
- Input: four-way joystick rotated 45 degrees to match Q*bert's diagonal movement axes —
  unique among arcade games.
- Iconic: the "@!#?@!" grawlix death balloon; synthesized gibberish "speech" from a Votrax
  chip; a physical pinball solenoid "knocker" that thumps when characters fall off the
  pyramid.
- Technical: MOS 6502 sound CPU at 894 kHz, Votrax speech synthesizer, 128 bytes RAM + 4 KB
  EPROM for sound. Gottlieb's fourth video game (the company's roots were in pinball).
