Breakout (Atari, 1976) is the brick-breaking paddle game designed by Nolan Bushnell and
Steve Bristow, with an early prototype built by Steve Wozniak — a discrete-logic cabinet
that used colored cellophane strips over a black-and-white monitor to simulate color.

## In this game

Breakout is the **Level 1 cabinet** in Keystroke Kingdom, teaching J and F. It anchors Act
1's opening visual target: 1-bit black and white, blocky rectangular sprites, zero curves,
zero anti-aliasing — the "raw beginning" of the [[discrete-logic-era]]. See
`.claude/rules/levels.md` Level 1.

## The facts

- Developer: Atari — designed by Nolan Bushnell and Steve Bristow; prototype built by Steve
  Wozniak. Released 1976.
- Visual style: monochrome CRT with colored cellophane strips overlaid to simulate 4 colors
  (yellow bottom row, green, orange, red top row of bricks). Black background, simple
  geometric shapes only.
- Input: rotary knob (paddle controller), horizontal movement only.
- Iconic: the paddle shrinks to half-width after the ball breaks through the top red row;
  ball speed increases at 4 hits, 12 hits, and on contact with orange/red bricks.
- Technical: discrete logic (TTL) — no CPU, no software. Production version used
  approximately 100 TTL chips (redesigned by Gary Waters at Cyan Engineering). Wozniak's
  prototype used only ~45 chips via unconventional "glitch logic" tricks that couldn't be
  reliably mass-manufactured.
- **Correction:** the prototype chip count is commonly rounded to "44" but the more
  precise, source-backed figure is ~45 chips.
