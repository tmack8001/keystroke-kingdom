The word "pixel" was first published in 1965 by Frederic C. Billingsley of NASA's Jet
Propulsion Laboratory, in two SPIE papers on digital image processing — a meta-card in
Keystroke Kingdom, since the player has been reading pixels the entire game.

## In this game

Level 14's history card spells `pixel` — X unlocks at Level 14. Card text: *"Everything
on this screen is made of tiny squares of light called pixels."* See
[[computing-history-moments]] for the full 17-card table.

## The facts

- **1965:** Frederic C. Billingsley publishes two JPL papers using the word "pixel"
  ("Digital Video Processing at JPL," SPIE Vol. 3, April 1965; "Processing Ranger and
  Mariner Photography," SPIE Vol. 10, August 1965) — the first *published* use.
- An earlier, unpublished oral-use account is attributed to Keith E. McFarland, meaning
  Billingsley may not be the absolute first to say the word, only the first to publish
  it.
- The in-game card doesn't claim Billingsley *coined* the word, just defines "pixel"
  without attaching a name — so the McFarland-priority nuance doesn't create an error in
  the player-facing text.
- Verdict: accurate as written. Research-doc language should say "first published"
  rather than "coined" for precision; no in-game text change needed.
