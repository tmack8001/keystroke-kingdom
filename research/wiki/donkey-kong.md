Donkey Kong (Nintendo, 1981), designed by Shigeru Miyamoto, is the platformer that
introduced Mario (originally "Jumpman") and is officially recognized by Guinness World
Records as the first video game to tell "a complete story, with beginning and end,"
delivered through cutscenes.

## In this game

Donkey Kong is the **Level 6 cabinet**, teaching E and I and opening Act 2 (The Computer
Wing). It runs on hardware derived from restructured [[galaxian]]-lineage Radar Scope
boards, and its expressive sprite personality (DK's shrug, Mario's death animation) is cited
in the [[nes-hardware]] concept page as the moment games started having characters, not just
objects. See `.claude/rules/levels.md` Level 6.

## The facts

- Developer: Nintendo, designed by Shigeru Miyamoto. Released July 1981 (Japan), October
  1981 (North America).
- Visual style: colorful construction-site platformer — red-capped Mario, brown ape,
  pink-dressed Pauline. Four single-screen stages, each representing 25 meters of height.
- Technical: built on restructured Radar Scope boards ([[galaxian]]-derived hardware). 128
  foreground sprites at 16x16 pixels, 256 background tiles at 8x8 pixels. Mario uses a
  single sprite; Pauline uses 2 sprites; Donkey Kong uses 6 sprites. Roughly 20 KB of total
  content.
- **Correction:** softened from a loose "first arcade game with a complete narrative" to
  Guinness World Records' precise framing — DK is documented as the first to combine a
  complete story with cutscene delivery, not simply "the first game with a narrative" in an
  unqualified sense (earlier games had told stories or set scenes separately).
- Origin of Mario's design: mustache implies a mouth; cap obviates hair animation — solving
  sprite-resolution limits with character design.
