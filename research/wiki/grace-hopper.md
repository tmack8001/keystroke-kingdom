Grace Hopper was a Harvard Mark II team member and career naval officer/computer
scientist who spent decades popularizing the story of the 1947 "first computer bug" —
though the historical record does not credit her with personally finding or logging the
moth herself. See [[mark-ii-moth-1947]] for the event itself.

## In this game

Level 13's history card spells `grace` — C unlocks at Level 13, pairing with the
level's "pause and read" Myst mood. Card text as originally written: *"Grace Hopper's
team found a real moth stuck inside their computer — the first computer 'bug.' She
taped it into the logbook."* **This needs correction** — see The facts below.

## The facts

- **September 9, 1947:** a moth is found and taped into the Harvard Mark II logbook,
  with the annotation "First actual case of bug being found." Date and computer are
  correct in the card.
- The Smithsonian's own object record for the actual logbook states it "was probably
  not Hopper's." Multiple sources (JSTOR Daily, citing the Smithsonian and the 1981
  *Annals of the History of Computing*) confirm Hopper herself repeatedly said in later
  interviews that she did not personally find or log the moth — a technician on her
  team did.
- Hopper's real, well-documented role: she was present on the Mark II team and spent
  decades retelling the story in public lectures, which is why the anecdote is
  attached to her name at all. "She told the story so well for 45 years that it became
  hers" (Silicon Canals).
- **Correction needed for player-facing text.** The sentence "She taped it into the
  logbook" is not supported by the sources. Suggested replacement text, preserving tone
  and word-length constraints:
  > "In 1947, a real moth got stuck inside Grace Hopper's team's computer — the first
  > time anyone taped a 'bug' into a logbook. Hopper told the story so well for
  > decades, it became hers."

  This keeps Hopper as the throughline (the card is titled "GRACE HOPPER" and she's
  still the reason the story survived), keeps the fun fact, and removes the specific
  unsupported claim. This change was **not** made to `js/history-cards.js` by the
  research pass — flagged here for a human/separate change.
- Same correction and reasoning appear on [[mark-ii-moth-1947]], the event page.
