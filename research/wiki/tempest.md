Tempest (Atari, October 1981), designed by Dave Theurer, was one of the first color vector
games, rendering abstract geometric tube-shaped playfields with Atari's Color-QuadraScan
display — and shipped with an infamous ROM bug granting 40 free credits and 255 lives.

## In this game

Tempest is not an assigned Keystroke Kingdom cabinet but exemplifies the color-vector branch
of the [[discrete-logic-era]] alongside monochrome-vector [[asteroids]].

## The facts

- Developer: Atari — designed by Dave Theurer (who also designed Missile Command). Released
  October 1981.
- Visual style: color vector graphics via Atari's Color-QuadraScan display. 16 unique
  geometric playfield shapes (including a flat line and the infinity symbol); one level set
  (65–80) renders in invisible black.
- Input: rotary knob (spinner/dial) for movement along the rim, plus fire button and
  "Superzapper" button (destroys all on-screen enemies once per level).
- Technical: custom Atari board with Color-QuadraScan vector graphics; no sprite hardware,
  all objects are line segments. Early shipped ROMs had a bug (from a last-minute logo
  tweak that broke an anti-piracy check) enabling 40 free credits and 255 lives, later
  patched.
