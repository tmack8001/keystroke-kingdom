Defender (Williams Electronics, 1981), designed by Eugene Jarvis, is the side-scrolling
shooter notable for pioneering the on-screen minimap and algorithmically generated particle
explosions — both innovations for arcade games at the time.

## In this game

Defender is not an assigned Keystroke Kingdom cabinet but is a notable early-golden-age
technical outlier — its wider-than-standard 320x256 resolution and 16-color display sit
alongside [[tempest]]'s color vector graphics as evidence of rapid hardware escalation in
Act 1's era.

## The facts

- Developer: Williams Electronics — design team led by Eugene Jarvis (with Larry DeMar, Sam
  Dicker, Paul Dussault). Released February 1981 (Japan), March 1981 (North America).
- Visual style: side-scrolling shooter, bright detailed sprites against a dark starfield,
  particle-effect explosions (unique for the era), minimap at screen top.
- Input: two-way joystick (up/down only) plus five buttons (thrust, reverse, fire, smart
  bomb, hyperspace) — a notoriously complex control scheme.
- Technical: Motorola 6809 CPU (gameplay/graphics) + Motorola 6800 (audio). Resolution
  320x256 pixels (wider than the industry-standard 256x256). 16-color display.
  Software-driven animation (not hardware sprites). Programmed entirely in assembly.
- Sam Dicker's algorithmically-generated particle explosions were never the same twice.
- Sourcing caveat: the 3x-AA-battery high-score-backup detail is single-sourced to
  Wikipedia this pass.
