Motor-learning research distinguishes between different types of feedback during skill
acquisition — Knowledge of Results (did the outcome succeed?) versus Knowledge of
Performance (how was the movement executed?) — and keyboarding-specific instruction
research finds that supervised, corrected practice outperforms unsupervised drill. No
source frames "non-punitive feedback vs. forced restart" as a typing-specific settled
finding; it is a general motor-learning principle that Keystroke Kingdom applies to typing.

## In this game

This research is the closest thing Keystroke Kingdom's One Rule has to an external citation,
and it stands in direct contrast to the punitive drill design of [[slam-dunk-typing]].
The rule — "the game should only ever stop a player's flow with joy, never with punishment"
— and its concrete implementation (wrong key: gentle nudge sound, the letter **stays on
screen and waits**, mistake logged quietly, no double penalty on recovery) is consistent
with, though not directly proven by, this literature: immediate, informational,
non-punitive correction is what the motor-learning research favors, and supervised
real-time correction beats unsupervised tutorial-only practice in keyboarding-specific
studies. This same body of research backs three other locked decisions from Act 5
planning: Backspace taught as a skill rather than a confession ("Backspace isn't failure.
Every writer uses it," from product.md); case-sensitivity only activating when the expected
character is genuinely uppercase (never punishing a CapsLock'd child); and modifier
keydowns never counting as mistakes (pressing Shift while expecting a capital letter is the
correct first half of the action, not an error). **Framing discipline matters here** — this
page should never be cited as "research proves the One Rule is correct," only as
"motor-learning research on feedback types is consistent with, and informed, the One Rule."

## The facts

- **Knowledge of Results (KR) vs. Knowledge of Performance (KP):** a peer-reviewed
  comparison of these informational feedback types in motor skill acquisition; both are
  established categories in the motor-learning literature.
- A systematic review (Taylor & Francis, 2021) examines when KP feedback is superior to KR
  feedback, and under what timing conditions — feedback type and timing both matter to
  learning outcomes.
- **Keyboarding-specific finding:** direct instruction with real-time correction
  outperforms unsupervised tutorial-only practice in elementary students (full text
  paywalled — verify exact quotes before excerpting if this claim is used more precisely
  elsewhere).
- A separate ERIC-indexed study (Ertl) examines initial touch-keyboarding speed achievement
  and longer-term skill retention in fifth graders.
- **Explicit framing caveat carried over from the hub doc:** no source frames "non-punitive
  feedback vs. forced restart" as a typing-specific settled finding. This is a general
  motor-learning principle applied to typing by project judgment, not a typing-pedagogy
  citation making that exact claim.
