Late-1970s pinball machines did not have literal NOVICE/EXPERT-labeled difficulty
switches. Electromechanical (EM) machines of the era used pre-drilled post positions
labeled "liberal, normal, and difficult"; contemporary solid-state machines like Stern's
1979 Trident used DIP switches to configure credits, balls per game, and replay/extra-ball
awards — none of it labeled by player skill level. This page exists as a record of a claim
that did not survive a citation pass.

## In this game

Keystroke Kingdom's opt-in Survival Mode uses three difficulty tiers — **NOVICE / ARCADE /
EXPERT** — tuned to be "challenging, never cruel" (EXPERT is roughly a 9-second window for
a 4-character pattern), with an arcade-culture mercy mechanic where a 15-streak earns a
quarter back, and a warm game-over message ("YOU TYPED n LETTERS. THE ARCADE REMEMBERS.").
Survival Mode is opt-in only — the base ADVENTURE mode has no timers or lives, consistent
with the One Rule (see [[error-feedback-pedagogy]]) — and even within Survival Mode, a
wrong keypress is never punished, only time pressure is added (see [[slam-dunk-typing]] for
the contrast case this was designed against).

An earlier design note claimed the NOVICE/ARCADE/EXPERT tier names were chosen because they
echoed "1979 pinball's literal NOVICE/EXPERT switches." **This claim did not survive the
citation pass and was corrected.** No manufacturer manual, IPDB entry, or pinball-history
source documents a NOVICE/EXPERT-labeled switch on any 1979-era machine. The tier names
themselves were kept — they're good, clear names for a typing game — but the historical
justification is now explicitly downgraded to "in the spirit of operator-adjustable arcade
difficulty settings," not a period-accurate factual claim. This page is the intended
worked example of what a preserved correction looks like: the design decision survives,
the historical claim backing it does not, and both facts are recorded side by side rather
than quietly dropping the original (wrong) claim.

## The facts

- **No source documents a literal NOVICE/EXPERT switch on any 1979 pinball machine.**
  This was checked directly against a real 1979 solid-state machine (Stern's Trident) and
  against general period pinball-adjustment documentation, and no such labeling appears.
- **Actual EM-era terminology:** pre-drilled post holes offering "liberal, normal, and
  difficult" settings (sometimes described as "liberal vs. conservative").
- **Actual solid-state-era mechanism (1979, e.g. Stern Trident):** DIP switches controlling
  credits, balls per game, and replay/extra-ball award thresholds — configurable by an
  operator, but not labeled by a skill-level name.
- **A second dead-end lead was also checked and ruled out:** Tempest (Atari, 1981) is
  sometimes informally claimed to have had "novice to expert" ratings; arcade-museum.com's
  DIP switch sheet for Tempest shows only Easy/Medium/Hard, not novice/expert language.
- Modern pinball machines do commonly use easy/normal/hard difficulty tiers — the concept
  of named difficulty tiers is real and current, just not documented as 1979-era
  terminology under the NOVICE/EXPERT naming.
