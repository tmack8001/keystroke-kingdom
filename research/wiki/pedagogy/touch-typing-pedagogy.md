Touch-typing curricula — from a 50-year-old standard keyboarding textbook through every
major commercial and educational typing tutor — teach keys in the same order for the same
physiological reason: home row first (where fingers rest, free muscle memory), then
outward in symmetric finger-pairs. This ordering traces back to touch typing's late-1880s
popularization, though its "inventor" is disputed.

## In this game

Keystroke Kingdom's entire level progression (`.claude/rules/levels.md`, "Why This Order")
follows this exact canon sequence: home row (J F D K → S L → A + the eighth key → G H),
then E/I → R/U → T/Y → W/O → Q/P outward in finger-pairs, then the bottom row in the same
pattern. This is not a novel curriculum — it is the standard progression shared by
[[mario-teaches-typing]] (1992), [[mavis-beacon]] (1987), [[dance-mat-typing]] (BBC), and
[[typingclub]] (web-based/K-12), confirmed independently across a 50-year-old commercial
keyboarding textbook and modern teacher handbooks. Keystroke Kingdom's stated departure
from the canon is not the key order but everything wrapped around it: one distinct arcade
cabinet per level with era-progressive graphics (versus MTT's single Mario skin the whole
way through), and a deliberately more thorough treatment of symbol keys — the Corner
Cabinet levels (21–23) — where nearly every canon tutor lumps `` ` - = [ ] ' \ `` into one
lesson or skips them.

Two Keystroke Kingdom-specific decisions extend the canon rather than following it exactly:
"Symbols before Shift" (unshifted base keys are learned by location first, so Shift becomes
the payoff that unlocks the keys' upper halves), and accepting that the left/right pairing
rhythm honestly breaks at levels 21–23 (symbol keys cluster right-pinky, so those levels are
framed as right-pinky-reach levels rather than forcing artificial hand symmetry).

## The facts

- The home-row-first, finger-pair-outward ordering is documented as standard, uncontroversial
  baseline practice — curricula document it as given; academic literature rarely argues for
  it as a contested finding.
- **Gregg College Keyboarding & Document Processing** (Ober, Johnson, Zimmerly, McGraw-Hill)
  is the 50+-year standard keyboarding textbook; its Lesson 1 is touch-typing ASDF JKL;,
  home row first.
- TypingClub's own **Typing Jungle Teacher Handbook** documents the identical structure:
  home-row-first, finger-to-key assignment, progressive outward sequencing — confirming the
  pattern persists unchanged from print-era textbooks into modern web-based tutors.
- An open textbook (BCcampus, "Key Concepts of Computer Studies") independently describes
  the home row as the anchor position from which all other keys are taught.
- **Historical footnote — popularizer, not inventor.** Touch typing was popularized (its
  invention is disputed) by **Frank Edward McGurrin**, who won a famous typing contest on
  **July 25, 1888** in Cincinnati against Louis Traub, typing without looking at the keys.
  **Bates Torrey** coined the term "writing by touch" in **1889**. Wikipedia's own account
  of McGurrin explicitly notes that whether he was first to touch-type "is disputed" — he
  should always be cited as the method's popularizer, not its settled inventor.
