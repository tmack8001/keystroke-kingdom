Racing is the dominant metaphor of browser-based typing practice, and has been for nearly twenty
years. **TypeRacer** (March 2008) is a racing game whose only input is typing — each player drives
a small car that advances as they type a passage, first to the finish line wins — and
**Nitro Type** (2011) carried the same idea into classrooms. Separately, **The Typing of the
Dead** (1999) proved the harder point: that replacing a game's input device with a keyboard is a
legitimate design move, not a gimmick.

## In this game

This page is the honest answer to a question asked of the **Act 7 — Speed / Racing** research:
*did typing ever genuinely feature in racing games?* In the commercial console and arcade canon,
essentially no. In the **typing-game** canon, racing is the default. So Act 7's central mechanic —
your car moves when you type — is not a novelty Trevor is inventing; it is the field-tested
standard with a seventeen-year track record in schools. Act 7's genuine originality will lie in
**removing the race-against-others pressure** that TypeRacer and Nitro Type are built on while
keeping the forward motion, exactly as Keystroke Kingdom's One Rule requires. Compare the
curriculum tutors [[mario-teaches-typing]], [[mavis-beacon]], [[dance-mat-typing]] and
[[typingclub]], and the punishment contrast case [[slam-dunk-typing]]. Full arc:
`research/racing-games-by-era.md` §10.

## The facts

**TypeRacer** (Alex Epshteyn, **March 2008**)
- A multiplayer online browser typing game in which players "complete typing tests of various
  texts as fast as possible, competing against themselves or with other users online." Each
  player controls a **miniature car that advances as they type**; faster and more accurate typing
  moves the car further toward the finish line.
- Passages are drawn from popular culture — "songs, films, television shows, video games and
  books" (cited examples include *Monty Python and the Holy Grail* and *A Clockwork Orange*).
- It **claims to be the first multiplayer typing game on the web**. Epshteyn — a computer-science
  master's graduate and former intern — built it because the Windows shareware program he learned
  touch typing on had no multiplayer mode. He used the OpenSocial API and the Google Web Toolkit.
- Recognition: PC Magazine's "Top 100 Undiscovered Web Sites of 2008."

**Nitro Type** (Teaching.com, **2011**)
- A free, school-oriented competitive typing racing game with a Teacher Portal offering safety
  controls, classroom management and performance measurement, plus class rostering and single
  sign-on via Google Classroom and Clever.
- **Sourcing caution:** the 2011 launch year and the founder attribution (Austin Butler) come
  from **nitrotype.blog**, a first-party blog, plus educator-review sites. Treat the year as
  reasonably sound and the **founder name as [unverified]**. Do not put it on a history card.

**The Typing of the Dead** (WOW Entertainment / Sega, **1999**, NAOMI arcade; Dreamcast 2000)
- Not racing, but the canonical proof of the input-swap concept: it is **Sega's 1998 light-gun
  game *The House of the Dead 2* with the gun replaced by a computer keyboard**. Players type
  letters, words and phrases to defeat advancing enemies.
- The changes are mostly cosmetic and gleefully committed to the bit: zombies' weapons become
  harmless items, and the agents carry **keyboards attached to backpack Dreamcasts** with
  oversized batteries.
- **What not to import:** its pressure loop is "type fast or the enemy reaches you" — a timer
  wearing a costume, and a One Rule violation.
