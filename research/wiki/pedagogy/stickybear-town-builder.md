Stickybear Town Builder (Richard Hefter and Dave Lusby, Optimum Resource, **1984** on Apple II;
re-released on a single 3.5-inch 800K disk in **1988**; **1992** on MS-DOS) is the
[[stickybear]] line's exploration title: you place buildings on empty land, the program builds
them and connects them with roads, and then you drive a little car around the town you made,
hunting map locations and hidden keys from cardinal-direction clues. Two things make it worth
its own page. It is the **only Stickybear title preserved for MS-DOS** — which makes it the
only documented candidate for "Stickybear on a dad's office PC." And its three releases
(1984 → 1988 → 1992) are a clean cross-section of the line's whole disk-format history.

## In this game

Town Builder is the *office-machine* half of the candidate set in [[bear-on-a-blue-screen]].
If the memory's load-bearing word is "**exploring**" rather than "side-scrolling," this is the
Stickybear that fits: a world you build and then move around inside. If the load-bearing word
is "you control **the bear**," it is not — you drive a car, viewed top-down, and Stickybear
himself is not the avatar. That split is exactly the discrimination that page is for.

Its design also quietly rhymes with something this project does: the child *makes* the world
and then plays inside their own artifact, which is inheritance #1 in
`research/educational-software.md` ("the child makes something, and it runs") and the same
shape as Act 6's **Your Own World** maker (`js/textworld/maker.js`). See [[stickybear]] for
the line and [[stickybear-basketbounce]] for the only title where you *are* the bear.

## The facts

- **Credits:** "developed by **Richard Hefter and Dave Lusby**, and distributed by Optimum
  Resource" — stated identically on the woz-a-day disk-image record and on both 4am/san inc
  preservation writeups (original and rev. 2).
- **Apple II original: 1984**, "runs on any Apple II with 48K" — a 5.25-inch floppy.
- **1988 re-release: a single 3.5-inch (800K) disk**, requiring "an Apple IIgs, Apple //c+,
  Apple //e, or 64K Apple ][+ with a compatible drive controller card." This is the same 1988
  800K re-release wave that [[stickybear-typing]] and Stickybear Word Problems went through.
- **MS-DOS: 1992**, published by Optimum Resource, developed with Brian A. Rice, Inc. and
  Susan Mahoney & Associates, Inc.; the archived record also notes Apple II and Commodore 64
  versions.
- **Gameplay, verbatim from the archived MS-DOS record:** *"The game starts with a green
  expanse of land, awaiting development. The player selects different kinds of buildings to
  erect on the site, which the game instantly builds and automatically connects with roads.
  Then, in the two play modes, the player gets to navigate a little car through the lanes and
  byways, as on a top-town [sic] map, trying to situate the car on a map location or at the
  site of a hidden key described only in cardinal directions (go south! now south-east!)"*
  Perspective is filed as **top-down**; subject as **geography**; genre as Educational /
  Racing-Driving / Simulation.
- **It was reviewed on the Commodore 64.** *COMPUTE!'s Gazette* issue 39 (September 1986)
  carries reviews by David and Robin Minnick of "Stickybear Typing Tutor and Stickybear Town
  Builder" — independent period evidence of C64 releases of both.
- **The MS-DOS archive package is 442 KB zipped, 44 files, launched from `Stickybe\TOWN.EXE`
  under DOSBox** — i.e. a small, single-disk DOS program, not a multi-disk installation.
- **Not established:** the screen palette in any version; whether the DOS release shipped on
  5.25-inch, 3.5-inch, or both; retail price; and whether Stickybear appears on screen during
  the driving modes at all.
