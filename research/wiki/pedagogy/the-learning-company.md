The Learning Company was founded on **May 8, 1980** in Palo Alto, California by **Ann
McCormick, Leslie Grimm, Teri Perl and [[warren-robinett|Warren Robinett]]**, funded
initially by a National Science Foundation grant. It produced [[reader-rabbit]],
[[rockys-boots]], Treasure Mountain and the Super Solvers line including
[[gizmos-and-gadgets]]. The founding fact that matters most to this project is the fourth
name: the programmer who hid the first video game easter egg inside
[[atari-adventure|Adventure]] left Atari and co-founded the educational software house that
taught a generation of children to read.

## In this game

Keystroke Kingdom already reveres Warren Robinett. Typing `adventure` on the title screen
answers `WARREN ROBINETT WAS HERE. 1979.` — described in `.claude/rules/easter-eggs.md` as
"the most thematically perfect egg in the game." What this research pass establishes is that
the egg and the *genre this game belongs to* share a single author: Robinett went from
hiding his name in a cartridge to co-founding The Learning Company, where he designed
[[rockys-boots]], a game that teaches seven-year-olds Boolean logic.

That is a real, citable through-line from [[easter-egg-culture]] to
[[educational-software]] — and it means the proposed learn-to-program act in
`docs/ROADMAP.md` §2 has a historical patron saint who is *already in this codebase*.

TLC is also the company at the centre of the era's consolidation and collapse: it was taken
over by SoftKey in 1995, SoftKey took its name, and the resulting company absorbed MECC
(1971's [[oregon-trail]]) and Broderbund ([[carmen-sandiego]], [[myst]]) before Mattel bought
the whole thing in 1999. Nearly every landmark in [[educational-software]] ends up inside
this one corporate entity — and then the entity ends. That arc is the honest ending of the
floppy-disk education era.

## The facts

- **Founded May 8, 1980, Palo Alto, California**, by **Ann McCormick, Leslie Grimm, Teri
  Perl and Warren Robinett** — the last "a former Atari, Inc. employee who had programmed
  the game Adventure."
- **Founding thesis:** the founders saw in "the Apple II" "an opportunity to teach young
  children concepts of math, reading, science, problem-solving, and thinking skills."
  **Initial funding came from a National Science Foundation grant** — worth noting, because
  it means the house began as a publicly-seeded research idea, not a consumer-software bet.
- **Product lines by age band:** Reader Rabbit (2–8), Treasure Mountain (5–9), Super Solvers
  (7–12), Student Writing & Publishing Center (7–adult), Foreign Language Learning (15–adult).
- **Robinett's own titles at TLC:** he "worked on several educational games there, including
  *Rocky's Boots* and *Robot Odyssey* for the Apple II."
- **Public company, then target:** IPO April 1992; 16 consecutive quarters of revenue and
  profit growth 1992–1995. **In December 1995, "TLC was acquired by SoftKey for $606M in a
  hostile takeover bid,"** after which SoftKey adopted the Learning Company name.
- **The roll-up:** the same SoftKey/TLC entity bought **MECC for $370 million in October
  1995** and **Broderbund on June 22, 1998 for approximately $420 million in stock** — after
  which it "terminated 500 Broderbund employees, representing 42% of the workforce."
- **The end:** Mattel purchased the company in **August 1999**; MECC "shut down in October
  1999 following Mattel's acquisition of SoftKey (renamed The Learning Company)."
- **Correctness caution — two different "Learning Company" eras.** Post-1995, "The Learning
  Company" is SoftKey wearing the name. A product badged TLC in 1997–99 (for example the
  All-Star Typing reissue noted in [[slam-dunk-typing]]) is not necessarily from the
  1980-founded house. Check the year before attributing design intent to the founders.
- **Unverified this session:** the specific NSF grant, the founders' individual backgrounds
  beyond Robinett's, and Douglas Carlston's Computer History Museum profile does not name
  Broderbund's products — it confirms only that Broderbund "commenced operation in 1980" and
  "In 1998 it was acquired by The Learning Company."
