Trevor's instinct — *"kids learn about moon, stars, sun and planets in 1st/2nd grade"* — **checks
out, and it is specifically FIRST grade.** The Next Generation Science Standards' topic
**"1. Space Systems: Patterns and Cycles"** contains performance expectation **1-ESS1-1: "Use
observations of the sun, moon, and stars to describe patterns that can be predicted."** That is
the curricular hook the space act can honestly claim.

## The facts

- **NGSS 1-ESS1-1 (Grade 1).** Performance expectation, verbatim: *"Use observations of the sun,
  moon, and stars to describe patterns that can be predicted."*
  - **Clarification statement:** *"Examples of patterns could include that the sun and moon appear
    to rise in one part of the sky, move across the sky, and set; and stars other than our sun are
    visible at night but not during the day."*
  - **Assessment boundary:** *"Assessment of star patterns is limited to stars being seen at night
    and not during the day."*
  - **Disciplinary core idea ESS1.A (The Universe and its Stars):** *"Patterns of the motion of the
    sun, moon, and stars in the sky can be observed, described, and predicted."*
- **NGSS 1-ESS1-2 (Grade 1)** covers **seasonal patterns of sunrise and sunset**, observed,
  described and predicted.
- The NGSS topic arrangement groups these as **"1. Space Systems: Patterns and Cycles."**
- **The honest limits of the claim** — state these, don't paper over them:
  - **Kindergarten NGSS has no space strand**; the Earth-and-space content at K is weather and
    sunlight's warming effect. So "1st/2nd grade" is right and "from kindergarten" is not.
  - **Grade 1 is about OBSERVABLE PATTERNS, not astronomy.** Planets, the solar system, gravity and
    rockets are **not** grade-1 NGSS content — ESS1.B (Earth and the Solar System) content on the
    solar system sits **later**; 1-ESS1-1's own articulation points forward to **5.ESS1.B**. So the
    defensible claim is: *sun, moon and stars are first-grade curriculum; planets, orbits and
    gravity are the words a child grows into across elementary school.* **That is actually perfect
    for this game**, which is built on content that ages with the player.
  - NGSS is a **voluntary** framework adopted state-by-state (California's own standards page
    mirrors 1-ESS1-1); it is not a national mandate. Don't say "what every US child learns."
  - **[unverified]:** the specific grade-2 space content was **not** checked this pass. Claim
    grade 1 (sourced) and treat "1st/2nd" loosely.
