MECC — the **Minnesota Educational Computing Consortium** (later Corporation) — was created
by the Minnesota Legislature in **1973** as a state-funded body to get computer software into
schools. It is the odd one out among the era's software houses: it was **public**, not a
startup. It published [[oregon-trail]], [[number-munchers]], Word Munchers, Lemonade Stand
and Museum Madness, licensed software to schools at consortium rates, and by 1982 had
distributed a quarter of a million copies worldwide. SoftKey bought it for $370 million in
1995 and it was shut down in 1999.

## In this game

MECC is the reason a generation of adults associate "the computer at school" with a specific
smell of carpet and a specific Apple II boot chime. For [[educational-software]] it plays a
particular role in the argument: **the best-loved educational software of the era was funded
by a state legislature and a National Science Foundation grant** (see
[[the-learning-company]]), not by the market. The market arrived later, consolidated
everything, and closed it.

The practical lesson for Keystroke Kingdom — a game a father is building for his daughter
with no commercial pressure at all — is that this is the *normal* condition for good
children's software, not an eccentric one. The teaching came first; the business came second
and ended it.

## The facts

- **Name and founding:** "Minnesota Educational Computing Consortium (later Corporation),"
  founded **1973** to advance the availability of computer software in education.
- **Public status:** established by the Minnesota Legislature as a state-funded organization,
  operating as a "Joint Powers Authority" with support from the University of Minnesota, the
  state colleges and the Department of Education. **It became a profit-making company in 1983
  while remaining state-owned.**
- **Distribution model:** schools purchased MECC software through **site licenses**. MECC
  "distributed 250,000 copies of MECC software around the world by 1982" and by the 1985–86
  school year "offered more than 300 products," generating roughly **$7 million in annual
  sales**.
- **Key titles:** [[oregon-trail|The Oregon Trail]] (by 1995 generating about one-third of
  MECC's $30 million revenue), [[number-munchers|Number Munchers]], Word Munchers, Lemonade
  Stand, Museum Madness.
- **Apple II push:** MECC began encouraging Apple II adoption in **1978**, which is how
  Oregon Trail reached a screen instead of a teleprinter.
- **End:** acquired by **SoftKey for $370 million in October 1995**; "shut down in October
  1999 following Mattel's acquisition of SoftKey (renamed The Learning Company)." See
  [[the-learning-company]] for the full roll-up.
- **Unverified this session:** what MECC stood for at each stage of its renaming
  (Consortium → Corporation dates), and its total title count beyond the 300+ figure for
  1985–86.
