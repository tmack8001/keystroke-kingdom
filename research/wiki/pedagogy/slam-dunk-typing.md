Slam Dunk Typing (Creative Wonders, 1997) is a basketball-themed CD-ROM typing drill game
— reissued in 1999 as All-Star Typing: Basketball Edition (The Learning Company) — that
wraps word-drill exercises in a sports scoring metaphor: type the word correctly to sink
the shot, miss and you brick it. It has no key-introduction sequence at all.

## In this game

Slam Dunk Typing is the resolved identity of what Keystroke Kingdom's research notes
originally called only "the basketball one" — a half-remembered late-90s edu-CD game from
Trevor's childhood. Once identified, it became the sharpest possible contrast case for
Keystroke Kingdom's One Rule ("the game should only ever stop a player's flow with joy,
never with punishment"): Slam Dunk Typing bricks the shot on a wrong keypress, a small
punishment baked directly into its core loop. Keystroke Kingdom's wrong-keypress behavior
(documented in `.claude/rules/tech.md` and `CLAUDE.md`, and grounded in
[[error-feedback-pedagogy]]) is the deliberate opposite — the
letter simply stays on screen and waits, no shot is ever missed. This comparison is also
why Survival Mode, added 2026-07-11, was designed so carefully: even in its opt-in
challenge tiers (see [[pinball-difficulty-settings]]), a wrong key still never punishes —
only time pressure does. Slam Dunk Typing has no key-introduction curriculum at all, unlike
the canon tutors ([[mario-teaches-typing]], [[mavis-beacon]], [[dance-mat-typing]],
[[typingclub]]) — it is pure word-drill, which is why it's grouped as a genre contrast
rather than a curriculum comparable.

## The facts

- Developer/publisher: Creative Wonders, 1997.
- Format: CD-ROM, basketball-themed word-drill minigames — Passing, Heads Up, Slam Dunk,
  Horse, Game Time, Sports Reporter.
- Mechanic: type the prompted word correctly to make the shot; a wrong keypress bricks it.
  No sequence for introducing new keys — the whole game is drill on words the player is
  assumed to already be able to spell.
- Reissue: **All-Star Typing: Basketball Edition**, The Learning Company, 1999 — same
  product line republished under a new title/publisher.
- **Provenance note:** this game was previously an unidentified reference ("the basketball
  one, late-90s edu-CD era") in earlier research drafts; the citation pass confirmed the
  title, publisher, year, and reissue via three independent archival/metadata sources.
