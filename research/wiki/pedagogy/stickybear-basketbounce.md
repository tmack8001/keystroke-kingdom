Stickybear Basketbounce (Optimum Resource / Weekly Reader Family Software, **1983**, Apple II
and Atari 8-bit, 5.25-inch, 48K) is the one title in the [[stickybear]] catalogue where the
player **is the bear**. You move Stickybear along the ground catching falling objects —
bricks, donuts, stars — into a stack of baskets, ducking what you can't catch and dodging
"moving ground obstacles," with each round adding more to dodge. It is an arcade-action game
wearing an educational badge, and it matters to this project for one reason: of every
Stickybear program whose gameplay could be sourced this session, it is the **only** one in
which the player directly controls a side-on, ground-level, moving Stickybear. That makes it
the single best candidate for the half-memory documented on [[bear-on-a-blue-screen]].

## In this game

Nothing in Keystroke Kingdom is built from Basketbounce, and nothing should be — it is a
catch-the-falling-thing arcade game, and the arcade-cabinet rule (`CLAUDE.md`) has no slot
free. Its value here is **identification**, not adaptation: it is the discriminating exhibit
in [[bear-on-a-blue-screen]], the page that tries to work out which piece of 1980s software
Trevor actually remembers. See [[stickybear]] for the line, [[stickybear-typing]] for the
title this project genuinely descends from, and `research/educational-software.md` for the
lineage argument.

One detail is worth borrowing if a card is ever written: the falling objects are *donuts and
stars*, not hazards. Even the era's arcade-flavoured drill kept its imagery comic — the same
instinct as our "enemies are comic and harmless" rule in `.claude/rules/theme-authoring.md`.

## The facts

- **Year: 1983.** Consistent across every archived artifact — the Apple II disk images, the
  Atari 8-bit Weekly Reader Family Software release, and the 4am/san inc preservation records.
- **Credits:** "developed by **Richard Hefter, Janie Worthington, and Steve Worthington**, and
  distributed by Optimum Resource." Two independent preservation records (woz-a-day and the
  4am crack writeup, plus a later rev. 2) state the same three names.
- **Machine:** "It runs on any Apple II with **48K**" — i.e. a 5.25-inch floppy machine of the
  era. Also released for **Atari 8-bit** computers by **Weekly Reader Family Software**.
- **What the player does**, from the Apple II package copy archived with the disk image,
  quoted verbatim: *"Your goal is to win as many points as you can by catching colorful,
  bouncing, falling objects — including bricks, donuts, and stars — before running out of
  baskets. While trying to accumulate points, you must duck falling objects and dodge moving
  ground obstacles… New obstacles and falling objects in each successive round require more
  and more skill in order to score."*
- **Independently corroborated from the Atari 8-bit packaging:** *"The goal is to help
  Stickybear win points by catching colorful falling objects — including donuts, stars, and
  bricks — before running out of baskets. As levels progress, obstacles and moving ground
  elements increase the difficulty."*
- **Two-player scoring:** *"Keep score on the computer to see which player's on top!"* — a
  hot-seat family game, not a solo drill.
- **It is NOT a side-scroller.** Nothing in any source describes a scrolling world or
  exploration. The player-controlled bear moves laterally on a playfield with "moving ground
  obstacles"; that is as close to "side-scrolling" as the Stickybear catalogue gets, and the
  distinction is the crux of [[bear-on-a-blue-screen]].
- **The original shipped broken.** A collector's archived copy notes: *"the original bugs out
  at level 4 so after several years I have found enough versions to finally make it work."*
  Treat as a single collector's account, not an established fact — but it is a very 1983
  detail.
- **Not established:** the screen colours (no source read this session describes the
  palette), a Commodore 64 or IBM PC release of *this* title, sales, or reviews.
