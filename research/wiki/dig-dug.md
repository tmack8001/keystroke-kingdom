Dig Dug (Namco, February 1982), designed by Masahisa Ikegami, is the underground digging
game whose music plays only when the character moves — a creative workaround because
composer Yuriko Keino couldn't make realistic footstep sounds, so she wrote a short melody
instead.

## In this game

Dig Dug is not an assigned Keystroke Kingdom cabinet but shares the Namco Galaga board with
[[galaga]], and its "kawaii" character design sits within the same early-golden-age era as
[[q-bert]].

## The facts

- Developer: Namco — designed by Masahisa Ikegami, assisted by [[galaga]] designer Shigeru
  Yokoyama; sprites by Hiroshi "Mr. Dotman" Ono. Released February 20, 1982 (Japan), April
  1982 (North America).
- **Correction (design credit):** "board designed by Shigeichi Ishimura" overstates his
  role — Ishimura was the board's hardware engineer; the game itself was designed by
  Masahisa Ikegami.
- Visual style: colorful underground cross-section, layered dirt (color changes per stage),
  round Pookas with oversized goggles, dragon-like Fygars.
- Input: four-way joystick (digs in the direction pressed) plus one pump button (inflates
  enemies).
- Technical: Namco Galaga arcade system board (same hardware as [[galaga]]). Tile-based
  graphics with multi-color sprites. 8-bit integer overflow kill screen at round 256.
