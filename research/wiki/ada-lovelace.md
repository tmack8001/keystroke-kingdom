Ada Lovelace wrote what is standardly regarded as the first computer program — a
published algorithm for computing Bernoulli numbers on Charles Babbage's never-built
Analytical Engine — in 1843, more than a century before any hardware existed to run it.

## In this game

Level 4's history card spells `ada`. This is the first level where the home row is fully
unlocked (A and the [[the-eighth-key|eighth key]] are Level 4's new keys), making it the
first level whose completion could plausibly spell a real word — and the curriculum

## The facts

- **1843:** Ada Lovelace publishes Note G, an algorithm for computing Bernoulli numbers,
  as an appendix to her translation of an Italian memoir on Babbage's proposed Analytical
  Engine. The Computer History Museum describes her as "often referred to as 'the first
  programmer.'"
- The Analytical Engine was never built in her lifetime — the program predates working
  hardware by over 100 years.
- There is genuine, mild academic debate about strict priority (Babbage himself wrote
  earlier, unpublished code snippets), but the "first programmer" framing is the standard
  one used by the primary institutional source (CHM) itself, and the game's card claim
  was verified as accurate with no correction needed.
