Phrack is the long-running underground hacker/phreaker e-zine whose first issue went
out over a BBS on November 17, 1985, founded by teenagers Taran King and Knight
Lightning (real name Craig Neidorf). Its February 1989 publication of an internal
BellSouth document about the E911 emergency system triggered a federal prosecution
that collapsed at trial — one of the sparks that led to the founding of the Electronic
Frontier Foundation. It is still published today, four decades later.

## The facts

- **November 17, 1985:** the first issue of Phrack was distributed over a bulletin
  board system by Taran King. Phrack's own articles are called "philes" — text files
  covering phreaking, hacking, and computer-security topics, written by the community.
- **Founders:** Taran King and Knight Lightning, both teenagers embedded in the
  phreak/hacker BBS scene. Knight Lightning's real identity, Craig Neidorf, became
  public through his 1990 prosecution (below); Taran King's real name is reported by
  one source as Travis Campbell, but this is a lower-confidence, non-institutional
  attribution — treat it as unconfirmed.
- **The E911 incident (February 1989, issue 24):** Phrack published an internal
  BellSouth administrative document about the Enhanced 911 emergency-call system.
  This led to Secret Service raids and a federal prosecution of Neidorf on ten felony
  counts (fraud and transportation of stolen property), with a maximum possible
  penalty reported at 65 years. The Electronic Frontier Foundation intervened,
  showing that Bellcore itself sold comparable documentation for about $13 — against
  the roughly $80,000 valuation prosecutors had placed on the leaked document. The
  government dropped all charges and a mistrial was declared on **July 27, 1990**,
  mid-trial. The case is widely credited as a catalyst in EFF's founding.
- [[hacker-manifesto|"The Conscience of a Hacker"]] (a.k.a. "The Hacker Manifesto"),
  by Loyd Blankenship ("The Mentor," a [[legion-of-doom|Legion of Doom]] member), was
  first published inside **Phrack, Volume 1, Issue 7, Phile 3**, in 1986.
- **Still active today:** Phrack ran roughly 69+ issues from 1985 through 2017 with
  gaps, moved from BBS distribution to the web, and continued publishing — issue #71
  arrived August 19, 2024, and issue #72, a 40th-anniversary edition, was distributed
  (~15,000 printed copies) at hacker conferences in 2025.
