"Rubber duck debugging" — explaining broken code line by line to an inanimate object
(traditionally a rubber duck) until the bug reveals itself — was named and popularized
by Andy Hunt and Dave Thomas's 1999 book *The Pragmatic Programmer*, via an anecdote
about a research assistant named Greg Pugh who carried one for exactly this purpose.

## The facts

- The term and anecdote come from *The Pragmatic Programmer: From Journeyman to
  Master* by Andrew Hunt and David Thomas, first published in 1999.
- The book describes a research assistant, Greg Pugh, who carried a rubber duck
  specifically so he could explain his code to it, line by line, out loud — the act of
  narrating the problem to something that "does not need to say a word" is what
  surfaces the bug.
- The technique has since become a widely used, semi-official practice across
  professional software engineering — some workplaces keep literal rubber ducks at
  desks for this purpose.
