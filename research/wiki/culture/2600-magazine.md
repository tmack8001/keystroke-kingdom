*2600: The Hacker Quarterly* is a print magazine founded in January 1984 by Eric
Corley (writing under the pen name "Emmanuel Goldstein," borrowed from the resistance
leader in Orwell's *1984*) and David Ruderman, who gave the publication its name.
Named after the 2600 Hz phone-phreaking tone (see [[phone-phreaking-2600hz]]), it
began as a three-sheet newsletter with a circulation of 25 and is still published
quarterly today — over 40 years later.

## The facts

- **Founded January 1984** by Eric Corley and David Ruderman, begun as a three-sheet
  newsletter with a circulation of only 25 copies.
- **"Emmanuel Goldstein"** is Corley's pen name, taken from the unseen resistance
  leader in George Orwell's *1984* — Corley cofounded the magazine partly in response
  to his own 1983 arrest for alleged unauthorized access to a corporate email system
  (the charges were later dropped), channeling the experience into building a printed
  forum for the community instead.
- **David Ruderman gave the magazine its name**, drawn from the 2600 Hz tone AT&T's
  network used to signal an idle long-distance trunk line — the same tone phone
  phreaks (notably [[phone-phreaking-2600hz|"Captain Crunch," John Draper]]) could
  produce with a toy whistle to seize free long-distance calls. Ruderman left the
  magazine after about three years.
- **Content:** articles on system security, encryption, phone-network exploration,
  reader letters, legal advice, and hacking stories — both a technical journal and,
  per its own self-description, a "muckraking magazine" exposing corporate and
  government misdeeds.
- **Still published today**, released quarterly (first Friday following each season
  change — usually January, April, July, October).
