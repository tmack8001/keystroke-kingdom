The video game "easter egg" — a hidden message or feature planted by a developer for
players to discover — traces its most widely cited origin to [[warren-robinett]]'s
hidden name in [[atari-adventure|Adventure]] (1979), and its conventions (secret magic
words, hidden rooms, developer signatures) form a throughline connecting text-adventure
culture, cheat codes, and today's console-log banners and view-source discoveries.

## In this game

Keystroke Kingdom's entire hidden-egg layer is built on the thesis that "the game's
deepest theme is that typing unlocks computing. Hacker culture is that theme's native
folklore. Every egg rewards the exact instinct the game exists to create — poke at the
machine and see what it says back." This page synthesizes the throughline the hub doc
traces from Robinett forward:

- **[[warren-robinett]] / [[atari-adventure]] (1979/1980):** the founding act — hiding a
  name because credit was denied. The origin point of "video game easter egg" as a
  cultural practice.
- **[[xyzzy]] / [[colossal-cave-adventure]] (1976–77):** the sibling tradition of magic
  words with a fixed, known response — the joke depends on getting the canonical text
  exactly right (`NOTHING HAPPENS.`).
- **[[konami-code]] (1986/1988):** the input-sequence cheat code as its own genre,
  originally a testing artifact left in by accident, later repurposed by other games
  (Contra) into a cultural meme independent of its origin.
- **[[iddqd-doom]] (1993):** the cheat-code tradition intersecting with a game's own
  design philosophy — Keystroke Kingdom refuses it on principle rather than granting
  it, turning a well-known code into a joke about its own no-punishment law.
- **[[view-source-culture]]:** the parallel, non-game tradition of curiosity-driven
  discovery — "poke at the page and see what it says back" — that the game's console
  banner and binary HTML comment both draw on directly.

Design rules governing all of the above (see [[hacker-culture]] for the full list):
summoned by name only, never interrupts play; kid-safe on discovery, parent-delightful
on recognition; era-authentic, ideally act-matched; cheat codes for lives get refused;
the console is the only breadcrumb, deeper eggs stay unhinted.
