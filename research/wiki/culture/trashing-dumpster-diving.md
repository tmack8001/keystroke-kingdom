"Trashing" (also called "garbology" or dumpster diving) was the practice of searching
phone-company and corporate trash for discarded technical manuals, printouts, and
internal documents — a decidedly low-tech but effective information-gathering method
used by phone phreaks and early hackers from the mid-1970s through the 1980s, before
office paper shredders became standard equipment. The term "dumpster diving" is first
attested in print in 1983, per the Oxford English Dictionary.

## The facts

- In phreak/hacker slang, dumpster diving was called **"garbology" or "trashing."**
  Phone phreaks and computer hackers used it, starting in the mid-1970s and through
  the 1980s, to find system manuals and other information useful for social
  engineering and getting around security.
- **Jerry Neil Schneider** is the best-documented early example: in the early 1970s
  he found discarded Pacific Bell equipment-ordering manuals in company dumpsters and
  used them to fraudulently order real telephone equipment. His story is recounted in
  A.J. Weberman's 1980 book *My Life in Garbology*.
- Masters of Deception members and phreaker Susan Headley are also cited as
  practitioners who used trashing to learn about telephone company systems.
- The specific term **"dumpster diving" first appears in print in 1983,** according
  to the Oxford English Dictionary.

## Kid-safety / framing note

This is naturally kid-safe if framed as **detective work** — "looking at what a
company threw away to figure out how its machines worked" — rather than as
trespassing or theft. Explicitly avoid endorsing it as a how-to; trash on private
property is legally risky in most places today. The wholesome angle: curiosity about
how systems work, satisfied by unconventional means, matching the game's ethos of
"poke at the machine and see what it says back."
