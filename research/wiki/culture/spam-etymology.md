"Spam" as a term for unwanted, repetitive messages traces to a Monty Python's Flying
Circus sketch first broadcast **December 15, 1970**, in which Viking customers in a
café drown out every conversation by chanting "Spam, Spam, Spam..." over a menu where
every single dish contains Spam. Early online communities (MUDs, chat channels,
Usenet) borrowed the chant literally to describe someone flooding a channel with
repeated text; by the early-to-mid 1990s the word had generalized to unsolicited bulk
email — even though the very first mass unsolicited email (sent by Gary Thuerk to
about 600 ARPANET addresses in 1978) predates the "spam" name itself by more than a
decade.

## The facts

- **December 15, 1970:** the Spam sketch airs as the closing segment of Monty
  Python's Flying Circus episode 25 — Viking patrons repeatedly interrupt with a song
  ("Spam, Spam, Spam, Spam… Lovely Spam! Wonderful Spam!"), escalating whenever a
  waitress tries to quiet them.
- **The online-culture borrowing:** early internet users flooding forums, chat
  channels, or MUDs with repeated junk text described it as "spamming" because it so
  closely resembled the sketch's escalating repetition drowning out normal
  conversation; the extension of "spamming" from disruptive chat-channel text to
  unsolicited bulk email is documented as occurring substantially between 1990 and
  1994.
- **A separate, earlier fact often conflated with the naming:** the very first mass
  unsolicited commercial email is credited to **Gary Thuerk, who in 1978 sent a
  message to roughly 600 ARPANET addresses** and was reprimanded for it — this
  predates the word "spam" being applied to email by well over a decade; the *event*
  (1978) and the *name* (early '90s) are two separate historical facts that shouldn't
  be merged into one date.
