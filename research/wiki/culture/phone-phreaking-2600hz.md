2600 Hz was the tone AT&T's network used to signal an idle long-distance trunk line —
phone phreaks (notably "Captain Crunch," using a cereal-box whistle that happened to
produce the exact frequency) could exploit it to seize free long-distance calls. The
tone gives its name to *2600: The Hacker Quarterly* (founded 1984). No source
documents an intentional connection between this tone and Atari's naming of the Atari
2600 console — that link is folklore/wordplay, not documented history.

## The facts

- 2600 Hz is confirmed as AT&T's idle-trunk signaling tone.
- The "Cap'n Crunch whistle" (John Draper) produced this exact tone from a cereal-box
  toy, allowing seizure of long-distance trunk lines.
- *2600: The Hacker Quarterly*, founded 1984, is named after the tone.
- **The Atari 2600 connection is explicitly hedged, not asserted.** One source
  (Computer Hope) frames any link between the tone and the console's name as
  "allegedly" — no source found during the citation pass documents an actual,
  intentional naming connection. This page (like the hub doc) keeps the phrasing
  hedged as "punningly" coincidental rather than stating it as fact.
