"Cyberpunk" was coined by author Bruce Bethke, who began writing a short story about
technologically-immersed young people in the spring of 1980 and, needing a title,
squished together "cyber-" (from cybernetics) and "punk" (music's anti-establishment
slang for troublemakers) — landing on "Cyberpunk" as the title itself. The story was
published in *Amazing Stories* magazine in 1983. The term went on to name an entire
science-fiction subgenre after William Gibson's *Neuromancer* (1984) popularized its
aesthetic the following year.

## The facts

- **Bruce Bethke** coined "cyberpunk," beginning the short story in the **spring of
  1980**; it was **published in 1983 in *Amazing Stories* magazine** (Ziff-Davis).
- **How he built the word:** by deliberately matching a word for technology
  ("cyber-," from cybernetics) with a word for troublemakers ("punk," borrowed from
  1970s punk-rock's anti-establishment tone) until he found a pairing that felt
  right.
- **The term spread as a genre label** the following year, after William Gibson's
  novel *Neuromancer* (1984) popularized its themes and aesthetic — Bethke's word
  became the established name for the technological-rebellion science-fiction
  subgenre, even though his own story predates *Neuromancer*.
