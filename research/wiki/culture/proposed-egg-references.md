## The facts

### sudo / xkcd 149
- [xkcd #149, "Sandwich"](https://xkcd.com/149/), published August 28, 2006, is the
  primary source of "sudo make me a sandwich."
- `sudo(8)`'s actual function (execute as superuser/another user per policy) is
  confirmed via the official Linux man page.

### The white rabbit / The Matrix
- *The Matrix* released in US theaters March 31, 1999.
- The literal on-screen computer message "Follow the white rabbit," sent to Neo early
  in the film, is confirmed via a film transcript source.

### Rubber duck debugging
- The name/anecdote (a researcher, Greg Pugh, who carried a literal rubber duck) is
  **popularized by** *The Pragmatic Programmer* (Hunt & Thomas, 1999) — sources
  support "popularized by," not "invented by," since the underlying practice of
  talking through a problem to an inanimate listener predates the book's specific
  naming/anecdote.

### Dial-up modem handshake / BBS era
- The dial-up handshake's screeching sound is explained technically (echo suppression
  disabling, tone/phase signaling) by Hackaday.
- The first public dial-up BBS is CBBS, Chicago, 1978, created by Ward Christensen and
  Randy Suess — confirmed by IEEE Spectrum, which also frames BBS culture as a
  social-media precursor.

### Y2K
- See [[y2k-bug]] for the fully-sourced version of this event (already built as the
  Level 19 history card). The two-digit-year storage cause and the largely-averted
  outcome are confirmed by Wikipedia and National Geographic Education.

### hello world (second proposed use)
- See [[hello-world]] for full sourcing — the earliest documented version appears in
  Kernighan's 1972 internal Bell Labs tutorial "A Tutorial Introduction to the
  Language B," predating K&R's 1978 book by six years. This proposed egg would be a
  *second*, separate use of the same history at the Level 25 free-type mode, distinct
  from the already-built history card.
