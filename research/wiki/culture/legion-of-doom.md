The Legion of Doom (LOD) was an invitation-only group of skilled phone phreaks and
computer hackers founded in May 1984 by a hacker using the handle "Lex Luthor" —
borrowed, like the group's name, from Superman's villain team. Widely considered the
most capable hacking group of the mid-to-late 1980s, LOD published its own technical
journals for the underground community and became locked in a rivalry with a rival
New York crew, the [[masters-of-deception|Masters of Deception]], known as the "Great
Hacker War." Several members were prosecuted in 1990–91 and the group wound down by
around 1991.

## The facts

- **Founded May 1984** by "Lex Luthor," after he split from an earlier group called
  Knights of Shadow (KOS) when his suggestions for new members were rejected.
  The name — Legion of Doom — comes directly from the villain team in the
  Super Friends cartoon, matching the comic-book-flavored handles common in the scene
  (Lex Luthor, Erik Bloodaxe, The Mentor).
- **Invitation-only,** organized initially through private bulletin board systems;
  membership was based on demonstrated technical skill in phreaking and hacking.
- LOD **published its own "Legion of Doom Technical Journals,"** contributing
  legitimate technical knowledge to the wider underground community — members
  maintained they generally didn't cause direct damage to systems, though
  authorities disputed this characterization.
- **Considered the most capable hacking group in the world** during its most active
  years, roughly 1984–1991.
- **The rivalry with Masters of Deception** escalated into what's been called the
  "Great Hacker War" (turn of the 1990s); LOD member Erik Bloodaxe is described as
  leading the group's side of the conflict.
- **Legal consequences:** multiple LOD members were arrested and prosecuted between
  1985 and 1990 for offenses including toll fraud and credit fraud (documented in the
  federal sentencing memorandum for *US v. Grant, Darden and Riggs*, December 1990).
  Loyd Blankenship — "The Mentor," author of [[hacker-manifesto|the Hacker
  Manifesto]] — retired from the scene after a US Secret Service raid in the early
  1990s.

## Kid-safety / framing note

Frame this as two rival clubs of extremely curious teenagers competing to understand
phone systems better than anyone else — like rival chess clubs, not a crime story. Be
honest but light that some members' activities did get them in real trouble with the
law (toll fraud, credit fraud); this is the natural place to land the game's own
distinction between "curious exploring" and "actually breaking things or taking
people's money," without dwelling on specifics.
