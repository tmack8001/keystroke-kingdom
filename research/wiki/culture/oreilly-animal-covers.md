Technical books from the publisher **O'Reilly** carry a black-and-white **animal
engraving** on the cover — a tarsier, a camel, a slender loris — and they have since the
mid-1980s. The reason is the best kind of accident: designer **Edie Freedman**, handed a
stack of UNIX handbooks, found the command names so strange that they *"sounded to me
like words that might come out of Dungeons and Dragons"*, went looking for imagery to
match, and *"came across some odd-looking animal engravings from the 19th century."*
O'Reilly's own menagerie page now lists **more than 1,500** animals.

## The facts

- **Who and when.** **Edie Freedman**, approached — per O'Reilly's own telling — by a
  neighbour who worked at the company in the **mid-1980s**. ⚠️ **The specific year 1986,
  and the specific first three titles (*Learning the vi Editor*, *sed & awk*,
  *lex & yacc*), appeared only in a search summary this session.** O'Reilly's own fetched
  page says *"mid-1980s"* and names *sed & awk* with slender lorises, but does **not**
  give a year or a definitive first-three list. **A card must say "the mid-1980s."**
- **Why animals — in Freedman's own words.** O'Reilly's history page quotes her on the
  UNIX vocabulary: it *"sounded to me like words that might come out of Dungeons and
  Dragons, a game that was popular with a geeky (mostly male) subculture."* Looking for
  matching imagery, *"I came across some odd-looking animal engravings from the 19th
  century."*
- **What the strange words were.** The commands whose names prompted this are real and
  still in use: `vi`, `sed`, `awk`, `lex`, `yacc`, `uucp`, `curses`. ⚠️ That full list
  came from a search summary; the fetched O'Reilly page names *sed & awk* only. The card
  should name at most the ones the fetched page supports, or none.
- **Before the animals, the books were plain.** Search summaries describe mid-1980s
  O'Reilly UNIX titles sold by mail order with *"plain brown covers held together by
  staples"*, and two bookstore titles with designer covers Tim O'Reilly rejected.
  O'Reilly's own fetched page confirms only the second half — *"the first two titles that
  were sold into bookstores"* had covers the company was not satisfied with. The
  brown-paper detail is **not** asserted here.
- **The count.** O'Reilly's own animals index, fetched this session, is paginated and
  states **"1 to 20 of 1510"** — i.e. **over 1,500** animals catalogued, each tied to its
  book. Its only introductory line is *"What's that animal on the front of your O'Reilly
  book? You'll find it listed here."* ⚠️ **This figure is a live web page and will drift.**
  Card copy should say **"more than 1,500"**, never a precise number.
- **The engravings' source.** O'Reilly's fetched history page says only *"19th century"*
  and that the company keeps *"a large collection of original 19th century engravings."*
  The widely-repeated attribution to the **Dover Pictorial Archives** of copyright-free
  18th- and 19th-century engravings appeared in a **search summary only** and is **not
  asserted**.
- **Not everyone liked them.** Search summaries report internal resistance — some at
  O'Reilly thought the animals weird or a bit scary — with Tim O'Reilly backing the
  quirkiness as a way to stand out. Plausible and charming; **not in the fetched text**,
  so not asserted.
