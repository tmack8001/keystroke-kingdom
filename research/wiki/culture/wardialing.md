War dialing (wardialing) is the practice of using a modem to automatically call every
phone number in a range, searching for other modems, computers, bulletin board
systems, or fax machines that answer. The technique predates its name — phreaks
called it "hammer dialing" or "demon dialing" earlier — but took the name that stuck
after the 1983 film *WarGames* showed its hero's computer dialing every number in
Sunnyvale, California to find other computers. Keystroke Kingdom already references
this same film via its [[wopr-wargames|WOPR/`joshua` secret]].

## The facts

- **Definition:** a technique to automatically scan a list of telephone numbers,
  usually every number in a local area code, to find modems, computers, bulletin
  board systems, and fax machines.
- **The term's origin:** from the 1983 film *WarGames*, in which the protagonist
  programs his computer to dial every phone number in Sunnyvale, California, looking
  for other computer systems — a scene that introduced the technique to a wide
  audience, including members of the real-world hacker group The 414s.
- **Prior terminology:** before the film, practitioners called the same technique
  "hammer dialing" or "demon dialing" — these terms faded once the WarGames-derived
  name caught on.
- **Use in practice:** hobbyists used it for exploration; malicious "crackers" used
  it to find entry points into systems or capture voicemail greetings for guessing
  accounts. By 1985 commercial wardialing software was being sold for Commodore
  computers, and such programs became common on BBSes through the 1980s–90s. The
  practice's popularity later prompted some US states to pass laws against automated
  dialing without intent to communicate with a person.
