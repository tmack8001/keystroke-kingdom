ELIZA, written by Joseph Weizenbaum at MIT and published in January 1966, is widely
recognized as the first chatbot — a pattern-matching program whose famous DOCTOR script
simulated a Rogerian therapist by reflecting the user's own statements back as
questions.

## In this game

Level 15's history card spells `eliza` — Z unlocks at Level 15, the alphabet's last
letter releasing the first machine you could talk to. Card text: *"The first chatbot.
ELIZA talked by turning your words into questions."* ELIZA is also a title-screen
easter egg: typing `eliza` triggers `HOW DOES TYPING MAKE YOU FEEL?`, and it whispers on
the Level 20 terminal — see [[hacker-culture]]. See [[computing-history-moments]] for
the full 17-card table.

## In-game easter egg reference

Per the hacker-culture hub: typing `eliza` on the title screen → `HOW DOES TYPING MAKE
YOU FEEL?` — a Rogerian-style reflection joke, in character for ELIZA's own mechanic of
turning statements into questions.

## The facts

- **January 1966:** ELIZA published in *Communications of the ACM*, developed by Joseph
  Weizenbaum at MIT (development running 1964–67).
- The DOCTOR script's mechanic — reflecting statements back as questions in the style of
  Rogerian psychotherapy — is directly confirmed by the original 1966 paper text.
- "First chatbot" is the standard framing, used even by the Computer History Museum
  itself, which holds a circa-1966 teleprinter transcript in its collection.
- Weizenbaum's own secretary reportedly asked to be left alone with the program to talk
  privately — the famous origin anecdote of what's now called "the ELIZA effect."
- Verdict: accurate, no correction needed.
