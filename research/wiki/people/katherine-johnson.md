Katherine Johnson was a **human computer** at NACA/NASA Langley from 1953, whose hand calculations
of orbital trajectories John Glenn personally asked to have checked before his 1962 orbital
flight — *"If she says they're good, then I'm ready to go."* She counted her greatest contribution
as the calculations that synchronised Apollo's Lunar Module with the Command and Service Module in
lunar orbit. Presidential Medal of Freedom, **2015**.

## The facts

- Joined **NACA at Langley in the summer of 1953**, hired into the segregated all-Black
  **West Area Computing** section, then moved into the **Maneuver Loads Branch** of the Flight
  Research Division, where a temporary assignment became permanent. She stayed as NACA became
  NASA in 1958.
- **John Glenn, Friendship 7, 1962.** The IBM machines producing the orbital trajectory were not
  fully trusted. NASA's own biography records the request to *"get the girl to run the same
  numbers through the same equations… by hand, on her desktop mechanical calculating machine"* and
  Glenn's *"If she says they're good, then I'm ready to go."*
- **Apollo.** By her own assessment her greatest contribution was the *"calculations that helped
  synch Project Apollo's Lunar Module with the lunar-orbiting Command and Service Module."*
- **Output and honours:** author or co-author of **26 research reports**; retired **1986**;
  **Presidential Medal of Freedom, 2015** (do not confuse with [[margaret-hamilton]]'s, 2016).
- **Attribution caution:** she is the best-known of the Langley human computers but not the only
  one — **Dorothy Vaughan** and **Mary Jackson** belong in the same story. Do not let one name
  absorb the group (the same error class as the ENIAC six; see [[eniac]]).
