**George Boole (2 November 1815 – 8 December 1864)** is the man who turned *true* and *false*
into algebra. In **1847** he published a pamphlet — *The mathematical analysis of logic, being
an essay towards a calculus of deductive reasoning* — arguing that logic belonged with
mathematics rather than with philosophy, and in **1854** he expanded it into *An investigation
of the laws of thought*. From **1849 until his death in 1864** he was the first Professor of
Mathematics at Queen's College, Cork (now University College Cork). He never saw a computer
and could not have imagined one. Yet the two-valued algebra he invented is the algebra every
digital machine on earth runs on — which is why a variable that can only be *true* or *false*
is still called a **boolean**.

## The facts

- **Dates.** Born **2 November 1815**; died **8 December 1864** (MacTutor; Linda Hall Library
  gives the same birth date). Aged 49 at death — **he predates every electronic computer by
  roughly eighty years.**
- **1847, the pamphlet.** UCC gives the full title: *"The mathematical analysis of logic,
  being an essay towards a calculus of deductive reasoning"* (1847). Linda Hall Library lists
  it as *"The Mathematical Analysis of Logic"* (1847) — and notes, usefully for provenance,
  that the library **does not own a copy** of the 1847 work, only the 1854 one.
  ⚠️ **MacTutor's Boole biography does not mention the 1847 work at all**; a lane working only
  from MacTutor would have dated Boolean algebra to 1854. Two of the three sources carry 1847,
  which is why it is safe.
- **1854, the book.** *"An investigation of the laws of thought: on which are founded the
  mathematical theories of logic and probabilities"* (UCC; MacTutor gives the same title with
  minor orthographic differences; Linda Hall shortens it). MacTutor on its consequence: *"It
  began the algebra of logic called Boolean algebra which now finds application in computer
  construction, switching circuits etc."*
- **Cork.** MacTutor: *"Boole was appointed to the chair of mathematics at Queens College,
  Cork in 1849"*, taking up the post in November. UCC's own heritage page: *"George Boole FRS
  (1815-1864), first Professor of Mathematics at Queen's College, Cork, 1849-1864."*
- **The line to computing.** MacTutor: *"Boolean algebra has wide applications in the design
  of modern computers. Boole's work has to be seen as a fundamental step in today's computer
  revolution."* UCC: *"Boolean logic is credited with laying the foundations for the
  information age."*
- **Claude Shannon is the link — and his date needs care.** Linda Hall Library: *"When, much
  later, in 1938, a young engineer named Claude Shannon was looking for a way to design
  switching circuits for telephones, he happened upon Boolean algebra"* and found it ideal for
  logic gates; *"Shannon went on to found information theory, with Boolean logic at its heart.
  Modern computer designers cannot do without it."*
  ⚠️ **Three different years are in circulation for one piece of work** — Shannon's MIT
  thesis *A symbolic analysis of relay and switching circuits* was, per search summaries,
  **submitted 10 August 1937**; Linda Hall dates the encounter to **1938** (the year the paper
  appeared in the AIEE *Transactions*); and **MIT's own DSpace record gives "Date Issued:
  1940"** (Shannon's degrees were conferred in 1940). This is the created-vs-published trap
  three deep. **Put no Shannon date on a card.** "Nearly a hundred years later" is safe and
  true against all three.
- **What is NOT claimed.** That Boole invented logic (Aristotle), that he invented binary
  (Leibniz and others long precede him), or that he anticipated computers (he did not, and
  every source frames the connection as something *later* engineers noticed). The precise
  claim is: **Boole made logic into algebra, and that algebra turned out to be what machines
  needed.**
