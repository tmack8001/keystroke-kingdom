Alan Turing was the principal designer of the Bombe, the machine that broke Naval
Enigma at Bletchley Park during World War II, and in 1950 published "Computing Machinery
and Intelligence," which proposed the question "can machines think?" and the Turing
Test.

## The facts

- **1939–1940:** Turing is principal designer of the Bombe, used to break Naval Enigma
  at Bletchley Park.
- **1950:** Turing publishes "Computing Machinery and Intelligence" in *Mind* (vol. LIX,
  issue 236), proposing the "can machines think?" question and the Turing Test.
- The card's single year attribution (implicitly 1950 per the source table) spans two
  real eras compressed into one: codebreaking dates to 1939–1945, "can machines think?"
  is specifically the 1950 paper. This is a defensible choice — 1950 is the more
  precisely citable date and often treated as Turing's headline "computer science
  begins here" moment.
- Verdict: accurate; no change needed to the card, though internally the codebreaking
  predates the paper by roughly a decade.
