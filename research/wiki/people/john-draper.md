John Thomas Draper, born March 11, 1943, is the phone phreak nicknamed "Captain
Crunch" (or "Crunchman") for discovering — via fellow phreaker Denny Teresi — that a
toy bosun's whistle packaged free inside boxes of Cap'n Crunch cereal produced
exactly the 2600 Hz tone needed to seize AT&T's long-distance trunk lines for free.
His phreaking (including building "blue box" tone-generator devices) made him a minor
celebrity, connected him with a young Steve Jobs and Steve Wozniak, and led to
several arrests across the 1970s–80s; he later wrote EasyWriter (1979), one of the
first word processors for the Apple II.

## The facts

- **Born March 11, 1943.**
- **The whistle discovery:** fellow phreaker Denny Teresi told Draper that the toy
  bosun's whistle free inside Cap'n Crunch cereal boxes produced the exact 2600 Hz
  tone AT&T's network used to signal an idle long-distance trunk — blowing it into
  the phone let a caller seize the trunk and make free long-distance calls.
- **Blue boxes:** Draper built electronic devices ("blue boxes") that reproduced the
  tones needed to manipulate the phone network more reliably than the whistle alone,
  central to the phreaking community's technical exploration in the early 1970s.
- **1971 celebrity moment:** journalist Ron Rosenbaum's Esquire article on phone
  phreaking relied heavily on interviews with Draper and made him a minor celebrity
  in the subculture.
- **Apple connection:** Draper worked as an independent contractor for Apple in 1977
  designing the "Charley Board" (to connect Apple II computers to phone lines,
  ultimately canceled), and later wrote **EasyWriter (1979)** — one of the first word
  processors for the Apple II — while in a work-release program.
- **Legal record (kept brief for kid framing):** Draper faced several prosecutions
  over the 1970s–80s tied to phone-fraud activity, including time in federal prison
  in the mid-1970s. He later worked in software, including at Autodesk from 1986.
