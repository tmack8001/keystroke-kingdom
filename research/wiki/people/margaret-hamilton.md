Margaret Hamilton led the team at the **MIT Instrumentation Laboratory** that wrote the on-board
flight software for NASA's **Apollo** Command and Lunar Modules — and, wanting that work to carry
the same standing as the other engineering disciplines around her, she **made up the term
"software engineering."** The Computer History Museum states it flatly: she *"made up the term
'software engineering' to distinguish it from other kinds of engineering."* She received the NASA
Exceptional Space Act Award (2003) and the Presidential Medal of Freedom (2016).

## The facts

- **Role:** Director of the **Software Engineering Division** at MIT's Instrumentation Laboratory;
  she *"led the team that created the on-board flight software for NASA's Apollo command modules
  and lunar modules."* The Instrumentation Laboratory contracted with NASA in **1961** to develop
  the Apollo guidance system. She had previously worked on the **SAGE** air-defence system
  (1961–63).
- **The term.** Attribute with the right verb. The Computer History Museum says she **"made up the
  term 'software engineering'"**, wanting to give software *"legitimacy"* comparable to other
  engineering disciplines. MIT's own account uses the softer **"credited with popularizing the
  concept of software engineering itself."** NASA's Apollo Lunar Surface Journal page says the
  concepts she and her team created *"became the building blocks for modern 'software
  engineering.'"* **Safe phrasing for a card: "she coined the term 'software engineering' — and
  fought for it."** Note the honest nuance: *coined* is CHM's claim, *popularized* is MIT's; both
  institutions agree she is the reason the phrase exists in common use.
- **What her software actually did.** NASA: her *"concepts of asynchronous software, priority
  scheduling, end-to-end testing, and man-in-the-loop decision capability… became the foundation
  for ultra-reliable software design."* During the Apollo 11 descent, roughly **three minutes
  before touchdown**, the software **overrode a command** to switch the flight computer's priority
  processing to a radar system — the mission continued.
- **The photograph.** The famous 1969 image of Hamilton standing beside a stack of printed Apollo
  source-code listings taller than she is; MIT's original caption describes her *"beside listings
  of the software developed by her and the team she was in charge of, the LM and CM on-board
  flight software team."*
- **In her own words (2009, via MIT):** *"There was no second chance. We knew that. We took our
  work seriously, many of us beginning this journey while still in our 20s."*
- **Honours:** NASA Exceptional Space Act Award (**2003**); Presidential Medal of Freedom from
  Barack Obama (**2016**); Computer History Museum Fellow (**2017**).
