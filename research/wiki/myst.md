Myst (Cyan, Inc., September 24, 1993), created by brothers Rand and Robyn Miller and
published by Brøderbund, was modeled and rendered on **Apple Macintosh Quadra** computers
using StrataVision 3D software — not Silicon Graphics workstations, a common and easy
conflation with Donkey Kong Country's genuinely SGI-based pipeline from the same era.

## In this game

Myst is the **Level 13 cabinet** in Act 3 (The Basement), teaching C and the comma. Its
photographic pre-rendered stills are the first pre-rendered-photographic-texture visual
leap in Keystroke Kingdom's fidelity ladder, described in the [[sixteen-bit-era]] concept
page. **This page carries the SGI-vs-Mac correction** — see below.

## The facts

- Created by Rand and Robyn Miller at Cyan, Inc. (Spokane, WA), published by Brøderbund.
  Released September 24, 1993, for CD-ROM.
- **CORRECTION (important, high-priority):** Myst was **not** rendered on Silicon Graphics
  workstations. It was modeled and rendered on **Apple Macintosh Quadra** computers using
  **StrataVision 3D** software (by Strata, Inc.), with additional modeling in Macromedia
  MacroModel, image editing in Photoshop, video work in Adobe Premiere — roughly 2,500
  rendered images and ~40–66 minutes of QuickTime animation, across six Macintosh Quadras,
  over roughly two years of development.
- Silicon Graphics workstations (reportedly 13 SGI Indigo units) were used by Cyan for the
  1997 **sequel**, *Riven* — not for the original 1993 Myst. This is a common and easy
  conflation between the two games' production pipelines. Contrast with [[donkey-kong-country]],
  which genuinely did use SGI hardware in the same era.
