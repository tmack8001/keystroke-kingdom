Brain, written in January 1986 by teenage brothers Basit and Amjad Farooq Alvi in
Lahore, Pakistan to deter software piracy of their own medical program, is credited as
the first virus for IBM PC-compatible computers — deliberately non-destructive, and
unique among early viruses for embedding the authors' real names and phone number.

## The facts

- The Brain boot-sector virus was written in January 1986 (one source dates it
  January 19) by brothers Basit Farooq Alvi (19 at the time) and Amjad Farooq Alvi in
  Lahore, Pakistan.
- The brothers told *Time* magazine they wrote it to protect their own medical
  software from unauthorized copying — intended narrowly as an anti-piracy measure,
  not to cause harm. It spread via floppy disk and deliberately avoided infecting hard
  disks, a design choice that limited its destructiveness.
- It is considered the first virus to target IBM PC-compatible computers, and it
  infected at least 100,000 disks between 1986 and 1989 across multiple countries.
- Brain is distinctive among early viruses for embedding the real names, address, and
  phone number of its creators — intended as contact information for "vaccination" —
  which led to the brothers receiving international phone calls from infected users.
