On **24 August 1995** Microsoft released **Windows 95**, and people queued outside shops
at **midnight** to buy a piece of software — which had never happened before and has
rarely happened since. Microsoft's own account: the launch *"was marked by midnight store
launches and lines of customers worldwide,"* and the product *"sold 7 million copies in
its first five weeks."* It introduced **the Start button and task bar, the recycle bin
and desktop shortcuts** — the desktop shape most computers still wear — and a **startup
sound composed by Brian Eno**. The marketing ran to hundreds of millions of dollars and
licensed a Rolling Stones song.

## The facts

- **The date: 24 August 1995.** NBC News, writing on the twentieth anniversary: *"Exactly
  twenty years ago Monday, Microsoft launched a new version of its desktop operating
  system."* The same article captions a photograph from *"the first store in Australia to
  sell the product at midnight August 24, 1995."*
- **Midnight queues, and Microsoft says so itself.** Microsoft's own launch page: the
  release *"was marked by midnight store launches and lines of customers worldwide."*
  **This is the kid-legible core of the card** — the picture of people sleeping on a
  pavement for a program.
- **What it actually introduced.** Microsoft: *"introduced the Start button and task bar,
  recycle bin and desktop shortcuts."* ⚠️ Note what that sentence does **not** say — it
  does not say Windows 95 invented the graphical desktop (it did not; see
  [[xerox-alto-gui]], 1973) or the mouse ([[engelbart-mouse]], 1964). **A card must say
  "introduced the Start button," never "invented the desktop."**
- **The sales figure.** Microsoft: *"Windows 95 sold 7 million copies in its first five
  weeks, becoming the world's most popular operating system."* ⚠️ **This is the vendor's
  own number and the superlative is the vendor's own too.** Use the 7-million-in-five-
  weeks figure with attribution; **do not repeat "world's most popular operating system"
  as a neutral fact** — no independent source was fetched for it.
- **The startup sound.** Microsoft: *"a startup sound composed by Brian Eno."* Fetched
  and attributable. (The much-told detail that Eno was briefed to make it about
  three and a quarter seconds long was **not** in the fetched page — not asserted.)
- **The marketing.** NBC News: Microsoft *"famously spent hundreds of millions promoting
  the OS,"* licensed a famous Rolling Stones song, and lit the Empire State Building in
  Windows colours. ⚠️ **Microsoft's own page does not mention the Rolling Stones at all**
  — the song connection rests on NBC News here. The commonly-cited *"$300 million"*
  figure and the song title *"Start Me Up"* appeared in **search summaries only** and are
  not asserted on this page.
- **Contemporaneous framing.** A search summary attributes to the *New York Times* the
  line that this was *"the splashiest, most frenzied, most expensive introduction of a
  computer product in the industry's history."* **The NYT article itself was not fetched**
  and the quote is therefore unusable as a citation — recorded only as a lead for a
  future pass.
- **Floppies, not a CD, for many buyers.** Search summaries describe shops selling *"the
  first Windows 95 floppy discs"* at midnight. Not independently confirmed here; a nice
  detail, left out of the card.
