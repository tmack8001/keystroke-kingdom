Ray Tomlinson, a programmer at BBN, sent the first networked email in 1971 by adapting
the local-messaging program SNDMSG to work across ARPANET host computers — and picked
the @ symbol to separate a username from its destination machine, the format every
email address still uses today.

## The facts

- Ray Tomlinson, working at BBN (Bolt, Beranek and Newman) on the ARPANET project,
  adapted an existing local-messaging program called SNDMSG — incorporating code from
  a file-transfer program called CPYNET — so a message could be sent to a user on a
  different, remote computer over the network, in 1971.
- He chose the @ symbol to separate the username from the destination machine's name,
  because it was rarely used in names at the time and clearly conveyed "at" — the
  `user@host` format is still the email standard today.
- Tomlinson later said the content of that very first test email (sent between two
  machines sitting next to each other) was insignificant and not preserved — he
  guessed it was something like "QWERTYUIOP."
