John McCarthy coined the term "artificial intelligence" in his 1955 proposal for what
became the Dartmouth Summer Research Project on Artificial Intelligence, held in the
summer of 1956 — the workshop widely regarded as the field's founding event.

## The facts

- John McCarthy (Dartmouth College) wrote the proposal that coined the phrase
  "artificial intelligence," co-authored with Marvin Minsky (Harvard), Nathaniel
  Rochester (IBM), and Claude Shannon (Bell Telephone Laboratories), for a "2 month,
  10 man study of artificial intelligence."
- The proposal was submitted in 1955; the workshop itself — the Dartmouth Summer
  Research Project on Artificial Intelligence — took place in the summer of 1956, and
  is standardly cited as the founding event of AI as an academic field.
- The proposal's own framing: the study would "proceed on the basis of the conjecture
  that every aspect of learning or any other feature of intelligence can in principle
  be so precisely described that a machine can be made to simulate it."
