Apollo 11 launched from Cape Kennedy on **July 16, 1969**, and on **July 20, 1969** the Lunar
Module *Eagle* landed on the Moon — *"Houston, Tranquility Base here. The Eagle has landed,"*
**4:17 p.m. EDT**. Neil Armstrong stepped onto the surface at **10:56 p.m. EDT** the same evening
(which is **July 21 in UTC**), with Buzz Aldrin following and Michael Collins in orbit above.

## In this game

**The anchor moment of the proposed space act**, and the point where the act joins the game's
existing computing-history card layer instead of sitting beside it. The bank already has one
moon-adjacent card — the Unix card, *"Two programmers at Bell Labs, Ken and Dennis, built a whole
new kind of computer system the same summer astronauts walked on the moon"* ([[unix]]) — and that
seam is currently unexploited. The card this moment wants is not "men walked on the Moon" (every
child hears that); it is **how they got there**: by typing verb–noun commands into the
[[apollo-guidance-computer-dsky]], running software written by [[margaret-hamilton]]'s team, on
trajectories checked by people like [[katherine-johnson]].

**Register rule for this whole act: wonder, not tragedy.** Real spaceflight history contains
genuine disasters. They are outside this game's emotional register and are **not** history-card
material — flagged here so nobody proposes one later.

## The facts

- **Launch:** July 16, 1969, **9:32 a.m. EDT**, from Cape Kennedy.
- **Crew and roles:** **Neil Armstrong** (Commander), **Buzz Aldrin** (Lunar Module Pilot),
  **Michael Collins** (Command Module Pilot, who stayed in lunar orbit and did not land).
- **Landing:** **July 20, 1969, 4:17 p.m. EDT**, on the south-western edge of the **Sea of
  Tranquility**. Armstrong: *"Houston, Tranquility Base here. The Eagle has landed."*
- **First step:** **10:56 p.m. EDT, July 20** — *"That's one small step for a man, one giant leap
  for mankind."* **Watch the date arithmetic:** in UTC the hatch opened 02:39:35 on **21 July
  1969** and Armstrong stepped off at 02:56:15 UTC. **US-clock July 20, world-clock July 21.**
  Both are correct; say which one you mean. (This is the created-vs-shipped error class in a
  different costume.)
- **During the descent**, *"Eagle's computer is sounding alarms"* — NASA describes it as the
  computer *"trying to do too many things at once."* See [[apollo-guidance-computer-dsky]] for why
  that did **not** end the landing, and for the caution against quoting alarm codes or
  fuel-remaining figures on a card.
- An estimated **650 million people** watched the televised broadcast.
