"Hello, world" — the first program most coders ever write — is documented as
originating in Brian Kernighan's 1972 internal Bell Labs tutorial for the "B" language,
and became the now-canonical convention through Kernighan & Ritchie's *The C
Programming Language* (first edition, published February 22, 1978).

## In this game

final card is every programmer's first sentence. Card text: *"The first program every
coder writes makes the computer say hello. You typed it by hand."* This moment also

## The facts

- **1972:** Kernighan's internal Bell Labs tutorial, "A Tutorial Introduction to the
  Language B," contains the earliest documented "hello, world" example.
- **February 22, 1978:** Kernighan & Ritchie's *The C Programming Language*, 1st
  edition, is published, with "hello, world" as the book's first example program
  (Section 1.1) — the appearance that made the convention universal.
- An intermediate 1974 Bell Labs memo also exists between the 1972 origin and 1978
  popularization.
- The in-game card uses 1978 — defensible as "the year hello world became a universal
  convention," though the deeper origin is 1972. Verdict: accurate but folds an origin
  date and a popularization date into a single year, same pattern as the
  [[world-wide-web]] card. No change needed to the card.
