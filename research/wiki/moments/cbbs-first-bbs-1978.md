On February 16, 1978, Ward Christensen and Randy Suess brought CBBS (Computerized
Bulletin Board System) online in Chicago — the world's first public dial-up bulletin
board system. They built it during downtime forced by the Great Blizzard of 1978,
which stranded the two members of their computer-hobbyist club (CACHE, the Chicago
Area Computer Hobbyists' Exchange); Christensen wrote the software while Suess
assembled the hardware.

## The facts

- **February 16, 1978:** CBBS goes online in Chicago, built by Ward Christensen and
  Randy Suess.
- **The blizzard story:** the Great Blizzard of 1978 hit Chicago with record
  snowfall, stranding both men — members of CACHE — indoors; the enforced downtime is
  what gave them the free time to build the system. They had a working version
  within about two weeks but let it be known as taking four, "so it wouldn't seem
  rushed."
- Christensen wrote the software; Suess assembled the S-100 hardware it ran on.
- They documented their creation in a **November 1978 Byte magazine article**,
  "Hobbyist Computerized Bulletin Board."
- **Historical significance:** CBBS is credited as the world's first bulletin board
  system, letting hobbyists exchange messages and information by dialing in with a
  modem, years before public internet access — and inspired the wave of BBSes that
  followed (see [[bbs-door-games]] for the door-game culture that grew out of that
  wave). Chicago's mayor proclaimed February 16, 2003, "BBS Day" for its 25th
  anniversary.
