"Super Scaler" is the umbrella term for Sega's late-1980s sprite-scaling arcade hardware
generation — games that calculated true 3D positions, scale, and zoom rates, then converted
those calculations back into fast 2D sprite rendering to simulate real-time 3D perspective
without actual polygon rendering.

## In this game

[[out-run]] (Sega, 1986, Level 21 in Keystroke Kingdom's parked Act 5/Corner Cabinets plan)
is the definitive Super Scaler game, chosen specifically for its sprite-scaling pseudo-3D
technique. Yu Suzuki's own account: *"All the calculations in the system were 3D, even from
Hang-On. I calculated the position, scale, and zoom rate in 3D and converted it backwards to
2D."* The Super Scaler lineage runs Hang-On (1985) → Out Run (1986) → After Burner, and
predates the genuine polygon-3D hardware of [[early-3d-era]] by roughly a decade.

## The facts

- Out Run (Sega, September 1986): director/designer Yu Suzuki. Custom Sega OutRun arcade
  board (based on Sega System 16). "Super Scaler" sprite-scaling technology, also used a
  year earlier in Hang-On.
- The technique is a pseudo-3D approach — full 3D position/scale calculations converted
  into 2D sprite rendering — distinct from real polygon rendering (contrast with
  [[time-crisis]], which uses genuine real-time 3D polygons a decade later).
- Note: the internal "Sega AM2" division sub-team credit could not be independently
  verified — sources confirm "Sega" + "Yu Suzuki," but not the AM2 label specifically.
