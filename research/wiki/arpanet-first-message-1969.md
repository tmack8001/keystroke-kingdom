On October 29, 1969, at 22:30, Charley Kline attempted to transmit the word "LOGIN"
from a UCLA Sigma 7 to an SRI SDS 940 over the fledgling ARPANET, under Leonard
Kleinrock's supervision — the system crashed after only two letters made it through,
making the internet's first-ever transmitted word "LO."

## The facts

- **October 29, 1969, 22:30:** transmission attempt from UCLA (Sigma 7) to SRI (SDS
  940), Charley Kline typing under Leonard Kleinrock's supervision, message received at
  SRI by Bill Duvall.
- The system crashed after transmitting "L" and "O" — the intended message was "LOGIN."
  Full login succeeded roughly an hour later on a second attempt.
- Kleinrock himself has called "LO" a "prophetic" accidental "hello" — the internet's
  first word, by accident, echoing the very first word humanity would later teach a
  computer to say back (see [[hello-world]]).
- Verdict: accurate, no correction needed — extremely well documented; UCLA's own site,
  written by Kleinrock himself, confirms every detail in the card.
