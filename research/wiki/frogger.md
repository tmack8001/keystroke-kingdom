Frogger (Konami, 1981), often miscredited to Sega alone, is the lane-crossing survival game
that arcade-museum.com's own trivia calls out for its true origin: "Although Frogger is
credited to Sega it was originally developed by Konami," with Sega/Gremlin handling North
American publishing.

## In this game

Frogger is not an assigned Keystroke Kingdom cabinet but represents the same
early-golden-age discrete/tile hardware period as [[centipede]] and [[defender]].

## The facts

- Developer: Konami (Japan); published in North America by Sega/Gremlin. Released August
  1981 (Japan), September 1981 (North America).
- Visual style: brightly colored top-down view divided into lanes — gray road with
  vehicles, green median, blue river with logs/turtles/alligators, frog homes at top.
- Input: four-way joystick; each tap moves the frog exactly one space (discrete, hop-based).
- Iconic: called "the arcade game with the most ways to die" (a real 1982 Softline magazine
  quote). Japanese opening theme is the children's song "Inu no Omawarisan"; the American
  version plays "Yankee Doodle" upon successfully homing a frog.
- Technical: Z80 processor architecture. Score maximum 99,990 (rolls over). DIP-switch
  configurable starting lives. 30-second timer per frog.
