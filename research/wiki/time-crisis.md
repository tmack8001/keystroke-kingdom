Time Crisis (Namco, December 1995) is the on-rails light-gun shooter that introduced the
foot-pedal cover mechanic — a genuine industry first — arriving about a year after Sega's
Virtua Cop (September 1994) had already established fully polygonal light-gun shooting.

## In this game

Time Crisis is not an assigned Keystroke Kingdom cabinet, but represents the transition into
full real-time 3D polygon rendering that Act 4 ([[early-3d-era]]) is built around.

## The facts

- Developer: Namco. Released December 1995 (Japan arcade).
- Visual style: full 3D polygon environments (castle interiors, courtyards, corridors)
  rendered in real time, hand-colored polygons simulating real-time lighting.
- Input: light gun (with recoil/blowback feedback) + foot pedal — press to emerge from
  cover and shoot, release to duck behind cover and reload. The gun hardware traces to
  Namco's Point Blank (1994).
- **Correction (polygon-first framing):** "one of the first fully polygonal arcade
  light-gun games" is overclaimed — Sega's Virtua Cop (September 1994) established that
  ~15 months earlier. Time Crisis's genuine, defensible first is the pedal-driven cover
  mechanic, which "changed light-gun game design forever."
- Technical: Namco Super System 22 arcade board. The PlayStation port required reduced
  polygons, halved frame rate, delayed enemy spawns, and eliminated off-screen rendering to
  compensate — evidence of how far ahead of home hardware the arcade original was.
