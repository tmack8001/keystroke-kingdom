Hedy Lamarr, a Hollywood movie star, co-patented a frequency-hopping "Secret
Communication System" in 1942 — an idea later cited as inspiration for the
spread-spectrum techniques underlying modern Wi-Fi and Bluetooth.

## The facts

- **1942:** Hedy Lamarr (filed as Hedy Kiesler Markey) and composer George Antheil are
  granted US Patent 2,292,387, "Secret Communication System," describing frequency
  hopping — originally intended to make radio-guided torpedoes jam-resistant.
- IEEE's own outreach materials explicitly tie her work to "modern wireless
  communication technologies, including Wi-Fi and Bluetooth" — framed as *inspiration*
  for the 802.11 standard rather than a direct technical lineage.
- The connection is indirect: her specific patent expired unused in 1959, and modern
  Wi-Fi uses different spread-spectrum schemes. But that's the same level of
  simplification IEEE itself uses for public audiences — verdict: accurate as an
  institutional-style simplification, no change needed.
