iRacing (launched **August 26 2008**) is the terminal point of the simulation branch of racing
games: a subscription, server-hosted online service built on **LIDAR-scanned** cars and tracks by
**Dave Kaemmer** — co-founder of Papyrus, and the designer of
[[indianapolis-500-the-simulation]] nineteen years earlier — with official partnerships across
real motorsport.

## In this game

The modern half of Act 7's terminal station (`research/racing-games-by-era.md` §11; concept page:
[[sim-racing]]). It completes the single studio lineage that makes the "sim" half of the act tell
itself: **1989 [[indianapolis-500-the-simulation]] → 1994 [[nascar-racing-1994]] → 1998
[[grand-prix-legends]] → 2008 iRacing.** Its **incident-points and licence-demotion** system is a
textbook One Rule violation — a permanent, punitive record of your mistakes. The reframe
recommended in the hub doc is to **invert** it: a clean-laps counter that only ever goes up, never
down.

## The facts

- Developer/publisher: **iRacing Studios**. **Dave Kaemmer** (co-founder of Papyrus Design Group)
  partnered with **John W. Henry** to form FIRST.net LLC in **2004**, which became iRacing.
  Public launch **August 26 2008**.
- Technology: **LIDAR-scanned cars and tracks**, aiming to "mimic real-life driving as closely as
  possible."
- Model: subscription-based, with **all sessions hosted on the publisher's servers**; a base
  content pack (24 cars, 32 tracks) expandable by individual purchase.
- **Licensing and safety systems:** a no-blame safety rating that assigns **incident points** for
  off-track excursions, losses of control and collisions; players progress through **Rookie, D,
  C, B and A** licence classes, with a **Pro** licence reserved for premier esports competitors.
- Professional standing: official partnerships with **NASCAR, IMSA, World of Outlaws, Porsche and
  the FIA**, including the eNASCAR Coca-Cola iRacing Series and driver-development programmes
  involving real racing professionals.
