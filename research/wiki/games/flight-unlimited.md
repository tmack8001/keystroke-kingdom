Flight Unlimited (**Looking Glass Technologies**, **June 7, 1995**, MS-DOS) is the aerobatic sim
that did two hard things at once: its terrain was built by **stereophotogrammetry** from real
aerial photographs, and its flight model was **real-time computational fluid dynamics** — *"air
acts as a fluid that automatically reacts to the shape of any object placed within it"* — rather
than a wind-tunnel lookup table. Conceived and led by **Seamus Blackley**, a particle physicist
and amateur pilot. **It contains no combat, deliberately.**

## In this game

**The strongest single argument that a kid-safe flight act is not a compromise.** Flight Unlimited
was *"the first real serious challenge to Microsoft's dominance"*, sold **780,000+ copies**, and
did it with **zero combat** — and its structure is a **flight instructor teaching you manoeuvres**,
which is Keystroke Kingdom's own shape. A simulated instructor talks you through rudder turns up
to Immelmann turns and tailslides, in real time, while you fly.

That is the flight act's pedagogical template: **a voice that teaches, a thing that is hard, and
nothing at all that punishes.**

## The facts

- **Developer/publisher:** LookingGlass Technologies, self-published. **June 7, 1995** for MS-DOS;
  1996 for Windows 95 and Macintosh.
- **Seamus Blackley** was project leader and chief physics programmer — a particle physicist and
  amateur pilot who conceived the aerobatics-focused game in **1992**. *(Yes: the same Seamus
  Blackley later associated with the original Xbox. That connection was **not** verified in this
  pass — do not assert it in game text without its own sourcing.)*
- **The flight model:** rather than wind-tunnel databases or simple force vectors, **real-time
  computational fluid dynamics**, enabling aerobatic manoeuvres that competitors could not do.
- **The terrain:** **stereophotogrammetry**, combining aerial photographs of France and the United
  States into digital terrain reproductions of roughly **11 square miles each** — photorealistic
  scenery, three years before consumer 3D cards were common.
- **Aircraft:** five, including the Pitts S-2B, Bellanca Decathlon, Sukhoi Su-31, Extra 300S and
  the **Grob G103 sailplane** — i.e. it ships with a **glider**, which matters for an act looking
  for non-powered, non-combat flying.
- **Teaching:** lessons covering *"basic and advanced flight techniques, ranging from rudder turns
  to challenging aerobatic maneuvers,"* with a **simulated instructor giving real-time guidance**
  through moves like Immelmann turns and tailslides.
- **Combat:** none. A deliberate design choice.
- **Commercial/critical:** 780,000+ copies by 2002; praised for realism and physics, criticised for
  demanding system requirements; considered *"the first real serious challenge to Microsoft's
  dominance."*
- **Punishment mechanics:** you can crash; there is no campaign, no lives and no mission to fail.
  Among the gentlest structures in the genre.
