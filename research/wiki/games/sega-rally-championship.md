Sega Rally Championship (Sega AM3, February 1995, Model 2) is the first racing game in which the
**road surface changes how the car behaves** — asphalt, gravel and mud modelled with different
friction properties — a milestone every rally and off-road racer since is downstream of.

## In this game

The surface-physics step in the recommended **Act 7 — Speed / Racing** spine
(`research/racing-games-by-era.md` §6). For a theme author the useful part is not the physics but
its *visual* consequence: different dust, spray and traction behaviour per surface. Note that
[[monaco-gp]] (1979) had already shifted its road to ice and gravel as a difficulty escalation
sixteen years earlier — Sega Rally is where that idea became a *handling model* rather than a
hazard.

## The facts

- Developer: **Sega AM3**. Arcade release **February 1995** (Japan); 1995 elsewhere. Hardware:
  **Sega Model 2**. Saturn port 1995 (by AM3), PC 1997.
- **Signature innovation:** driving on different surfaces with varying friction, with the car's
  handling changing accordingly — described as "the first racing game to incorporate this
  feature" and "one of the milestones in the evolution of the racing game genre."
- Structure: a World Championship of three stages where the player's finishing position at the
  end of one stage becomes the starting position of the next; finishing first on all three
  unlocks an optional fourth "Lakeside" stage. Also an early rally game with cooperative play
  alongside competitive multiplayer.
- **Correction to circulating claims:** frequently dated **1994** and credited to **AM5**;
  Wikipedia's record gives **Sega AM3, February 1995 (Japan)**.
- **Punishment mechanics:** per-stage time limits, with position carried forward between stages
  — cumulative pressure rather than a single clock.
