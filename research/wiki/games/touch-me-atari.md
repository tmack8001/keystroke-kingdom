*Touch Me* (Atari, **1974**, arcade) is a short arcade cabinet with four large circular buttons
that blinks a sequence of lights and asks the player to repeat it back, adding one step each
round. It is the ancestor of the entire **call-and-response** branch of the rhythm genre:
Ralph H. Baer built [[simon-electronic-game]] directly from it, judging Atari's execution
"terrible" and making the feedback musical instead. It is also the only rhythm-genre ancestor
that is a **real arcade cabinet from the discrete-logic decade** — which makes it the one
candidate that could open a music act inside Keystroke Kingdom's 1979 arcade fiction.

## In this game

Proposed as the **opening station of a music/dance act** ([[rhythm-games-by-era]] §2) for the
same reason [[racing-games-by-era]] opens on Monaco GP rather than Excitebike: it is era-true to
the arcade the game is set in, and its rendering bar ([[discrete-logic-era]]) is one Act 1
already draws. Atari already owns Level 1 (Breakout) and Level 23 (Missile Command), but *Touch
Me* is a **clearly distinct game**, which the one-game-per-cabinet rule permits (the
Pac-Man / Ms. Pac-Man precedent) — flag for Trevor's eye, not a collision. See [[atari-inc]].

**The mechanic translates to a keyboard with zero distortion** — four buttons are four keys,
nothing scrolls, and there is no timing window to widen or forgive ([[call-and-response]],
[[rhythm-on-a-keyboard]]).

**Punishment to strip (One Rule):** **three mistakes and the game ends**, plus a response time
limit. Delete both: the sequence simply waits, which is exactly what a letter already does in
this game's sacred wrong-keypress contract.

## The facts

- **Developer / publisher:** Atari, Inc. **Arcade release 1974**; a **handheld version followed
  in 1978**, released in response to [[simon-electronic-game]]'s success.
- **Cabinet:** "a short arcade cabinet" with **four large circular buttons**.
- **Play:** the machine shows "a sequence of blinking electric lights"; the player repeats the
  sequence in the same order. Each successful round adds another button. A **digital score
  display** counts completed sequences. **Three mistakes end the game.**
- **Lineage:** Baer saw *Touch Me* and adapted its formula into Simon with **coloured buttons
  and musical tones**. ⚠️ **Date conflict, disclosed:** the *Touch Me* article dates this to
  **1977**; the *Simon* article dates Baer's encounter to **1976**. Both were fetched
  2026-09-13 and they disagree — write "the mid-1970s," not a year.
