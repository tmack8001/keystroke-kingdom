Street Fighter II: The World Warrior (Capcom, March 1991) launched the 1990s fighting-game
boom on Capcom's CP System (CPS-1) hardware, and its beloved combo system emerged as an
unintended discovery during development, not a planned feature.

## In this game

Street Fighter II is the **Level 12 cabinet** in Keystroke Kingdom's Act 3 (The Basement),
teaching B and N. Its enormous, detailed sprites were the visual shock the game's era
demands after Act 2's NES-class fidelity — a technical and narrative correction from an
earlier draft that placed it at Level 4, alongside [[pac-man]] on the home row, which was
wrong: Street Fighter II belongs in the 90s Basement. See `.claude/rules/levels.md` Level
12.

## The facts

- Developer: Capcom. Released March 1991.
- Visual style: large, detailed, beautifully animated 2D sprites at 384x224 resolution.
  Internationally themed stage backgrounds; each of 8 characters with unique design
  language.
- Input: 8-way joystick + six buttons (three punch, three kick — light/medium/heavy). The
  six-button layout became the fighting-game standard.
- **Correction (combo system):** "unintended bug" softened to "unintended discovery," per
  series director Akira Nishitani's own account — he specifically resists calling it a bug,
  describing it as "a chance side effect of some game settings we had adjusted" that the
  team deliberately kept and expanded once found.
- Technical: Capcom CP System (CPS-1) — the 14th game on the platform. Variable memory
  allocation per character (commonly cited as Ryu 8 megabits / Zangief 12 megabits, traced
  to a 2002 Edge magazine retrospective). An independent primary-document analysis of
  Capcom's original sprite-memory sheets gives different relative figures in different
  units (Zangief 19 "sheets," Ryu 13.5) — similar ratio, different units; treat the megabit
  figures as "as reported by Edge," not an uncontested primary fact. First 16-megabit SNES
  cartridge for the home port.
- Music by Yoko Shimomura.
