*Band Hero* (Neversoft / Activision, **3 November 2009**) is the pop-facing spin-off of
[[guitar-hero]] — the same engine and the same plastic instruments, with a **65-song Top 40
pop-rock setlist** in place of Guitar Hero 5's harder-rock 85. Trevor named it directly in the
act brief; it is included here for completeness and because it is the closest the
plastic-instrument boom came to a deliberately family-facing product.

## In this game

Nothing new mechanically — it **inherits Guitar Hero 5's Rock Meter, scoring multipliers and
failure**, so its entire punishment ledger is [[guitar-hero]]'s. Its only distinct value to
[[rhythm-games-by-era]] is as evidence about **audience**: the boom's publishers understood
there was a younger/broader audience and reached for it by changing the *setlist*, not the
*mechanics*. The mechanics stayed punishing. That is the pattern this act would have to break.

⚠️ **Attribute the audience claim carefully.** The "aimed at teenage girls" framing is a
**reviewer observation**, based on the Taylor Swift inclusion, the colour scheme and the song
selection — **Activision made no such statement on the record.** This is the popularizer-vs-
inventor error class in miniature: say "critics read it as aimed at…", never "Activision
targeted…".

**ESRB rating not checked this pass** — see [[guitar-hero]].

## The facts

- **Developer:** Neversoft (PS2 port by Budcat Creations; DS and Wii by Vicarious Visions).
  **Publisher:** Activision.
- **Release:** **3 November 2009** (North America), 6 November (Europe), 25 November
  (Australia). PS2, PS3, Xbox 360, Wii, Nintendo DS.
- **Content:** 65 songs of pop and mainstream rock, against Guitar Hero 5's 85 harder-rock
  tracks.
- **Instruments:** lead guitar, bass, drums and vocals in full band play, with drop-in/drop-out.
- **Audience:** multiple reviewers identified it as aimed at teenage girls, citing Taylor
  Swift's inclusion, the colour schemes and the song selection. No publisher statement to that
  effect appears in the source.
