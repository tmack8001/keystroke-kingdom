Formula One Grand Prix (Geoff Crammond / MicroProse, 1991, Amiga, Atari ST and PC) is the first
authentic simulation of the Formula One World Championship — the real cars and all sixteen
circuits of the 1991 calendar, with tyre wear and damage modelled — and the direct ancestor of
every licensed F1 game since.

## The facts

- Designer **Geoff Crammond**; publisher **MicroProse**. Released **1991** for Amiga, Atari ST
  and PC. (Some sources date the Amiga release 1992; treat the month, and the 1991-vs-1992
  split, as unsettled.)
- Subject: **the first authentic simulation of the Formula One world championship**, featuring
  the cars and all **16 international Grand Prix circuits of the 1991 calendar**, playable as a
  quick race, a single race, or a full championship.
- Simulation features: modelled **tyre wear and damage**; manual or automatic gear selection; a
  **driving-line display**; configurable steering, brake and throttle aids — a feature set that
  "set the stage for a lot of features we expect in modern titles."
- Sequels: Grand Prix 2, Grand Prix 3 and Grand Prix 4. A modern Steam re-release, *Geoff
  Crammond Racing*, has been announced.
