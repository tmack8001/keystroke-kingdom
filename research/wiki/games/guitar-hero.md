*Guitar Hero* (**Harmonix** developed, **RedOctane** published, **8 November 2005**,
PlayStation 2) took [[guitarfreaks]]'s idea to a Western console audience with a controller
carrying **five coloured fret buttons, a strum bar and a whammy bar**. Notes scroll down a
fretboard; the player holds the matching fret and toggles the strum bar as each arrives. The
franchise reached **25 million units and $2 billion at retail** and then collapsed: Activision
**disbanded the Guitar Hero business unit in February 2011**.

## In this game

The title most likely to be proposed for a music act, and the one with the **most to strip**.

- **The input does not translate.** Five plastic frets plus a strum bar is not a keyboard.
  It reaches a keyboard only by **emulation**, which is exactly what [[frets-on-fire]] did in
  2006 (F1–F5 as frets, Enter to pick) — a real precedent, but one where the keyboard is
  *pretending to be a guitar neck* rather than being itself ([[rhythm-on-a-keyboard]]).
- **Its audio punishes you.** A missed note produces an ugly sound; [[rock-band]] escalated this
  to **muting your instrument out of the mix**. Compare [[taiko-no-tatsujin]], which refuses to
  do either.
- **Its licensing history is the good news** — see [[music-licensing-in-games]] and
  [[rhythm-games-by-era]] §10. Guitar Hero launched with **30 cover versions** re-recorded by
  WaveGroup Sound and only 17 masters. **Original/re-recorded music is not this project's
  compromise; it is the path the genre's biggest hit took at launch.**

**Punishment to strip (One Rule):** the **Rock Meter** — *"if the meter drops too low the song
will prematurely end in failure"* — plus a **score multiplier that rises with consecutive
correct notes** and Star Power that doubles it. Note the multiplier especially: it makes the
*next* mistake cost more than the last one, a compounding penalty aimed at exactly the player
this game is built for. Multipliers and streak meters are the same class of thing **INV-82**
removed from Act 7.

⚠️ **Two precision notes.** (1) *"Booed off stage"* is the game's folklore, not a sourced
phrase; the documented wording is that the song "will prematurely end in failure" — do not print
the booing line as a quotation. (2) **ESRB ratings were not checked this pass.** Guitar Hero
ships licensed rock lyrics; per [[racing-games-by-era]] §16b's standing rule, any ESRB citation
must state the platform list on the record. A ratings pass is a prerequisite before this becomes
child-facing content.

**What to draw:** a PlayStation 2, a television, and the plastic guitar on the floor
([[console-hardware-silhouettes]]). Not a coin-op.

## The facts

- **Developer:** Harmonix. **Publisher:** RedOctane. **Release: 8 November 2005** (North
  America), PlayStation 2.
- **Controller:** "five different-colored fret buttons" plus a strum bar and a whammy bar; the
  whammy boosts sustains and Star Power.
- **Play:** notes scroll down a fretboard display; the player must "hold down the fret button
  corresponding to the note shown and toggle the strum bar" as it arrives.
- **Rock Meter:** tracks success and failure; if it drops too low the song ends prematurely in
  failure.
- **Scoring:** points for correct notes, chords and sustains; consecutive correct notes raise a
  score multiplier; Star Power doubles it.
- **Soundtrack:** **30 cover songs recreated by WaveGroup Sound** plus **17 master recordings**
  from indie groups — 47 tracks spanning the 1960s to 2005.
- **Series arc:** **Guitar Hero III** became "the first single video game to sell more than
  US$1 billion at retail" (3.5 million copies in seven months). The series ran to **twenty-five
  releases** (2005–2015) and **25 million units / $2 billion**. By 2009 rhythm-game sales fell
  ~50% short of projections; in **February 2011** Activision disbanded the Guitar Hero unit,
  citing "continued declines in the music genre." **Guitar Hero Live** (2015) introduced a
  **six-button, two-row controller** meant to "mimic actual guitar fingering," undersold, and
  led to layoffs.
- **Provenance footnote:** Harmonix's own current games page does **not** list Guitar Hero — its
  footer notes the trademark belongs to Activision Publishing, Inc.
