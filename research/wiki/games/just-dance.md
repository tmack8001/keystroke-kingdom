*Just Dance* (Ubisoft Paris / Ubisoft, **17 November 2009**, Wii) is the rhythm game that
outlived the genre it launched into: no dedicated peripheral (just a Wii Remote), no fail state
in normal play, and — as a franchise — **more than 90 million units by November 2024**. Its
design contains the exact architecture `product.md` mandates: **failure exists only inside an
opt-in mode.**

## In this game

This is the most important commercial validation in [[rhythm-games-by-era]] for the way
Keystroke Kingdom is already built.

- **The Adventure/Survival split, shipped in 2009.** Players "can always complete songs in
  standard play modes." Elimination lives in a separate, chosen mode — **"Last One Standing,"**
  which gives seven lives and eliminates you when they run out. That is precisely
  `product.md`'s *"Survival Mode is opt-in only — never the default."* Adopt wholesale.
- **The market voted for it.** The plastic-instrument franchises collapsed in 2009–2011
  (see [[guitar-hero]]); the peripheral-free, fail-free, family-facing one passed 90 million.
  Only [[taiko-no-tatsujin]] — also gentle, also no ugly failure — came through with it.
- **Its input does not translate.** Whole-body motion with a Wii Remote is not a keyboard
  gesture. Take the *policy*, not the *play*.

**Punishment to strip (One Rule):** almost nothing in standard play. The judgement words —
**X (move not copied) / OK / Great** — are still grading, and grading is the class of thing
**INV-82** removed from Act 7, so the words would need rethinking. The seven-lives elimination
belongs in an opt-in mode exactly where Ubisoft put it.

**What to draw:** a Wii, a television, and a Wii Remote — no peripheral, which is the point.

## The facts

- **Developer:** Ubisoft Paris. **Publisher:** Ubisoft. **Release: 17 November 2009**, Nintendo
  Wii.
- **Play:** hold a Wii Remote in the right hand and mirror an on-screen dancer's choreography;
  motion detection, **no dance pad or instrument peripheral**.
- **Judgement:** **X** (move not copied), **OK** (attempted but imperfect), **Great**
  (accurately matched). Score targets of 10,000 (Standard) and 15,000 (Master). Later entries
  add "gold moves" for bonus points.
- **Failure:** **none in standard play** — "players can always complete songs in standard play
  modes." The **"Last One Standing"** mode gives seven lives and eliminates players who lose
  them.
- **Reception:** critics called the gameplay "simplistic" and "accessible to a casual audience,"
  with some saying it lacked "depth or even basic game concepts."
- **Sales:** **4.3 million copies of the first game by October 2010**; the franchise **exceeded
  90 million units by November 2024**.
- **Why it survived:** the series article credits the Wii's reach into non-gamers plus a growing
  fitness culture, and positions the franchise across "education, entertainment, and exercise"
  rather than against core gaming, with support for up to six players and "popularity among a
  wide age range and dance skill level."
