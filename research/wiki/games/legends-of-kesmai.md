Legends of Kesmai (Kesmai Corporation, launched on America Online in late 1996, with
some sources citing a 1997 full/commercial release) is the 2D-graphical successor to
[[island-of-kesmai]] — replacing its ASCII/pseudo-graphics with sprite-based visuals
while keeping the same persistent island world — and is the specific point in the
Kesmai lineage where a **documented, named player-guild system** appears (SKY, TREE,
ROSE, among others). It is the strongest candidate found this session for the "joined
a clan and adventured together" cue specifically, at the cost of no longer being ASCII.

## In this game

**Best match for the clan-system cue; loses the ASCII cue.** See

Both games share the same underlying world and lineage claim (a documented forerunner
of EverQuest/WoW-style MMORPGs), so if the memory blurs "ASCII-ish" and "had guilds,"
Legends of Kesmai sitting directly downstream of Island of Kesmai (same creators, same
island, same company) is a very plausible single-memory blend of both games' features
— common for a childhood memory of something played once, years apart, on evolving
software.

## The facts

- **Launch:** Legends of Kesmai (LOK) launched on America Online in 1996 (some
  sources, including MobyGames' formal release-date field, cite 1997 — likely
  reflecting a late-1996 rollout vs. broader/full commercial availability; treat as
  "late 1996 into 1997," not a single confirmed date). Also offered free to CompuServe
  users and via GameStorm, a now-defunct dial-up gaming service.
- **Visual upgrade from Island of Kesmai:** 2D sprite graphics replaced Island of
  Kesmai's roguelike pseudo-graphics/ASCII display — a real graphical MMORPG, not
  text-mode, distinguishing it from its parent game on exactly the ASCII cue.
- **Character classes:** wizards, thaumaturges, fighters, knights, martial artists,
  and thieves — players could group together to explore the island and surrounding
  areas.
- **Guild system, confirmed and named:** players could create and join guilds.
  Documented named examples include **SKY** (capped at 12 members), **TREE** (drawn
  largely from Wizards who had played British Legends, another CompuServe game), and
  **ROSE** (a thieves-only guild) — real, sourced, structured social groups a player
  joined and adventured within, matching the brief's specific "clans/alliances/
  factions you JOINED and adventured alongside" cue precisely.
- **Company fate:** Electronic Arts acquired Kesmai in 1999; both Island of Kesmai and
  Legends of Kesmai were retired commercially in 2000.
- **Considered an MMORPG ancestor:** described by multiple sources as "among the first
  successful graphical multi-player online role-playing games" and, alongside its
  parent title, cited as influential on the EverQuest/WoW-era MMORPG questing design.
