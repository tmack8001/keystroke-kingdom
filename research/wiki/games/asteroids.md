Asteroids (Atari, November 1979), designed by Lyle Rains (concept) and Ed Logg (design and
program), is the vector-graphics shooter that rendered its geometric asteroids and
triangular ship as pure glowing wireframe lines rather than pixels.

## In this game

Asteroids is not an assigned Keystroke Kingdom cabinet but exemplifies the vector-display
branch of early arcade hardware alongside [[tempest]], contrasting with the raster/bitmap
approach of [[space-invaders]]. Both belong to the [[discrete-logic-era]] / early sprite
hardware period covered by Act 1.

## The facts

- Developer: Atari — concept by Lyle Rains, design and programming by Ed Logg. Released
  November 1979.
- Visual style: vector graphics — bright white/green lines on black, no pixels, no sprites,
  no filled shapes. Screen wraps in both axes.
- Input: five buttons — rotate left, rotate right, thrust, fire, hyperspace (random
  teleport). No joystick.
- Technical: MOS 6502 CPU. Atari QuadraScan / Digital Vector Generator (DVG) draws
  geometric lines rather than raster pixels — effectively infinite resolution. Maximum 26
  asteroids on-screen simultaneously (large asteroids at the cap split into one medium
  piece instead of two to enforce it). 13 hand-wired sound effects — no dedicated sound
  chip.
- The vector hardware was adapted from Atari's earlier [[lunar-lander-1979]] — and the first 300
  Asteroids cabinets shipped in **Lunar Lander cases**.
- The hardware credit for the vector generator/sound board is historically murky: older
  sources credit "Howard Delman," while arcade-museum.com's current people page credits
  "Wendi Allen" (her current name) — use "Wendi Allen" if a hardware credit is ever added.
