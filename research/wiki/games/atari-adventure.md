Adventure is the Atari 2600 game in which programmer [[warren-robinett]] hid his name
inside a secret room in June 1979, submitted to Atari management that same month, ahead
of the game's March 1980 commercial release — widely cited as the first video game
easter egg.

## In this game

`adventure` typed on the title screen triggers `WARREN ROBINETT WAS HERE. 1979.` — see
[[warren-robinett]] for the person and the full note on why "1979" (hidden) rather than
"1980" (released) is the correct year for the egg text. See [[hacker-culture]] and
[[easter-egg-culture]].

## The facts

- **June 1979:** the hidden-room source code is submitted to Atari management.
- **March 1980:** commercial release.
- The hidden room was Robinett's direct response to Atari's policy of stripping
  developer credit from its games, to prevent competitors from poaching programmers and
  to deny developers negotiating leverage.
- Widely cited (Forbes among others) as the reference point that later inspired the
  "Easter egg hunt" framing device of the novel/film *Ready Player One*.
