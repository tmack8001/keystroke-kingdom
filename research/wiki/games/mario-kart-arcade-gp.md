**Mario Kart arcade cabinets exist, and they always have been real coin-ops.** *Mario Kart
Arcade GP* is a Namco-developed, Nintendo-published arcade sub-series that began in **2005** on
the **Triforce** arcade board and is **still manufactured and sold today** as *Mario Kart Arcade
GP DX* — a seated, steering-wheel, 42-inch, four-cabinet-linkable machine listed on Bandai Namco
Amusement America's own commercial-products site. It is a *different line of games* from
[[mario-kart|Super Mario Kart]] (the 1992 SNES title), not an arcade port of it — which is the
distinction a previous research pass collapsed into the flatly misleading sentence *"There is no
Super Mario Kart arcade cabinet. Do not draw one."*

## In this game

This page exists because of a correction. See [[mario-kart]]'s Corrections section (2026-09-13)
for the full story; the short version is that a narrow, defensible claim about the **1992 SNES
title** was written as an absolute claim about **the franchise**, with a "do not draw one"
instruction attached — which would have steered an Act 7 cabinet-art author away from a real,
photographable, currently-in-production arcade machine that a child has very plausibly already
played at a water park.

The governing principle, written up in full on [[home-games-in-cabinets]], is: **draw a real
thing.** For Act 7's Super Mario Kart station the honest objects are *either* the SNES and
television that the 1992 game actually ran on (Trevor's preferred direction — see
[[console-hardware-silhouettes]]), *or* a real Mario Kart arcade cabinet from this line. What is
ruled out is the third option: inventing a 1992 coin-op that never existed and implying it did.

## The facts

**The line, in order.**

| Title | Year | Developer / publisher | Hardware |
|---|---|---|---|
| *Mario Kart Arcade GP* | **2005** | Namco, published by Nintendo | **Triforce** arcade board |
| *Mario Kart Arcade GP 2* | **March 2007** | Namco / Nintendo | Triforce |
| *Mario Kart Arcade GP DX* | **late 2013** | Bandai Namco / Nintendo | not stated by the sources fetched — **[unverified]** |
| *Mario Kart Arcade GP VR* | **14 July 2017** | Bandai Namco | HTC Vive hybrid cabinet |

- **It is a genuine third-party collaboration.** *Mario Kart Arcade GP* is **the first Mario Kart
  game developed by a third-party company** and the first with non-Nintendo characters: eight
  Mario-franchise racers (Mario, Luigi, Peach, Wario, Bowser, Toad, Yoshi, Donkey Kong) plus
  **Pac-Man, Ms. Pac-Man and Blinky**. Later entries added Waluigi, Mametchi and Don-Chan.
- **It runs a console engine on console-derived arcade hardware.** The Triforce board is an
  arcade platform in the GameCube family, and the Super Mario Wiki records the game as using
  **"a modified version of the *Mario Kart: Double Dash!!* game engine."** So the relationship
  to the home games is not metaphorical — it is literally the console game's engine, in a
  cabinet. *(The Triforce board's Nintendo/Sega/Namco joint origin is widely repeated but **was
  not independently verified this pass** — the web-search budget ran out. Treat "Triforce, an
  arcade board in the GameCube family" as the safe claim and **[unverified]** for the
  three-company detail.)*
- **Cabinet form:** a dedicated sit-down machine with **a steering wheel, gas pedal and brake
  pedal** — not a joystick. The original is a two-player dedicated cabinet, and **up to four
  cabinets can be linked** for four-player racing.
- **NamCam / NAM-CAM:** a camera in the cabinet **photographs the player's face** and puts it on
  their kart on screen (framed, in the original, with Mario's hat and moustache). The DX version
  adds "Funny Face Frames" that stretch and squeeze the photo. This is a genuinely
  arcade-only feature — it cannot exist on a home console of the era, which is exactly why the
  arcade version is not a redundant reskin.
- **Content:** the 2005 original ran **24 courses across six cups** (Mario, DK, Wario, PAC-MAN,
  Bowser, Rainbow); the current DX has five selectable cups over 40 rounds, 20+ karts and 90+
  item types, per the manufacturer's own listing.
- **DX is a current commercial product, not a memory.** Bandai Namco Amusement America lists it
  with a **42-inch HD monitor**, rainbow LED lighting, an integrated seat, dimensions of roughly
  **41″ W × 63″ D × 94″ H**, and linking for up to four cabinets. That is why a modern arcade,
  a Dave & Buster's, or a resort arcade can and does have one on the floor.

**Date precision — the sources disagree, so do not state a month.** Wikipedia gives *October
2005*; Nintendo Life gives **Japan 12 August 2005 / US & EU 1 October 2005**; the Super Mario
Wiki gives **North America October 2005 / Japan November 2005**. Three sources, three orderings.
**Say "2005."** (This is the created-vs-shipped precision trap in its regional form.)

## Corrections this page makes to the wiki (2026-09-13)

1. **"There is no Super Mario Kart arcade cabinet. SNES only. Do not draw one."** ([[mario-kart]],
   Corrections item 3, written 2026-09-13.) The narrow claim — that the **1992 SNES game** was
   never itself released as a coin-op — is defensible and no source found this pass contradicts
   it. But the sentence was written as an absolute about the franchise, on a page that **never
   mentioned the Namco arcade line at all**, and the graph had no page for it. A reader following
   that instruction would have concluded, wrongly, that a Mario Kart arcade machine is a thing
   that does not exist. It exists; it has existed since 2005; **you can buy one new today.**
2. **`research/racing-games-by-era.md` §16c item 6** carries the same error in the same breath as
   a correct one: *"[[mario-kart]] (SNES only — no arcade Super Mario Kart exists)."* Corrected
   in §17 of that document, added this pass.
