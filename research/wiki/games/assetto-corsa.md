Assetto Corsa (Kunos Simulazioni, December 19 2014) is the modern benchmark for what "simulation"
means technically in a racing game — laser-scanned circuits, a tyre model that simulates flat
spots, heat cycles, graining and blistering, real-time active aerodynamics driven by telemetry
channels — developed at an R&D office **inside** the Vallelunga racing circuit in cooperation with
real drivers and teams.

## In this game

The clearest single worked example for [[sim-racing]], the concept page that defines Act 7's
terminal point (`research/racing-games-by-era.md` §11). Its value here is that the technical
claims come from the **developer's own product description**, not a reviewer's summary — which is
what lets the research draw a hard line between simulation and arcade racing
([[daytona-usa]], [[ridge-racer]], [[need-for-speed]]) rather than an impressionistic one.

## The facts

*(All of the following is Kunos Simulazioni's own published product description.)*

- Release **December 19 2014**, PC. Developer **Kunos Simulazioni**.
- **Tyre model:** "tyre flat spots, heat cycles including graining and blistering."
- **Aerodynamics:** "very advanced aerodynamic simulation with active movable aerodynamics parts
  controlled in real time by telemetry input channels," plus "hybrid systems with KERS and energy
  recovery simulation."
- **Track geometry:** circuits "developed using Laser scan technology, in order to guarantee the
  highest level of accuracy possible." Journalism describes the method as driving a laser-firing
  survey truck along each circuit to map every corner, bump and groove to millimetre accuracy.
- **Telemetry:** "advanced setup options and telemetry for data analysis," plus in-game telemetry
  apps.
- **Environment:** an adjustable time-of-day mode that calculates sun position in real time from
  each track's geographical coordinates.
- **Development context:** built at the Kunos Simulazioni R&D office located just inside the
  international racing circuit of **Vallelunga**, "with the cooperation of real world racing
  drivers and racing teams."
