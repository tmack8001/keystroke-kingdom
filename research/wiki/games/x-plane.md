X-Plane (**Laminar Research**, created by **Austin Meyer**, first released **1995**) is the flight
simulator that computes rather than looks up: its flight model uses **blade element theory** —
breaking a wing or propeller into small elements and solving the forces on each of them in real
time — instead of replaying pre-computed wind-tunnel tables. The **professional version of
X-Plane 12 is FAA-certified**, and can be used to log FAA-approved flight-training hours on
tested, certified hardware. Windows, macOS and Linux; mobile versions since 2009.

## In this game

**The flight act's "this stopped being a game" station** — the aviation counterpart to what
[[iracing]] and [[assetto-corsa]] are for the racing act ([[sim-racing]]). It is the clean answer
to a child's question *"is it real?"*: **real enough that real pilots count the hours.**

It is also the act's best excuse to show a **physics** visualisation rather than a prettier
picture — see the "visualise as force arrows / data channels" note on
[[flight-sim-rendering-eras]].

## The facts

- **Developer:** Laminar Research; **creator:** Austin Meyer. **First released 1995.** Its
  predecessor was Meyer's **"Archer-II IFR"** (a Piper Archer II simulator, created **1993**).
- **Blade element theory:** the simulator's distinguishing feature — an aerodynamic method that
  analyses a surface (classically a propeller blade) by **breaking it into smaller components and
  determining the forces on each**, computed live. *PC Gamer* called version 5.43 the *"current
  standard-bearer for flight model realism in a commercial simulation."*
- **FAA certification:** the **professional version of X-Plane 12 is certified by the FAA**;
  Laminar distributes FAA-certified versions for professional use, and FAA-certified flight
  training hours can be logged — but **only** when the computer meets minimum frame-rate
  requirements and the hardware and all associated simulation hardware are themselves tested and
  FAA-certified. **Be precise: "the professional version, on certified hardware" — not
  "X-Plane is FAA-approved."**
- **Platforms:** Windows, macOS, Linux; iOS, Android and webOS versions since 2009. Current
  version at time of writing: **X-Plane 12.4.1, released March 26, 2026.**
- *Macworld* judged its features comparable to FAA-approved simulators.
- **Punishment mechanics:** none structurally — it is a sandbox simulator, not a scored game.
