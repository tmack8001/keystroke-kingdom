Monaco GP (Sega, November 1979) is a scrolling overhead road racer that is doubly perfect for
Keystroke Kingdom: it is from **1979**, the year the game's arcade is set in, and it is **one of
the last Sega arcade games built from TTL discrete logic instead of a CPU** — over 100 chips
across two circuit boards, no microprocessor at all.

## In this game

**The recommended opening station for Act 7 — Speed / Racing**
(`research/racing-games-by-era.md` §0, §1, §15). It solves two problems at once: the act gets a
generationally honest start a decade before [[excitebike]], and the theme author gets to work at
the same [[discrete-logic-era]] fidelity bar Act 1 already uses for [[breakout]] and
[[space-invaders]] — no new rendering vocabulary required. Its escalating hazards (the road
narrows; the surface turns to ice and gravel) are an early ancestor of
[[sega-rally-championship]]'s surface friction, and its overhead view contrasts cleanly with
[[night-driver]]'s first-person pylons and [[pole-position]]'s rear-view road.

## The facts

- Developer/publisher: **Sega** (Sega/Gremlin for worldwide distribution). Release
  **November 1979** in Japan and the United States; January 1980 worldwide. An upgraded
  **Pro Monaco GP** followed in March 1980 (Japan) / July 1980 (worldwide); an SG-1000 home port
  arrived December 1983.
- Technical: **one of the last arcade games made by Sega to use transistor–transistor logic (TTL)
  discrete logic circuits instead of a microprocessor-based CPU** — over 100 chips across two
  boards, with images stored in ROM and sound generated by analog circuitry.
- Rendering/gameplay: overhead perspective, scrolling road, five areas. Difficulty escalates as
  AI rival cars get faster, **the road narrows**, and **the surface changes to ice and gravel**.
- Cabinets: three styles, including a deluxe cockpit "designed in approximation to sitting behind
  the wheel of a race car" with a padded steering wheel and accelerator pedal, plus tabletop and
  upright variants.
- Commercial: third highest-grossing arcade game in Japan in 1979 and fifth in 1980; **the most
  popular arcade driving game in the US in 1981**, with a record run on the arcade charts from
  1980 to 1987.
- **Punishment mechanics:** race against a clock across the five areas; collisions destroy the
  car.
