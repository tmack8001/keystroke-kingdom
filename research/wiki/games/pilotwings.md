Pilotwings (**Nintendo EAD**, Super Famicom **December 21, 1990**; SNES North America **August 23,
1991** as a launch title; Europe 1992) is an *amateur* flight simulator built as a **Mode 7**
showcase: you earn pilot licences through lessons in **light plane, hang glider, skydiving and
rocket belt**, each with its own controls. Shigeru Miyamoto produced. **ESRB E** (Mild Violence).

## In this game

**The recommended 16-bit station for the flight act** — the same Mode 7 rendering step the racing
act gets from [[f-zero]], but aviation, civilian, and structurally a *curriculum*: a graded lesson
tree, which is what Keystroke Kingdom already is. It is the flight equivalent of the design idea
`research/racing-games-by-era.md` §7 flags in [[gran-turismo]]'s licence tests — **take the
curriculum, delete the grade.**

Note the cabinet-rule judgement call: [[pilotwings-64]] is the same *franchise*. The rule permits
"clearly distinct games in the same franchise" (the Pac-Man / Ms. Pac-Man precedent), and a Mode 7
SNES game and an N64 3D game are clearly distinct — but using both is the only franchise doubling
in the flight arc, so it wants Trevor's eye.

## The facts

- **Developer/publisher:** Nintendo EAD / Nintendo, with **Shigeru Miyamoto** leading production.
- **Dates:** Japan **December 21, 1990**; North America **August 23, 1991** (an **SNES launch
  title**); Europe 1992.
- **Technology:** an extensive **Mode 7** showcase — the SNES's ability to rotate and scale a
  single raster plane, *"which mimics 3D graphics by rotating and scaling flat objects."* No
  polygons. Reviewers' praise was led by the graphical presentation.
- **Content:** lessons in **light plane flight, hang gliding, skydiving and the rocket belt**,
  each with its own controls and mechanics; bonus stages involve an **attack helicopter** (the
  likely source of the ESRB's *Mild Violence* descriptor — worth knowing before anyone calls the
  game combat-free).
- **Structure:** the player *"attempts to earn pilot licenses through lessons"* — a graded
  progression. **Punishment mechanic:** score thresholds gate progress; a failed lesson is
  repeated.
- **Rating:** **ESRB E for Everyone, Mild Violence.**
