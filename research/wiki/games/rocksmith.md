*Rocksmith* (Ubisoft San Francisco / Ubisoft, **18 October 2011**, Xbox 360 and PS3; Windows
2012) is the rhythm game you plug a **real electric guitar** into, via the **Real Tone Cable**.
It is the only entry in this project's rhythm research whose **punishment column is empty**:
songs cannot be failed. Instead the game splits each song into phrases and serves a **denser or
sparser note pattern** depending on how the last phrase went.

## In this game

The single most valuable design transplant in [[rhythm-games-by-era]], and unlike
[[gran-turismo]]'s licence tests (the equivalent find in [[racing-games-by-era]]) it needs
**nothing deleted at all**.

- **It is `tech.md` §Adaptive Pacing, shipped commercially in 2011.** *"Track average time
  between correct keypresses… player slows down → game slows down with them."* Rocksmith does
  the same thing on the content axis: it adapts **density**, never **deadline**. That
  distinction is the whole design lesson — a rhythm game can adapt to a learner without ever
  moving a finish line, because it never had one.
- **It proves failure is separable from teaching.** Ubisoft's own store copy: the game *"adapts
  to your skill level and offers comprehensive practice tools and options, ensuring you progress
  quickly, no matter where you start."* Nothing about surviving.
- **Its input does not translate** — a real six-string guitar is not a keyboard. Take the
  pedagogy, not the instrument.
- **Honest caveat on the teaching claim.** Reviews were **mixed** on whether it actually teaches
  guitar: one critic called it "an amazing learning tool for the guitar," others argued it
  "fails as a way to learn guitar" because of input lag and how the difficulty adjustment works.
  **No formal study of learning outcomes is cited.** Do not present Rocksmith as proven
  pedagogy — present it as proven *architecture*.

**What to draw:** a console, a television, and **a real guitar with a cable running to the
console** — never a plastic one, which is the joke of the product.

## The facts

- **Developer:** Ubisoft San Francisco (with Ubisoft Osaka on later editions). **Publisher:**
  Ubisoft. **Release: 18 October 2011** (North America), Xbox 360 and PS3; Windows October 2012.
- **Input:** the **Real Tone Cable**, a USB adapter into any electric guitar's standard output
  jack. The launch bundle included an Epiphone Les Paul Junior for players without one. The
  cable is **sold separately** from the current Steam edition.
- **Dynamic difficulty:** "Each song is split into a number of phases, and depending on how
  accurate the player is during a phase, a subsequent phase may provide more difficult note
  patterns." **Players cannot fail songs** — the system adjusts difficulty instead of ending
  play.
- **Guitarcade:** technique-focused practice mini-games (hammer-ons, power chords) dressed as
  arcade games — including a Galaga-inspired shooter called "Ducks."
- **Educational reception:** mixed, as above; no formal learning-outcome study cited.
