Out Run (Sega, September 1986), directed and designed by Yu Suzuki, is the definitive
"Super Scaler" road racer — a pseudo-3D driving game built by converting full 3D position,
scale, and zoom calculations back into 2D sprite rendering.

## In this game

Out Run is the **Level 21 cabinet** in Keystroke Kingdom's parked Act 5/Corner Cabinets
plan — chosen for its sprite-scaling pseudo-3D, radio-station soundtrack choice, and
arcade-luxury fantasy, recreated as faithful pixel art (sunset gradient, palms, red
convertible). See `research/video-game-history.md` "Cabinet assignments — history notes for
the newer picks," and the [[super-scaler]] concept page for the underlying technology.

## The facts

- Developer: Sega, director/designer Yu Suzuki. Released September 1986 (Japan), November
  1986 (North America).
- Visual style: pseudo-3D racing, camera near ground level; bright varied European scenery
  based on Suzuki's own scouting trip through Monaco, the French Riviera, and Italy; red
  Ferrari Testarossa convertible.
- Input: steering wheel + stick shift (high/low gear) + accelerator/brake pedals; deluxe
  sit-down cabinets feature hydraulic motion simulation.
- Iconic: three selectable radio soundtracks ("Passing Breeze," "Splash Wave," "Magical
  Sound Shower" by Hiroshi Kawaguchi) — an in-game radio-selection first, later adopted by
  Grand Theft Auto. Branching route paths to five possible endings. The synthwave music
  genre adopted "outrun" as an alternate genre name from this game's aesthetic.
- Technical: custom Sega OutRun arcade board (based on Sega System 16). "Super Scaler"
  sprite-scaling technology (also used a year earlier in Hang-On) — Suzuki: *"All the
  calculations in the system were 3D, even from Hang-On. I calculated the position, scale,
  and zoom rate in 3D and converted it backwards to 2D."*
- Note: the internal "Sega AM2" division credit could not be independently verified in
  sources fetched this pass — all confirm "Sega" + "Yu Suzuki," but the AM2 sub-team label
  is widely repeated in enthusiast sources without a directly confirmed primary citation.
