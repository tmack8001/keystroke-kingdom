Double Dragon (Technos Japan, April 1987) defined the two-player cooperative belt-scrolling
beat 'em up formula that became the genre standard — though it did not originate the format,
which Technos's own Nekketsu Kōha Kunio-kun / Renegade (1986) introduced a year earlier.

## In this game

Double Dragon is not an assigned Keystroke Kingdom cabinet, but represents the late-80s
beat-'em-up genre alongside [[contra]] on the path toward Act 3's 16-bit era
([[street-fighter-ii]]).

## The facts

- Developer: Technos Japan (licensed by Taito for US/EU distribution). Released April 1987.
- Visual style: post-apocalyptic urban "belt scroll" side-scrolling. 384 colors displayed
  simultaneously from a 4,096 (12-bit) color palette. Four stages: city slum, factory,
  forest, gang hideout.
- Input: joystick + three buttons (punch, kick, jump); combinations produce throws, elbow
  strikes, headbutts, hair grabs; weapons can be knocked from enemies and picked up.
- **Correction (genre origin):** "the belt-scrolling beat 'em up genre essentially started
  here" is an overclaim — Technos's own Nekketsu Kōha Kunio-kun / Renegade (1986)
  introduced the belt-scroll format a year earlier. Double Dragon's real, defensible claim
  is defining the two-player co-op belt-scroll formula that became the genre standard.
- Iconic: two-player co-op with a betrayal twist at the arcade ending (the NES port cut
  co-op and made brother Jimmy an ordinary final boss). Mad Max / Fist of the North Star
  inspired post-apocalyptic aesthetic. Soundtrack by Kazunaka Yamane.
- Technical: three 8-bit processors running in parallel (two Motorola 6809s plus one
  Hitachi HD6309). Yamaha YM2151 FM synthesis sound chip.
