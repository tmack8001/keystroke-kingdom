Gauntlet (Atari Games, October 1985) is the four-player cooperative dungeon crawler famous
for its TMS5220C speech-synthesizer narration — "Warrior needs food, badly!" — described by
Wikipedia as "the most elaborate hardware design in Atari's history to date."

## In this game

Gauntlet is not an assigned Keystroke Kingdom cabinet but represents the mid-80s expansion
of arcade hardware complexity alongside [[bubble-bobble]] and [[dragons-lair]].

## The facts

- Developer: Atari Games. Released October 1985.
- Visual style: top-down orthographic dungeon crawler — four distinct character sprites
  (Thor, Thyra, Merlin, Questor), hordes of small enemy sprites, dark dungeon palettes with
  bright character/item colors.
- Input: 8-way joystick + two buttons (fire and magic) per player; four-player simultaneous
  play on a wider-than-standard control panel.
- Iconic: TMS5220C speech synthesis chip (Atari's first coin-op with a voice synthesizer),
  voiced by Ernie Fosselius (an outside filmmaker, not an Atari programmer), speech encoded
  by Earl Vickers. Health drains continuously; inserting coins adds health.
- Technical: Atari Gauntlet arcade system board — called "the most elaborate hardware
  design in Atari's history to date" (Wikipedia-primary superlative, not independently
  corroborated this pass). 7,848 units produced (also Wikipedia-primary).
- A previously-circulated claim that speech encoder Earl Vickers is "the same person who
  programmed [[tron]]'s music" could not be confirmed by any source linking the two credits
  — removed as unverified. Both individual credits (Vickers on Gauntlet speech, Vickers on
  Tron music) stand independently.
