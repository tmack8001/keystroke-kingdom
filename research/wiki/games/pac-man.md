Pac-Man (Namco, 1980), designed by Toru Iwatani, is the maze-chase game deliberately
designed to be "cute, colorful and appealing" to attract a broader audience beyond the
space-shooter crowd — and one of the most recognizable characters in video game history.

## In this game

Pac-Man is the **Level 3 cabinet**, teaching S and L, and marks the first level where real
words appear in Keystroke Kingdom's word bank (ads, lads, falls, flask, lass). Its four
colors on black (blue maze, yellow Pac-Man, four distinct ghost colors) define Act 1's third
visual step — "the first recognizable face in the game." See `.claude/rules/levels.md`
Level 3. The Galaxian flagship appears as [[galaxian]]'s cameo bonus fruit in this game.

## The facts

- Developer: Namco, designed by Toru Iwatani, with a nine-person development team. Released
  May 1980 (Japan test), July 1980 (Japan release), October 1980 (USA).
- Visual style: bright pastel colors on black. Blue maze walls, yellow Pac-Man, four
  distinctly colored ghosts (red Blinky, pink Pinky, cyan Inky, orange Clyde) with
  expressive blue eyes.
- Input: four-way joystick (no diagonals).
- Technical: Z80A CPU at 3.072 MHz. 16 KB ROM, 3 KB SRAM, 8 KB character ROM. Sprites are
  16x16 pixels, 4 bits per pixel. 6 usable sprites (0 and 7 reserved for screen timing). Two
  custom ICs for sync bus control and video RAM addressing.
- Iconic: the kill screen at Level 256 (integer overflow); Power Pellets reversing the chase
  dynamic; intermission cutscenes between levels.

## Citation spot-check (2026-07-12, mechanics-fidelity audit follow-up)

The ghost-colors claim ("four distinctly colored ghosts: red Blinky, pink Pinky, cyan
Inky, orange Clyde") was checked against its sole existing citation, pacman.com's history
page — that page names all four ghosts (Japanese and English) but does **not** state
their colors in English text, making the citation thinner than it appeared. Added
reference 3 below (Wikipedia's dedicated "Ghosts (Pac-Man)" page) to carry the color
claim directly, and confirmed it also documents that all four ghosts were originally
going to be a single color (red) until Iwatani's team won a 40–0 internal vote for
multicolor — a fact not currently in this page's "facts" list but worth folding in on a
future pass. No correction needed to the claim itself; it was accurate, just under-cited.
