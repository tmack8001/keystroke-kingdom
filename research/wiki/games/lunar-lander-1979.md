Lunar Lander (**Atari, August 1979**) was **Atari's first vector-graphics arcade game**: a
line-drawn lunar surface, a line-drawn lander, a **big physical throttle lever** instead of a
joystick, and a descent you win by running out of neither fuel nor nerve. Its lineage is a
computing-history story in miniature — a **1969 text game written in FOCAL on a PDP-8 by a high
school student**, to a **1973 graphical version on a DEC GT40 terminal**, to an arcade cabinet
seen by an Atari engineer **on a tour of NASA's Ames Research Center**.

## In this game

**The single best-fitting station in this entire research pass.** It is **1979** — the exact year
Keystroke Kingdom's arcade is set in. It is the **Moon**. It is **vector graphics**, a rendering
technique the game does not yet own anywhere. It is **not** an assigned cabinet. And its history
runs straight through the game's existing computing-history spine: minicomputers, FOCAL and BASIC,
DEC terminals, and a NASA research centre.

It is also the natural **hinge between the flight arc and the space arc** — the moment the
altitude axis leaves the atmosphere. See `research/flight-simulators-by-era.md` §8.

## The facts

- **Release:** **August 1979**, Atari, arcade. **Atari's first vector graphics game** and *"the
  first multiple-perspective video game"* — the view **zooms in** as the lander nears the surface.
- **Credits:** developed by **Wendi Allen (credited on release as Howard Delman)** and
  **Rich Moore**, starting in autumn 1978. *(Attribution note: cite the developer by her name,
  Wendi Allen, and note the original screen credit — do not silently use only the 1979 credit.)*
- **The control** is the memorable part: Allen specified a **large throttle handle** rather than a
  joystick, wanting *"more physicality"* and **continuously adjustable thrust** instead of
  on/off firing.
- **The lineage, in order** (this is the part usually garbled):
  1. **1969** — *Lunar Landing Game*, a **text** game written in **FOCAL** for the **DEC PDP-8**
     by **Jim Storer**, then a high school student. Converted to BASIC by **David H. Ahl**, who
     printed three versions in his 1973 book *101 BASIC Computer Games*.
  2. **1973** — **DEC commissioned** a real-time graphical version, **Moonlander**, written by
     **Jack Burness** to show off the new **DEC GT40 graphics terminal**; distributed with DEC
     computers and shown at trade shows.
  3. **1979** — Allen had seen Burness's Moonlander **while touring NASA's Ames Research Center**
     in the mid-1970s; when Atari needed a game for its first vector display, she and Moore
     refined the concept into the arcade cabinet.
- **Gameplay/punishment:** manage a finite fuel supply to land softly on marked flat ground; harder
  landing sites carry **score multipliers**. **Inserting a coin bought more fuel** — so the game
  could be continued indefinitely, which is an unusually forgiving arcade contract for 1979 and is
  much closer to Keystroke Kingdom's "mercy quarters" than to a lives system.
- **Commercial:** **4,830 cabinets**, a moderate success eclipsed by **Asteroids** that November —
  and the **first 300 Asteroids cabinets shipped in Lunar Lander cases.**
