Galaxian (Namco, 1979) introduced the first multi-colored arcade sprites — individual pixels
within a single sprite could display different colors — and pioneering tilemap hardware that
directly influenced Nintendo's Donkey Kong board and, ultimately, the Famicom/NES
architecture.

## In this game

Galaxian is not itself an assigned Keystroke Kingdom cabinet, but its tilemap hardware is
the technical ancestor of [[donkey-kong]]'s board, and its color-sprite breakthrough
foreshadows the richer palettes of Act 1's later levels ([[pac-man]], [[galaga]]). The
Galaxian flagship famously appears as the 6th bonus fruit in [[pac-man]].

## The facts

- Developer: Namco. Released September 1979 (Japan, per court-record-sourced Wikipedia
  date), February 1980 (North America).
- **Correction — not "first true color":** Galaxian is often called the first true-color
  arcade game, but color itself predates it (Atari's Color Gotcha, 1973, and Indy 800,
  1975). Galaxian's genuine, defensible first is **multi-colored sprites** — per-pixel
  color within a single sprite.
- Visual style: scrolling starfield background (a first for arcade games), colorful alien
  formation (red, blue, purple flagships with escorts), white player Galaxip.
- Technical: pioneering tilemap hardware — 8x8 pixel tiles, animated multi-color sprites
  over a scrolling background. Reduced processing/memory requirements by up to 64x compared
  to Space Invaders' bitmap approach. This hardware directly influenced Nintendo's Radar
  Scope/Donkey Kong board and, ultimately, Famicom/NES architecture.
- Date discrepancy disclosed: Wikipedia (court-record-sourced) gives Japan September 15,
  1979; arcade-history.com gives October 31, 1979 (returned a 403 on fetch, so noted but not
  cited as read). The stronger-sourced September date is used here.
