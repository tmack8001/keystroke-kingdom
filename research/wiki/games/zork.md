Zork, developed 1977–1979 at MIT by Tim Anderson, Marc Blank, Bruce Daniels, and Dave
Lebling for the PDP-10, is the single-player interactive-fiction game that expanded on
[[colossal-cave-adventure]]'s two-word parser into full sentence understanding — the
canonical example of Archetype B's *single-player* branch, as distinct from
[[mud]]'s inherently multiplayer branch.

## In this game

**Archetype B comparison point.** Zork/Colossal Cave Adventure and MUD1 share the
exact same surface presentation (room description text, typed compass directions,
verb-object commands, no picture) — which is why they get conflated in memory. The
dividing line is multiplayer: Zork is one player alone with the machine; MUD1 (see
[[mud]]) was multiplayer from its first networked version in 1980. If the specific
1990s memory this research is grounding involves *other people being there*, that
points to the [[mud]] line, not this one — Zork itself is not a dialup-multiplayer
memory. Both lines share the founding ancestor: Colossal Cave Adventure (already
documented in [[colossal-cave-adventure]] and referenced by the game's `xyzzy` easter
egg).

## The facts

- **1977–1979:** developed at MIT, inspired directly by Colossal Cave Adventure
  (1976), with the explicit goal of a richer, more capable parser — accepting
  compound commands like "open the large door, then go west" rather than Adventure's
  strict two-word `VERB NOUN` grammar.
- **1979:** the MIT team founds Infocom.
- **1980–onward, commercial release:** Infocom splits the expanded game into three
  commercial titles — Zork I: The Great Underground Empire, Zork II: The Wizard of
  Frobozz, Zork III: The Dungeon Master — ported across personal computers via the
  in-house "Z-machine" virtual machine and Zork Implementation Language (ZIL), so one
  game could run on many different microcomputers without a full rewrite per
  platform.
- Single-player throughout; no networked or multiplayer version was part of the
  original commercial line.
- **The Loud Room, a "type the one right word" puzzle** *(confirmed 2026-07-17)*:
  one room can only be solved by typing the single word `echo` — real, documented
  precedent for a puzzle where the correct answer IS a specific typed word, cited
  as grounding for a possible Act 6 riddle/NPC mechanic — see
  [[ascii-overhead-world-conventions]] Q5. [Zork — Wikipedia]. **[fetched]**
