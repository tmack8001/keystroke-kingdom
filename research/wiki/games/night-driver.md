Night Driver (Atari, October 1976, led by Dave Shepperd) is one of the first published video
games with real-time first-person graphics — and it faked almost all of them: the road is scaled
white rectangles receding into black, and the player's car hood is **painted on a plastic overlay
taped over the monitor** rather than drawn by the game at all.

## In this game

A candidate early station for **Act 7 — Speed / Racing**
(`research/racing-games-by-era.md` §1), and a genuinely cheap-to-recreate rendering technique:
rows of shrinking rectangles plus a static foreground sprite is about twenty lines of canvas.
Its honesty about faking depth rhymes with [[super-scaler]] a decade later and with the
overlay-and-cellophane tricks already documented for [[breakout]] and [[space-invaders]].

## The facts

- Developer: **Atari, Inc.**, with development led by **Dave Shepperd**. Arcade production began
  **October 1976**. Two cabinet styles (upright — with internal blacklight — and sit-down).
- Rendering: black-and-white monitor. The road edges are drawn as **scaled rectangles
  representing "pylons"**; the car itself is *not rendered* — it is a painted plastic overlay on
  the screen showing the vehicle's hood.
- Controls: a single accelerator pedal, a steering wheel, and a four-position gear lever.
- **Precision point — a common overclaim:** Night Driver is "commonly believed to be one of the
  first published video games to feature real-time first-person graphics," **not** flatly the
  first. It was inspired by an earlier German coin-op, **Nürburgring 1**, released earlier in
  1976 — Shepperd reportedly worked from reference material showing only "a few little white
  squares."
- **Punishment mechanics:** timed sessions; leaving the road crashes the car. Scoring rolled
  over at 1000 points.
