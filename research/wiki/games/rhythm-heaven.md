*Rhythm Heaven* (Japanese *Rhythm Tengoku*) is Nintendo's rhythm series — **five entries, 2006 to
2026**, produced and composed by **Tsunku**, developed by **Nintendo SPD** (later EPD) with
**TNX**. It is listed in [[rhythm-games-by-era]] §8 as the modern heir of the
[[call-and-response]] lineage, and **this page's job is to say plainly that it is not one.** The
series' own description is that *"the gameplay focuses on audio cues rather than visual cues"* —
i.e. play **in time with a beat you hear**, not **echo a phrase back at your own pace**. Its
genuinely call-and-response parts (Drum Lessons, pre-stage rehearsals) are the teaching wrapper
around a metric game, not the game. It earns its place here anyway, for a different reason: it is
the series that documents, across four generations, **exactly how a rhythm game hands a stuck
player a way out** — and the widely-repeated version of that mercy rule turns out to be wrong
about when it arrived.

## In this game

**Keep it as a cousin, not a descendant** ([[call-and-response]] Correction 4). If a Keystroke
Kingdom music cabinet cites a lineage, the untimed end of it is [[touch-me-atari]] (1974) and
[[simon-electronic-game]] (1978); Rhythm Heaven sits with [[parappa-the-rapper]] and
[[space-channel-5]] on the *metric* side, where the answer belongs to a bar of music.

**What IS worth taking — and it is the only thing on this page a designer should act on:**

1. **The rehearsal.** *"Most levels allow the player to practice before attempting to clear the
   game"* (the 2008 DS entry), and the 2007 Sega arcade version added *"practice sessions"* for
   games that had no tutorial on Game Boy Advance. A demonstration with **nothing riding on it**,
   offered before every stage, is a One-Rule-native idea that a commercial rhythm series treated as
   standard. Compare `.claude/rules/levels/act1.md`'s intro drills, which already work this way.
2. **The escape hatch — but built the way *Megamix* built it, not the way the series article
   summarises it.** *Rhythm Heaven Megamix* (2015/16) ships the three things a stuck child needs
   and the earlier entries did not: you can **"skip games after a set amount of fails"** using
   in-game coins, there is **"an input timing gauge to show the players' timing"**, and the game
   will show **"when to press buttons when a player is stuck."** That is a hint ladder, a
   visualiser and a bypass — the shape of `product.md`'s hint settings, arrived at independently.
3. **What must NOT be taken:** the **Try Again / OK / Superb** rank on every stage, and the gate
   attached to it. *Rhythm Tengoku* (2006): *"players must achieve an OK rank in order to proceed
   to the next game."* *Rhythm Heaven Fever* (2011): *"an 'OK' or 'Superb' rank is required to
   clear the level and progress onto the next."* That is a **grade that gates progress** —
   [[rhythm-games-by-era]] §8's forbidden category, and the exact class of thing **INV-82** took
   out of Act 7.

**What to draw** (per [[racing-games-by-era]] §17's *"draw a real thing"* rule, applied by
[[console-hardware-silhouettes]]): a **Game Boy Advance**, a **Nintendo DS**, or a **Wii and a
television** — never an arcade cabinet, *except* that a genuine Sega coin-op of *Rhythm Tengoku*
exists (**20 September 2007**), so an arcade cabinet is defensible if it is drawn as that one.

## The facts

### The series

- **Producer/composer: Tsunku** (with Masami Yone as co-composer); **Kazuyoshi Osawa** is credited
  as the creator/programmer; developed by **Nintendo SPD (2006–2015)**, **Nintendo EPD (2026)**,
  with **TNX**.
- **The five entries:**

| Entry | Date (JP) | Platform |
|---|---|---|
| *Rhythm Tengoku* | **3 August 2006** | Game Boy Advance (+ Sega arcade, 20 Sept 2007) |
| *Rhythm Heaven* | **31 July 2008** (NA 5 Apr 2009) | Nintendo DS |
| *Rhythm Heaven Fever* | **21 July 2011** (NA 13 Feb 2012) | Wii |
| *Rhythm Heaven Megamix* | **11 June 2015** (NA 15 Jun 2016) | Nintendo 3DS |
| *Rhythm Heaven Groove* | **2 July 2026** | Nintendo Switch |

- **Sales:** *Rhythm Heaven* (DS) **3,040,000** copies worldwide (as of Dec 2014) and the
  sixth best-selling game in Japan in 2008; *Megamix* **1.03 million**; *Groove* **0.87 million**
  (Japanese sales only).
- **The defining mechanic:** *"The gameplay focuses on audio cues rather than visual cues to convey
  information to players"* (series); *"the game primarily relies on audio cues to indicate the
  rhythm"* (*Rhythm Tengoku*). Modes are split into **Rhythm Games**, **Endless Games** and
  **Rhythm Toys**.
- **Drum Lessons** (2006) let the player drive a drum kit from the GBA's buttons, and were
  *"directly derived"* from the original tech-demo concept. The DS entry has **unlockable guitar
  lessons**, earned with Superb ratings.

### The ranking system, entry by entry — because the summary and the entries disagree

| Entry | Ranks | Is a passing rank required to advance? | Documented escape hatch |
|---|---|---|---|
| *Rhythm Tengoku* (2006) | Try Again / OK / Superb | **Yes** — *"players must achieve an OK rank in order to proceed to the next game"* | **None mentioned** |
| *Rhythm Heaven* (2008) | Try Again → Superb | **Yes** — at least *"Just OK"* or *"OK"* to progress | Practice before most levels (not Remixes / some sequels) |
| *Rhythm Heaven Fever* (2011) | Try Again / OK / Superb | **Yes** — *"an 'OK' or 'Superb' rank is required to clear the level"* | **None mentioned** |
| *Rhythm Heaven Megamix* (2015) | 0–100 score + Try Again / Just OK / Superb | A **passing score** on "trial gates"; no statement that Try Again blocks story-mode advance | **Yes** — *"skip games after a set amount of fails"* (coins), plus an input-timing gauge and a show-me-when-to-press assist |
| *Rhythm Heaven Groove* (2026) | not documented this pass | — | — |

### ⚠️ The skip-after-three-Try-Agains rule — resolved further than the graph had it

The **series** article states: *"If a stage is too difficult, the game allows the player to skip
levels when players get a 'Try Again' rank three times in a row, **in most stages**."*
[[call-and-response]] Correction 5 recorded this as **disputed and [unverified]**, because
*Rhythm Tengoku*'s article says an OK rank is *required* to proceed. **Four entry-level articles
fetched this session let the dispute be narrowed rather than left open:**

- **The 2006, 2008 and 2011 entries' own articles all state a passing rank is required, and none
  of the three mentions a skip.**
- **The 2015 entry's article documents a skip — and it is coin-purchased after "a set amount of
  fails," not a flat "three Try Agains in a row."**

**Best reading of the evidence: the mercy mechanic is a LATE-series feature, and the series-level
sentence back-projects it onto entries whose own articles contradict it.** The prior lane's
hypothesis (*"plausibly a per-entry difference — the skip may have arrived in a later entry"*) is
**supported**. It is still **[unverified]** which entry first shipped it and on what exact trigger,
because no Nintendo-published source could be reached (§Unsourceable) — but *"Rhythm Heaven has
always let you skip after three failures"* should **not** be written anywhere as a fact, and
**certainly not as precedent for a design decision here**.

## Corrections from citation pass *(2026-09-13)*

1. **Narrows [[call-and-response]] Correction 5 and [[rhythm-games-by-era]] §8 point 3.** The hub
   lists *"Rhythm Heaven's skip-after-three-Try-Agains"* among the genre's escape hatches.
   **The skip is documented only at series level and only with the hedge "in most stages," while
   the 2006, 2008 and 2011 entries' own articles each state a passing rank is required to advance
   and mention no skip at all.** The one entry that documents a bypass — *Megamix* (2015) — does it
   with **coins after a set number of fails**, not three consecutive Try Agains. *(Hub and
   sibling page not edited — not this lane's files.)*
2. **Confirms [[call-and-response]] Correction 4.** *"The gameplay focuses on audio cues rather
   than visual cues"* (series) and *"the game primarily relies on audio cues"* (*Tengoku*) are both
   verbatim and both re-fetched this session. Rhythm Heaven is a **weak** fifth link in the
   call-and-response chain; its call-and-response lives in Drum Lessons and rehearsals.
3. **The series is longer than the graph assumed.** *Rhythm Heaven Groove* shipped on **Switch, 2
   July 2026** (Nintendo EPD) — a fifth entry that postdates every other page in this rhythm
   cluster. Anyone writing "the series ran 2006–2015" is now wrong.
4. **Credit precision.** Tsunku is **producer and composer**, not "creator" — the series article
   credits **Kazuyoshi Osawa** (programmer) as creator and **Yoshio Sakamoto** as *Tengoku*'s
   producer. The collapse of all three into "Tsunku's game" is the common error.
