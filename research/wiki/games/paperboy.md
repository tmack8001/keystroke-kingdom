Paperboy (**Atari Games**, arcade **April 1985**; John Salwitz and Dave Ralston) is the bicycle
game — and it has the best control panel in this whole survey: an actual **bicycle handlebar**
(a modified Star Wars yoke) with a button on each side to throw papers, **pushed forward to
accelerate and pulled back to brake**. Atari System 2 hardware, a 10 MHz DEC T-11 CPU.

## In this game

**The recommended human-powered station for Act 7.** The racing act's two-wheeler slots are already
[[excitebike]] (motocross) and [[hang-on]] (motorcycle); Paperboy adds the class *neither* covers —
**a bicycle a child could actually own** — at a joyfully ordinary scale: a suburban street, a
Sunday morning, a job. After a decade of race cars, "deliver the papers" is a lovely tonal breath,
and the isometric street is a distinct look from every rear-view road in the act.

Its one caution: the game's comedy is largely **property damage** (windows, gnomes), which wants a
light touch under the house rules — but nothing in it is violent or scary, and nobody is ever hurt.

## The facts

- **Developer/publisher:** **Atari Games**; developed by **John Salwitz** (programming) and
  **Dave Ralston** (art), with hardware by **Doug Snyder**.
- **Release:** **April 1985** — and here is a precision trap worth recording: **the title screen
  says 1984**, but the cabinet released in April 1985. Cite 1985 for the release and note the
  screen date rather than "correcting" one against the other.
- **Controls:** a **bicycle handlebar** — a modified **Star Wars yoke** — with a throw button on
  each side; **push forward to accelerate, pull back to brake**.
- **Hardware:** **Atari System 2**; a **10 MHz DEC T-11** main CPU with a 2.2 MHz **MOS 6502** for
  sound and coin inputs. *(The T-11 is a DEC PDP-11-architecture chip in an arcade cabinet — a nice
  minicomputer-heritage detail for a game that already loves those.)*
- **Perspective:** oblique/isometric scrolling street — visually distinct from the rear-view road
  that dominates the racing canon.
- **Punishment mechanics:** you have a route and a finite number of papers; missing subscribers
  loses you customers, and crashing into obstacles ends a life. Wednesday-to-Sunday weeks with a
  training course between them.
