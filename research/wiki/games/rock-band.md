*Rock Band* (Harmonix / MTV Games / Electronic Arts, **20 November 2007**, Xbox 360 and PS3)
turned the plastic-instrument game into an **ensemble**: a Fender-Stratocaster-shaped guitar, a
four-pad drum kit with a kick pedal, and a USB microphone, played together. **51 of its 58 songs
were master recordings.** Its most interesting mechanic for this project is also its cruellest:
a failing player is **muted out of the mix**, and a bandmate can rescue them — **but only
twice.**

## In this game

Cited in [[rhythm-games-by-era]] §7 (the ledger) and §8 (what survives) for one idea and one
warning.

- **The idea worth stealing — Overdrive rescue, with the cap deleted.** *"Any active player can
  activate their Overdrive to bring failed players back into the song, saving the band member."*
  A teammate pulling you back in is the only genuinely **cooperative anti-failure mechanic in
  the whole genre**, and a parent-and-child version of it has no loser in it. The cap ("a band
  member can only be saved twice; after the third failure, they cannot be brought back") is the
  only part that must go.
- **The warning — public, audible failure.** Failing out **mutes your instrument from the audio
  mix** while your friends keep playing. That is punishment delivered socially and audibly at
  once, and it is the opposite of *"a child should leave every session feeling capable."*

**Rock Band 3 (26 October 2010)** is the genre's one literal keyboard — a **25-key
velocity-sensitive MIDI keyboard**, wearable as a keytar — plus **Pro modes** designed to
"accurately mimic playing of real instruments," which reviewers said left players with real
muscle memory. ⚠️ **This is not the precedent it appears to be:** its mapping is **pitch →
key**, not **letter → key**. It is a musical keyboard, not a QWERTY one, and it is not evidence
that a typing keyboard has ever been a rhythm instrument ([[rhythm-on-a-keyboard]]).

⚠️ **"Rock Band has a No Fail mode" is thinly sourced.** The **Rock Band 3** article states *"a
'no-fail' option exists"*; the **Rock Band (series)** article does not mention one at all.
Probable, not established. What *is* documented for younger players is **LEGO Rock Band's
"Super Easy" difficulty**, added specifically for them.

**ESRB ratings were not checked this pass** — see the same caution on [[guitar-hero]].

**What to draw:** an Xbox 360 or PS3, a television, and the instruments on the floor
([[console-hardware-silhouettes]]).

## The facts

- **Developer:** Harmonix. **Publisher:** MTV Games. **Distributor:** Electronic Arts.
- **Release:** Xbox 360 and PS3 **20 November 2007** (North America); PS2 18 December 2007;
  Wii 22 June 2008.
- **Instruments:** guitar modelled on a Fender Stratocaster; drum kit with four pads and a kick
  pedal; USB microphone for vocals.
- **Vocals:** scored on "how closely they match the relative pitch of the song's vocalist," with
  a pitch indicator.
- **Band Meter / rescue:** falling to the bottom fails a player out and **mutes their instrument
  from the audio mix**; any active player can spend **Overdrive** to bring them back; **a member
  can only be saved twice — the third failure is permanent for that song.**
- **Difficulty:** Easy, Medium, Hard, Expert. The series added **"Super Easy" in LEGO Rock Band
  for younger players**.
- **Recordings:** **51 of the 58 songs are master recordings** — a sharp contrast with
  [[guitar-hero]]'s cover-heavy 2005 launch. See [[music-licensing-in-games]].
- **Rock Band 3 (26 October 2010):** 25 full-sized velocity-sensitive MIDI keys, usable outside
  the game; Basic mode maps five white keys to coloured notes while Pro mode uses ten white and
  black keys with real fingering; Pro guitar/bass require matching specific strings and frets;
  three-part vocal harmonies.
