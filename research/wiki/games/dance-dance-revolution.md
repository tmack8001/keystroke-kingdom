Dance Dance Revolution (Konami's Bemani division, September 26, 1998) introduced full-body
gaming via a physical dance platform with pressure-sensitive directional pads — replacing
hands with feet as the primary input, and launching an entire genre of rhythm/exercise
gaming.

## In this game

Dance Dance Revolution is not an assigned Keystroke Kingdom cabinet, but marks the late-90s
close of the arcade era covered by this research file, contemporaneous with
[[goldeneye-007]] and [[final-fantasy-vii]].

## The facts

- Developer: Konami (Bemani division). **Correction (release date):** the original release
  is September 26, 1998 (Japan) — an earlier draft's "November 1998" date actually
  described the later "Internet Ranking Version" revision (November 18, 1998), a real date
  attached to the wrong event. North America arcade release followed March 1999.
- Visual style: scrolling arrows moving upward toward stationary "receptor" arrows; pink
  arrows for vertical (up/down), baby blue for horizontal (left/right); neon-lit cabinet
  with illuminated translucent dance panels.
- Input: raised metal dance platform with four acrylic glass directional pads per player,
  each with four pressure-activated switches; metal safety bar for balance.
- **Correction (grading scale):** the 1998 original used S/A/B/C/D grading — the familiar
  AAA-to-E scale came later; AAA debuted in 5thMix (2001). Also, "Butterfly" by Smile.dk
  (the franchise's most iconic song) was added in 2ndMix (1999), not present at the 1998
  launch.
- Technical: Bemani System 573 (PlayStation-based) for early versions.
