Wave Race 64 (**Nintendo EAD**, Japan **27 September 1996**, North America **4 November 1996**) is
a personal-watercraft racer whose real subject is **water**. Its wave simulation — built after a
programmer's tech demo of wave physics caught Shigeru Miyamoto's eye — used the N64's alpha
blending to make water *"simultaneously transparent and reflective"*, and the swell actually moves
the rider. **ESRB E, no descriptors.**

## In this game

**The recommended water station for Act 7 — Speed / Racing.** It is a genuine generational step
rather than a reskin: the car arc's rendering ladder never covers **a moving, physical surface that
changes how the vehicle behaves moment to moment**, which is a different idea from
[[sega-rally-championship]]'s per-surface friction (that changes grip; this changes the *ground's
shape*). A theme author gets something to draw that no car cabinet offers.

Its **power-arrow buoy mechanic is also a One-Rule gift**: passing buoys correctly *adds* speed
(up to five arrows), so the reward is additive and the "penalty" for a missed buoy is simply
**losing the bonus you had built** — the same shape as [[mario-kart]]'s coins, which
`research/racing-games-by-era.md` §12 already flags as the most reusable idea in the genre.

## The facts

- **Developer/publisher:** Nintendo EAD / Nintendo. **Japan 27 September 1996; North America
  4 November 1996; PAL 29 April 1997.** Nintendo 64; later iQue Player (2003) and Virtual Console.
  *(Date caution: some sources give 1 November for the NA date; Wikipedia's article says
  **4 November 1996**.)*
- **Origin:** a programmer's tech demo showing off **wave programming** on the new hardware caught
  producer **Shigeru Miyamoto's** attention and became the game's core mechanic.
- **Water technology:** the N64's **alpha blending** produces water that is *"simultaneously
  transparent and reflective."* The swell is a dynamic surface rather than a texture trick.
- **Buoy slalom:** pass **red buoys on the right, yellow on the left**. Each correctly navigated
  buoy lights a **power arrow** — up to five — increasing acceleration.
- **Punishment mechanics:** missing a single buoy **resets accumulated power**; missing **five
  buoys** in a race, or straying outside the pink course-boundary buoys for **more than five
  seconds**, is **disqualification**. (So there *is* a real fail state to strip — but the
  moment-to-moment loop is additive.)
- **Reception:** IGN 9.7/10; Metacritic 92; praised for controls and dynamic water.
- **Rating:** **ESRB E for Everyone**, no content descriptors, no interactive elements.
