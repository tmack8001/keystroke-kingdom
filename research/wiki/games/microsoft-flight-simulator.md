Microsoft Flight Simulator is **Microsoft's longest-running software product line, predating
Windows by three years** — version 1.0 shipped for the IBM PC in **November 1982**, licensed from
Bruce Artwick's subLOGIC ([[flight-simulator-fs1]]). It is one of the longest-running series in
all of software, was inducted into The Strong's **World Video Game Hall of Fame in 2021**, and its
2020 incarnation streams a **>2.5-petabyte** model of the actual Earth. It is also the single best
window in games onto rendering technology climbing across forty years.

## In this game

**The spine of the proposed flight act** (`research/flight-simulators-by-era.md`). One product
name carries the player from wireframe to photogrammetry, so an act can literally *be* this
franchise's history and still hit Trevor's hard "graphics must climb with the era" constraint —
see [[flight-sim-rendering-eras]].

Two things make it a Keystroke Kingdom fact rather than merely an aviation fact:
1. **It was a compatibility test for the PC itself.** *"If a computer could run Microsoft Flight
   Simulator and Lotus 1-2-3, it was 100% IBM PC-compatible."* A game was the industry's yardstick
   for whether a computer was real. That is a first-class history card for a game whose thesis is
   *typing unlocks computing*.
2. **You flew it with the keyboard.** See [[keyboard-as-cockpit]] — Microsoft shipped a printed
   **Quick Reference Card** in the FS 4.0 box in 1989.

## The facts

- **Origins.** Bruce Artwick's subLOGIC shipped FS1 for the Apple II in January 1980; in **1982**
  subLOGIC licensed an IBM PC version to Microsoft, marketed as **Microsoft Flight Simulator**.
  Wikipedia dates Artwick's development start to **1977**.
- **Version 1.0, November 1982, IBM PC.** A **Cessna 182 Skylane** over four US regions — Chicago,
  Los Angeles, New York City and Seattle — starting by default at **Meigs Field, Chicago**. It
  also carried a **"Europe 1917"** mode flying a Sopwith Camel with dogfighting, inherited from
  FS1's "British Ace." Sold about **800,000 copies in its first five years**.
- **The graphics ladder** (see [[flight-sim-rendering-eras]] for the full table): CGA (1.0, 1982) →
  RGB/Hercules (2.0, 1984) → EGA high-resolution (3.0, 1988) → dynamic scenery (4.0, 1989) →
  **texture mapping, first in 5.0 (1993)** → satellite imagery (5.1, 1995) → **DirectX 3D
  (FS98, 1997)** → 3D elevation and GPS (FS2000, 1999) → air traffic control (FS2002) → 3D clouds
  and real-world weather sync (FS2004) → **FSX (2006)** → *Microsoft Flight* (2012, a casual spin-
  off, discontinued) → **MSFS (2020)** → **MSFS 2024**.
- **Sublogic's own parallel line** continued too: Flight Simulator II (Apple II December 1983;
  C64 June 1984; Atari 8-bit October 1984) and Flight Simulator III (Macintosh March 1986; Amiga
  and Atari ST November 1986, with a windowing system allowing multiple simultaneous 3D views).
- **Scale.** *Video Games & Computer Entertainment* called it in 1989 *"unquestionably the most
  popular computer game in the world, with nearly two million copies sold."* By **June 1999** the
  series had sold **21 million units**. It took **seven Guinness World Records** in the 2008
  *Gamer's Edition*, including "Longest Running Flight Sim Series."
- **MSFS (2020).** Developed by **Asobo Studio** (~120 people), published by Xbox Game Studios;
  **PC August 18, 2020**; Xbox Series X|S July 27, 2021. Streams a Bing Maps dataset of
  **>2.5 petabytes** from Azure — **37,000 airports** (30–40 handcrafted, the rest AI-generated
  from satellite imagery), 2 million cities, 1.5 billion buildings, 2 trillion trees, 117 million
  lakes — with live weather (meteoblue) and live air traffic (FlightAware). **ESRB E**, no content
  descriptors. **No combat.** When you crash, *the game cuts to black with a message explaining
  the cause* — no damage animation, no collision depicted.
- **MSFS 2024** (November 19, 2024) adds a **Career Mode** of real civilian aviation jobs —
  medevac, remote cargo, **aerial firefighting**, **search & rescue**, up to airline transport
  pilot — with ~54 training courses, certifications, and a per-mission difficulty rating
  *"to prevent non-experienced pilots from ending up in complex scenarios."* **ESRB E.**

## Corrections carried on this page

- **"The series began in 1982."** Only *Microsoft's* involvement did. The software line begins with
  subLOGIC's FS1, **January 1980** (written 1979). Say "Microsoft's longest-running product line"
  precisely — that framing is Wikipedia's and it is correct; "the first flight simulator" is not
  the same sentence.
- **The Strong's own 2021 press release contradicts itself** — its body text says the game launched
  in **1982**, while the quotation from assistant VP **Jeremy Saucier** says it *"debuted in 1983."*
  **Use 1982**, which matches every other source.
