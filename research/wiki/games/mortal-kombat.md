Mortal Kombat (Midway, August 1992), created by Ed Boon and John Tobias, used digitized
footage of real actors (via Hi-8 camcorder) for photorealistic fighters — and its graphic
Fatalities helped trigger the 1993–1994 US Senate hearings that led, via the IDSA, to the
creation of the ESRB rating system.

## In this game

Mortal Kombat is not an assigned Keystroke Kingdom cabinet, but its role in prompting the
ESRB is directly relevant to any content-rating or industry-history discussion adjacent to
[[street-fighter-ii]] and the fighting-game era.

## The facts

- Developer: Midway — created by Ed Boon and John Tobias. Released August 1992 (a few
  retrospectives cite October 8, 1992, but contemporaneous trade-press data showing MK
  already the #2 upright cabinet by September 1992 supports the August date).
- Visual style: digitized sprites from real filmed actors — 64 colors and roughly 300
  frames of animation per character, 8 MB of graphics data total. Palette swaps created
  additional characters (Scorpion's yellow suit recolored to blue for Sub-Zero, green for
  Reptile).
- Input: 8-way joystick + 5 buttons (high punch, low punch, high kick, low kick, block) —
  a dedicated block button, unlike Street Fighter II's hold-back-to-block.
- Iconic: Fatalities (gruesome finishing moves); "FINISH HIM!" announcer call; stop-motion
  model for Goro by Curt Chiarelli; 4-person development team, ~10 months, ~$1 million
  budget.
- The game's violence sparked the 1993–1994 US Senate hearings (Senators Lieberman and
  Kohl, December 9, 1993 and March 4, 1994), leading first to the IDSA industry body (April
  1994), then the ESRB (active September 1994).
- Technical: Midway Y Unit and T Unit arcade boards (~400x256 resolution).
