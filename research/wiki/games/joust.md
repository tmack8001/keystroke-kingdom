Joust (Williams Electronics, 1982), designed by John Newcomer, put knights on flapping
ostriches and storks in a momentum-based flight model — a genuine technical differentiator
for its era, and one of the earliest arcade games to offer simultaneous two-player
cooperative or competitive play.

## In this game

Joust is the **Level 5 cabinet**, teaching G and H and completing Act 1's home row. Its
fluid, almost-physical sprite animation (wing-flap cycles, bubbling lava) is the visual peak
of Act 1 before the Mystery Cabinet reveal. Completing Level 5 triggers the transition into
Act 2 ([[donkey-kong]]'s Computer Wing). See `.claude/rules/levels.md` Level 5 and
`.claude/rules/transitions.md` "Mystery Beat."

## The facts

- Developer: Williams Electronics — designed by John Newcomer (his first game at Williams);
  programmed by Bill Pfutzenreuter; art by Janice "Jan" Woldenberg-Miller; cabinet art by
  Python Anghelo; audio by Tim Murphy and John Kotlarik. Released 1982.
- Visual style: fluid, physical-feeling sprite animation — knights on ostriches (Player 1)
  and storks (Player 2) with genuine wing-flap cycles. Orange lava glow, black sky, tan
  knights.
- Input: one "Flap" button (repeated presses create momentum-based ascend/hover/descend) +
  2-way joystick (left/right only, no up/down) — unconventional for its time.
- Iconic: two-player simultaneous mode allowing cooperative play or optional
  player-vs-player combat — an early example of arcade games offering that choice. The
  "Lava Troll" (giant hand) drags down low-flying mounts. ~26,000 cabinets produced.
- Technical: custom Williams hardware; momentum/physics-based flight model, not simple
  4-directional movement.
