Colossal Cave Adventure, written by Will Crowther c. 1975–76 and expanded by Don Woods
in 1977, is the founding work of the text-adventure genre — the game that introduced
[[xyzzy]] as its magic word and the canonical "Nothing happens" response when used
incorrectly.

## In this game

The game itself isn't directly reproduced in Keystroke Kingdom, but its magic word
[[xyzzy]] is a title-screen easter egg — see that page and [[hacker-culture]] for the
in-game text. Listed here as an `entity` page since Colossal Cave Adventure is the
origin work the XYZZY joke depends on for its accuracy. It's also the shared ancestor
of both branches of prose "type a direction, read what happens" games explored for a
possible Level 20/text-adventure direction: single-player [[zork]] (its direct
successor) and the inherently multiplayer [[mud]] line (MUD1's creator named his game
in tribute to a Zork variant, having played Adventure-descended games first) — see
[[mud]] for the archetype distinction.

## The facts

- Will Crowther writes the original version c. 1975–76 (sources give "1975," "1975–76,"
  or "c. 1976" depending on source).
- Don Woods expands the game in 1977, which is when the well-known "Nothing happens"
  wording is most commonly cited from.
- The game is broadly credited as the origin of the interactive-fiction/text-adventure
  genre.
- **The one/two-word compass parser** *(confirmed 2026-07-17)*: players moved via
  one- or two-word commands including single-letter compass abbreviations (`n`,
  `s`, `e`, `w`) — per Wikipedia's own framing, this became "standard in
  interactive fiction titles" from this game forward. Cited as the origin point for
  typed-directional movement, researched as precedent for a possible Act 6
  tile-world redesign — see [[ascii-overhead-world-conventions]] Q4.
