*Space Channel 5* (Sega AM9 / United Game Artists, **16 December 1999**, Dreamcast) is the
purest **Simon-says** game in the commercial rhythm canon: reporter Ulala watches an opponent
perform a sequence of moves and calls, then **repeats it back in rhythm**. Produced and
conceived by **Tetsuya Mizuguchi**, directed by **Takashi Yuda**.

## In this game

Cited in [[rhythm-games-by-era]] §8 as the third link in the **call-and-response** chain —
[[touch-me-atari]] 1974 → [[simon-electronic-game]] 1978 → [[parappa-the-rapper]] 1996 →
*Space Channel 5* 1999 → [[rhythm-heaven]] 2006 — and specifically as the entry that shows the
form **scaling to a character-driven world with a story**, not just an abstract drill. The
machine performs; the machine waits; you answer. That wait is the only known way to hold a
rhythm mechanic and this game's sacred wrong-keypress contract in the same hand
([[call-and-response]]).

**Punishment to strip (One Rule):** a **ratings meter** that falls on mistakes and **fails the
player at zero**, plus boss fights that give **one heart per mistake** — lives under another
name. Keep the caller/echo structure and the characters; delete the meter and the hearts.

**What to draw:** a Dreamcast and a television ([[console-hardware-silhouettes]]) — not a
coin-op.

## The facts

- **Developer:** Sega AM9 (later United Game Artists). **Publisher:** Sega.
- **Release:** **16 December 1999**, Dreamcast (Japan); PlayStation 2 ports 2002–2003; Game Boy
  Advance 2003.
- **Play:** the player controls Ulala, who "mimics the movements and vocalisations of her
  opponents" in rhythm with the music. Visual and audio cues indicate the required button
  presses; the game alternates dance sections with shooting sections.
- **Scoring / failure:** correct timing raises a ratings meter; mistakes lower it. **Ratings at
  zero = failure.** In boss encounters the player has hearts — one lost per mistake — and losing
  them all is a game over requiring a restart.
- **Credits:** Tetsuya Mizuguchi (producer, principal concept); Takashi Yuda (director, and the
  voice of a supporting character, Fuze).
