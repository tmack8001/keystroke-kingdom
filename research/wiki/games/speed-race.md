Speed Race (Taito, November 1974), designed by Tomohiro Nishikado, is the first vertically
scrolling video game — a driving game whose real invention was **scrolling itself**: Nishikado
animated the road and modulated the speed of oncoming cars so the world moved past a fixed
player car.

## In this game

Documented for **Act 7 — Speed / Racing** (`research/racing-games-by-era.md` §1) mainly as
history and as a warning. Its collision rule — a crash "resets the player's speed and starts
them at the beginning of the track" — is the single clearest One Rule violation in the whole
genre: total progress loss from one mistake. Keystroke Kingdom's sacred wrong-keypress behavior
(the letter stays and waits, nothing is lost) is the exact opposite, and Speed Race is the best
contrast case for saying so. Its designer went on to make [[space-invaders]] (Level 2).

## The facts

- Developer: **Taito**. Designer: **Tomohiro Nishikado**. Release **November 1974** (Japan);
  February–March 1975 in North America via Midway, rebranded **Racer** (February 1975) and
  **Wheels** (March 1975), the latter adding a sit-down cabinet.
- Innovation: the first vertically scrolling video game. The scrolling is a trick — an animated
  background road image plus speed-modulated oncoming traffic — not a true camera.
- Controls: steering wheel to move left/right, accelerator for speed.
- Rendering: overhead, monochrome, sprites with collision detection; the track width narrows and
  widens as the player moves up the road.
- **Punishment mechanics:** a **90-second timer**, extendable by earning points; colliding with
  another car **resets the player's speed and returns them to the beginning of the track**.
