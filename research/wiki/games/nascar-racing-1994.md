NASCAR Racing (Papyrus Design Group, November 2 1994, MS-DOS) is the game that proved
ultra-realistic racing simulation was not a niche market — over a million copies for the original
and over two million for it and its sequel by March 1998, at a time when "sim" meant "hardcore."

## In this game

The era-honest home for Trevor's **NASCAR** anchor in **Act 7 — Speed / Racing**
(`research/racing-games-by-era.md` §7). The pairing worth building a station around: in **the same
year, 1994**, the NASCAR cultural moment produced both this simulation and Sega's arcade
[[daytona-usa]] (March 1994). Two very different games about one sport, eight months apart — the
genre's arcade/simulation fork made visible without a word of explanation. Part of the Papyrus
lineage [[indianapolis-500-the-simulation]] → here → [[grand-prix-legends]] → [[iracing]].

## The facts

- Developer: **Papyrus Design Group**. Publisher **Virgin Interactive** (MS-DOS);
  **Sierra On-Line** for the Mac and PlayStation versions.
- Release: **November 2 1994** (MS-DOS); Classic Mac OS **November 2 1996**; PlayStation 1996.
- Sales: 350,000 units by December 1995 and 400,000 by February 1996; the bundled Track Pack
  added over 225,000 in 1998; **NASCAR Racing and its sequel shipped above 2 million copies
  globally by March 1998**, with the original alone passing 1 million by 2004.
- Reception: *Computer Gaming World* ranked it the 31st-best computer game ever released (1996);
  *PC Gamer*'s editors called it "the best racing game ever created" and gave it their 1994 Best
  Sports Game award.
- **Sourcing caution:** the fetched Wikipedia article does **not** state whether the game held an
  official NASCAR licence, nor does it detail its damage or physics models. Community
  sim-racing sources describe Gen-4 chassis and Hoosier/Goodyear tyre options; treat those
  specifics as **[unverified]** until a stronger source is found.
