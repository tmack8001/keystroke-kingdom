Gran Trak 10 (Atari, May 1974) is the first arcade car-driving video game and the first video
game to use a steering wheel — a monochrome, overhead, discrete-logic cabinet that shipped with
the complete driving-controls vocabulary (wheel, accelerator and brake pedals, four-position
gear stick) already intact at the genre's birth.

## In this game

The earliest candidate opening station for **Act 7 — Speed / Racing** (see
`research/racing-games-by-era.md` §1). Its rendering sits squarely inside Keystroke Kingdom's
Act 1 fidelity bar — [[discrete-logic-era]] hardware, one colour, rectangles — so it costs a
theme author nothing extra. The recommended opener is [[monaco-gp]] (1979, matching the game's
own fictional year), with Gran Trak 10 as the "this is where it all began" cold open if one is
wanted. Contrast its overhead view with [[night-driver]]'s first-person pylons and
[[pole-position]]'s rear-view road.

## The facts

- Developer/publisher: **Atari, Inc.** (built through its subsidiary Cyan Engineering — the same
  outfit behind [[breakout]]'s production redesign). Arcade release **May 1974**.
- Controls: steering wheel, accelerator **and** brake pedals, and a gear stick with reverse,
  first, second and third.
- Rendering: monochrome, overhead perspective, single-screen track walled with pylons.
- Technical first: believed to contain **the first use of integrated-circuit (mask ROM) memory
  in an arcade game**, storing its graphical data — a departure from earlier diode-based
  approaches.
- Claims: "the first arcade car driving video game" and "the first video game to use a steering
  wheel."
- **Punishment mechanics** (must be reframed under the One Rule): a countdown timer starting at
  78 seconds (adjustable to ~105); colliding with a pylon wall crashes and spins the car.
- Business footnote: manufacturing miscalculations meant Atari lost roughly **$100 per cabinet**,
  contributing to a ~$500,000 fiscal-year loss — about as much as the company had made the prior
  year.
