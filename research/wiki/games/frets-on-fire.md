*Frets on Fire* (Unreal Voodoo, **3 August 2006**, GPL, Windows/macOS/Linux) is the single most
important artefact in this project's rhythm research, because it is the one that answers the
input question literally. In its own words on SourceForge: **"Frets on Fire is a game of musical
skill and fast fingers. The aim of the game is to play guitar with the keyboard as accurately as
possible."** Its documented default bindings: **F1–F5 are the five frets; Enter picks.** It won
the Assembly 2006 game-development competition and was downloaded **over 14 million times** on
SourceForge between 2007 and May 2017.

## In this game

The proof that rhythm games run on keyboards — **and the proof of the ceiling**
([[rhythm-on-a-keyboard]], [[rhythm-games-by-era]] §6).

- **Yes, the keyboard works.** Written in Python by a hobbyist, with a 14-million-download
  audience and a modding scene (FoFiX) that re-themed it to look like [[guitar-hero]] and
  [[rock-band]]. Nobody needed a plastic guitar.
- **But the keyboard is not the instrument — it is standing in for one.** F1–F5 are *pretending
  to be frets*. The player is even invited to hold the keyboard like a guitar neck. The letters
  mean nothing; the keys are just five buttons that happen to be on a keyboard. Every keyboard
  rhythm game found this pass does the same thing: [[stepmania]]'s arrow keys pretend to be a
  dance pad; osu!mania's keys pretend to be falling columns.
- **So the gap stands:** **no rhythm game found treats letters as the notes.** That is the space
  a Keystroke Kingdom music act would be inventing, with no precedent to borrow tuning from.

**Punishment to strip (One Rule):** it inherits [[guitar-hero]]'s loop wholesale — a note
highway with a timing window. Everything in Guitar Hero's ledger row applies here.

**What to draw:** a computer and **a keyboard held like a guitar** — which is genuinely what
this game looks like, and is the only cabinet-art joke in this whole area that is also true.

## The facts

- **Developer:** Unreal Voodoo. **Release: 3 August 2006**; final official version 5 November
  2008 (the SourceForge project's last file release is dated **18 September 2014**,
  `FretsOnFire-1.3.110-win32.zip`).
- **Platforms:** Windows, macOS, Linux. **Written in Python**, licensed under the **GNU General
  Public License** (the SourceForge listing specifies **GPLv2**), with other free/open-source
  components under various licences.
- **Controls (from the project's own how-to documentation):** **F1, F2, F3, F4, F5 are the five
  frets; Enter is the pick/strum button.** The instruction is to "press and hold the
  approriate [sic] frets when notes appear and tap the pick button when the notes hit the row of
  keys at the bottom of the screen." These are defaults and are rebindable in the settings menu.
- **Recognition:** winner of the **Assembly 2006** game-development competition.
- **Community:** **Frets on Fire X (FoFiX)** is the major fork, with custom themes mimicking
  Guitar Hero and Rock Band; players ran their own high-score servers. **Over 14 million
  downloads** on SourceForge, 2007 – May 2017.
