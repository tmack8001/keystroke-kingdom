*Beat Saber* (Beat Games, early access **1 May 2018**, full release **21 May 2019**) is the
modern endpoint of the note-highway lineage — [[beatmania]] → [[dance-dance-revolution]] →
[[guitarfreaks]] → [[guitar-hero]] → [[rock-band]] → here — and the one that strips the genre down
to its irreducible core: coloured blocks arrive **"in sync with the song's beats and notes"** in a
**4×3 grid**, and you cut them with two VR sabers, in the right colour, from the right direction.
It matters to this project for exactly two reasons, and neither is its gameplay. First, it is
[[rhythm-games-by-era]] §8's proof that *the note highway **is** the timing window*. Second, and
more usefully: **it ships the trap.** Its **"No Fail" modifier lets you finish a song you would
otherwise have lost — and lowers your final rank for it.** That is a mercy that costs something,
which is not mercy, and it is the single clearest example in the genre of the mistake this game
must not make.

## In this game

### The trap, named — and verified

> *"The 'No Fail' modifier allows the player to complete the song without fail if the energy bar
> depletes, **but the final rank will be lowered**."*
> — *Beat Saber*, Wikipedia, fetched 2026-09-13

**The claim in [[rhythm-games-by-era]] §7 and §8 is real and verbatim, not a paraphrase.** It is
also symmetrical in the same sentence-family: the *"Super Fast Song"* modifier **raises** rank
while playing at 150% speed. So the scoring system is explicitly a **difficulty-weighted grade** —
making it easier costs rank, making it harder buys rank.

**Why that is a One Rule violation even though nobody is ejected:** the punishment did not go
away, it moved into the scoreboard. A child who turns on the safe mode is told, quietly and
permanently, that the safe way is the lesser way. `.claude/rules/product.md`'s architecture is the
opposite by construction — **SURVIVAL is opt-in and additive, EXPERIENCE tiers are "MORE play,
never a penalty," and word-length is "a best-effort suggestion, never a wall."** Nothing you can
switch on in this game makes your result worth less.

**The rule this page exists to hand the spec lane:** *if a Keystroke Kingdom rhythm cabinet ever
ships a gentleness switch, that switch must not touch any number the player sees.* The moment a
"no fail" toggle is worth fewer points, a lower rank, a dimmer star or a smaller celebration, it
has become Beat Saber's modifier with a warmer font.

### The secondary lesson: original music first, licensed catalogue later

Beat Saber launched on **original music written for it by Jaroslav Beck**, who *"met the team in
Prague to convince them to let him create the soundtrack."* Licensed packs came **after** the game
was a hit — Monstercat (March 2019), Camellia (January 2020), Metallica (December 2024) — and the
official site now lists **eight volumes of Original Soundtracks** alongside packs from Queen, Daft
Punk, Billie Eilish, BTS and the rest. **A four-million-copy rhythm game bootstrapped on music it
owned.** That is the same arc [[guitar-hero]] took with cover versions, and it is the argument
[[music-licensing-in-games]] makes for why Lyria is this project's correct and only path.

### Why it is not a cabinet candidate

- **The input does not translate** ([[rhythm-games-by-era]] §6): two 6-DoF motion controllers
  swung through 3D space have no keyboard mapping, and faking one would be a key-glyph pun of the
  kind [[racing-games-by-era]] §17 and the cabinet-art rule forbid.
- **It requires a VR headset.** Every other station in this project's research can be drawn as a
  cabinet, a console or a computer; this one cannot be played by a five-year-old in a browser at
  all. If it appears anywhere, it appears as **history** — the last stop on the note-highway
  timeline — not as a machine on the floor.
- ⚠️ **Age suitability was NOT established this pass.** Neither the Wikipedia article nor
  `beatsaber.com` states a minimum age, health warning or parental guidance where this tooling
  could read it, and headset-manufacturer age policies were not checked. **Do not assert anything
  about whether a child may play it.**

## The facts

- **Developer:** Beat Games (Prague). **Publishers:** Beat Games and, from 2019, Oculus Studios.
  **Early access 1 May 2018; full release 21 May 2019** (PlayStation 4 and Windows). Platforms
  now include PS4/PS5, Meta Quest, SteamVR and Windows Mixed Reality.
- **The loop:** *"a stream of approaching blocks laid out in sync with the song's beats and notes,
  located in one of the 12 possible positions of a **4x3 grid**."* Blocks carry a colour (matching
  one saber) and may be *"marked with an arrow indicating one of eight possible directions."*
  Scoring depends on *"the length and angle of the swing and the accuracy of the cut."* There are
  also **bombs** not to hit and **walls** to dodge.
- **The failure mechanic:** *"Underneath the path where the blocks travel is a white 'energy bar,'
  also called 'battery'. At the beginning of every song, the bar is halfway full, slowly filling up
  as the player hits notes correctly."* Mistakes drain it, and *"if the bar becomes empty, the
  level will end."* It is the [[guitar-hero]] Rock Meter and the [[dance-dance-revolution]] Dance
  Gauge under a third name — see [[rhythm-games-by-era]] §7.
- **Modifiers:** Beat Games' own store copy advertises **"5 difficulty levels, 15 gameplay
  modifiers, custom environment colors and more."** Two of the fifteen are documented in detail:
  **No Fail** (finish anyway, **rank lowered**) and **Super Fast Song** (150% speed, **rank
  raised**).
- **Music:** original soundtrack by **Jaroslav Beck**; a **custom-song editor** was announced in
  May 2018, delayed, and **added in May 2019**; licensed packs from March 2019 onward. The official
  site lists **8 volumes** of Original Soundtracks plus packs including Monstercat, Camellia,
  Metallica, Coldplay, Queen, Daft Punk, The Rolling Stones, Britney Spears, Imagine Dragons,
  Lizzo, Billie Eilish, BTS, Linkin Park and Green Day.
- **Scale:** *"over a million copies by March 2019"*, **over four million by February 2021** —
  i.e. the modern genre's biggest commercial success, achieved **without** plastic peripherals or
  (at launch) a licensed catalogue. Compare the collapse figures in [[rhythm-games-by-era]] §4.

   **full release 21 May 2019**. §7's ledger row says "(2018)" — defensible for when it appeared,
   but a card or cabinet plaque should not say "released 2018" without the qualifier.
