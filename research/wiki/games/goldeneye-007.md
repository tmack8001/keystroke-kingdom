GoldenEye 007 (Rare, 1997), lead designed by Martin Hollis over roughly 2.5 years of
development, is credited with setting the standard for multiplayer console combat — but its
famous four-player split-screen mode was a late addition, not part of the original plan, and
was nearly cut for time.

## In this game

GoldenEye 007 is the **Level 17 cabinet** in Act 4 (The Command Center), teaching 4 and 7
(ring fingers). See `.claude/rules/levels.md` Level 17.

## The facts

- Developed by Rare, published by Nintendo, released 1997 for N64. Lead designer Martin
  Hollis; development began January 1995 (~2.5 years).
- **Correction:** four-player split-screen multiplayer was **not part of the original
  plan** — it was added late in development (starting ~March/April 1997), primarily
  implemented by programmer Steve Ellis, and was nearly cut for time.
- It is nonetheless genuinely credited as pioneering/notable for console FPS: Edge magazine
  (via Wikipedia) says it "set the standard for multiplayer console combat" until Halo
  (2001) surpassed it; The Strong Museum calls it "still considered one of the best
  multiplayer experiences ever produced on a Nintendo system."

## Gameplay

*Documented here for research fidelity; see the downstream note below for how this
must be abstracted before it reaches a child.* In single-player, the player completes
linear, mission-based levels (drawn from the film's plot) with multiple objectives per
level — recovering items, destroying targets, defeating specific enemies, rescuing
hostages, or using gadgets — with no freedom to leave the mission area, though small
side-routes to the same objective exist. Difficulty (Agent/Secret Agent/00 Agent) changes
which objectives are active and how tough enemies are. Stealth is a first-class,
encouraged (not mandatory) approach: frequent unsuppressed gunfire alerts distant guards
and can trigger alarms that spawn more enemies, while silenced weapons and sniper rifles
let the player clear rooms quietly; over 20 weapons are available, damage is
location-based (headshots strongest), and manual/free aiming (rather than a fixed
center-screen reticle, the era's FPS norm) was a genuine innovation. The four-player
split-screen deathmatch mode — added late in development, not part of the original plan
— lets up to four players compete across several game styles (e.g. "You Only Live Twice,"
which gives each player two lives before elimination). The signature mechanic the game is
remembered for is exactly this pairing: a stealth-and-objectives single-player structure
married to console-native four-player split-screen deathmatch, which together are widely
credited with legitimizing the FPS genre on consoles.
