Midnight Club: Street Racing (Angel Studios, later Rockstar San Diego; published by Rockstar
Games, October 2000) was a PlayStation 2 launch-window open-city street racer set at night in
New York and London. It was this project's leading candidate for Act 7's modern open-road
station until a ratings check found that **the PlayStation 2 game is ESRB T for Teen, not E** —
the "E" that circulated in earlier research is the **Game Boy Advance** port's record.

## In this game

**Documented here as a rejected candidate, not a planned station.** The Act 7 research
(`research/racing-games-by-era.md` §8, §9, §15) recommended Midnight Club as the first-choice,
"rating-clean" open-road alternative to [[grand-theft-auto]]. **That recommendation is
withdrawn**, and this page exists so the reason is findable rather than lost in a report:

- **ESRB's record for the PlayStation 2 title is `T` (Teen), descriptor `Animated Violence`.**
- **ESRB's `E` record is for the Game Boy Advance version**, a different game on different
  hardware, listed under the title "Midnight Club Street Racing."

Under the project's own bar — kid-safe first, and *verify superlatives and convenient facts*
— a T-rated console game is not the right terminal-era cabinet for a game a five-year-old
plays. The recommended station is **[[need-for-speed-underground]]** (2003), whose
**console/PC** ESRB record is **E for Everyone**.

There is a second, softer reason to prefer Underground: Midnight Club's core loop is
**police-evading street racing in live city traffic**, which carries the same
authority-figure-punishment register `racing-games-by-era.md` §12 already flags for cutting
in [[need-for-speed]] (1994). Underground's core loop is a **garage**.

## The facts

- Developer **Angel Studios** (acquired by Take-Two in 2002 and renamed **Rockstar San Diego**);
  publisher **Rockstar Games**. **PlayStation 2, October 26 2000** in North America — a launch-
  window title for the console. A Game Boy Advance version followed.
- Open-city night street racing with point-to-point checkpoint routes through **New York and
  London**, with live traffic and police.
- **ESRB, PlayStation 2: `T` for Teen, content descriptor `Animated Violence`, no interactive
  elements.**
- **ESRB, Game Boy Advance ("Midnight Club Street Racing"): `E` for Everyone, no descriptors,
  no interactive elements.**
- The discrepancy is visible in third-party databases too: MobyGames and Glitchwave both list
  the game as **T**, which is what prompted this recheck.

## Why the error happened — and what it teaches

The earlier pass searched ESRB for the game's title, got a hit, and read the letter. It did not
read the **platform** on the record. ESRB rates **per platform**, and ports are frequently rated
differently because they *are* different builds — a Game Boy Advance version of a PS2 street
racer contains far less of what earned the T.

This is the project's recurring **plausible-but-wrong** failure class: the fact was convenient
(a rating-clean substitute for GTA, exactly when one was wanted), it came from an
institutional source, and it was still wrong. The fix is a rule, now applied across this wiki:
**every ESRB citation must state the platform list shown on the record.**
