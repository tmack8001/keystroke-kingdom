Tron (Bally Midway, August 1982) is the glowing-neon arcade tie-in to Disney's 1982 film,
combining four distinct sub-games (I/O Tower, MCP Cone, Light Cycles, Battle Tanks) inside
one of the most visually distinctive cabinet designs ever built.

## In this game

Tron is parked as a possible Act 6 "mastery wing" cabinet alongside [[mario-kart]] — a
post-graduation greatest-hits wing at modern fidelity where historical-ordering rules are
deliberately loosened, since Zelda, GoldenEye, DKC, and FF7 already own cabinets. See

## The facts

- Developer: Bally Midway. Released August 1982, licensed from the Disney film of the same
  year.
- Visual style: glowing neon aesthetic — color-coded sub-games (blue player Light Cycle vs.
  yellow opponents; red player tank vs. blue enemies). The cabinet itself was distinctively
  lit in glowing blue/black light.
- Input: 8-way joystick with trigger button + rotary dial for aiming.
- Iconic: 12 levels named after programming languages (RPG, COBOL, BASIC, FORTRAN, etc.);
  approximately 10,000 cabinets sold; named "Coin-Operated Game of the Year" by Electronic
  Games magazine.
- Technical: Midway MCR-II arcade system board — raster graphics, not vector, despite the
  glowing-lines aesthetic. Music programmed by Earl Vickers.
- Minor naming discrepancy noted: arcade-museum.com's own game description names the four
  sub-games Light Cycles, Grid Bugs, Tanks, and MCP Cone — slightly different labels than
  "I/O Tower" and "Battle Tanks" (Wikipedia's naming). Both describe the same content.
