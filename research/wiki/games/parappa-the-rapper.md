*PaRappa the Rapper* (NanaOn-Sha / Sony Computer Entertainment, **6 December 1996** Japan /
**17 November 1997** North America, PlayStation) is the game Wikipedia calls **"the first true
rhythm game"** and credits with "setting the basic template for subsequent titles in the rhythm
game genre." Produced and designed by **Masaya Matsuura** with artwork by **Rodney Greenblat**,
it works by **call-and-response**: a teacher raps a line, symbols matching that line appear on a
bar across the top of the screen, and the player answers by pressing the matching buttons.

## In this game

The **most instructive single title in the genre for Keystroke Kingdom**, because it is the one
that combines both of the genre's grammars — a scrolling notation bar *and* a teacher who waits
for you to answer — so it shows exactly which half is adoptable and which half is not.

- **Adoptable:** the teacher/student structure. Someone performs a phrase; you answer it. That
  is [[call-and-response]], and its pause is native, not bolted on.
- **Adoptable:** *arbitrary symbols on a bar mapped to buttons* is structurally identical to
  *letters mapped to keys*. This is the closest thing in the genre to a letter-as-note idea
  ([[rhythm-on-a-keyboard]] §the gap).
- **Not adoptable:** the ladder. See below.

**Punishment to strip (One Rule) — this is one of the two harshest entries in the ledger:**
performance is graded **COOL / GOOD / BAD / AWFUL**; performing badly twice in a row **demotes**
you a rung; and *"if the player ends the song on a Bad or Awful ranking or drops below the Awful
ranking at any point in the song, they will fail the song and have to start over."* A grade, a
demotion, and a restart-from-zero, stacked. Take the structure whole and **delete the entire
ladder** — every answer is an answer.

**Correction it forces on the genre's own story:** PaRappa is a **popularizer, not an
inventor**. [[simon-electronic-game]] (1978) and [[dance-aerobics]] (1987) both precede it, and
Wikipedia's own music-video-game article names Simon the earliest rhythm game. Right verb:
PaRappa **set the template**.

**What to draw:** a PlayStation and a television — never a coin-op.

## The facts

- **Developer:** NanaOn-Sha (with Japan Studio assistance). **Publisher:** Sony Computer
  Entertainment. **Producer/designer:** Masaya Matsuura. **Artist:** Rodney Greenblat.
- **Release:** Japan **6 December 1996**; North America **17 November 1997**. PlayStation;
  later PSP (2006) and PS4 (2017).
- **Play:** "a bar at the top of the screen will appear, showing symbols that match up to the
  teacher's lyrics"; the player times button presses to answer the teacher's rapped line.
- **Grading:** Awful / Bad / Good / Cool. A **Good** ranking is required to clear a stage;
  reaching **Cool** unlocks special endings.
- **Failure:** ending a song on Bad or Awful, or dropping below Awful mid-song, fails the song
  and restarts it.
- **Status:** "considered to be the first true rhythm game" and "influential in setting the
  basic template for subsequent titles in the rhythm game genre."
