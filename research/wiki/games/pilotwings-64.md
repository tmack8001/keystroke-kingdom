Pilotwings 64 (**Nintendo EAD, Nintendo R&D3 and Paradigm Simulation**; Japan **June 23, 1996**,
North America **September 29, 1996**) was one of only **two North American Nintendo 64 launch
titles** alongside Super Mario 64. Its co-developer **Paradigm Simulation was a Texas graphics
company that built military vehicle and flight simulators** before it made a video game — the
clearest single point where the real simulator industry and the games industry touch. **ESRB E.**

## In this game

**The recommended early-3D station for the flight act.** It supplies the hardware-3D rendering
step — *level of detail, mipmapping, z-buffering, texture filtering, anti-aliasing* — that the
racing act gets from its PS1/N64 block, but in the air and over real-world landmarks. And the
Paradigm story is the act's best "these two worlds are the same world" beat: the people who built
**actual** flight trainers built this.

## The facts

- **Developers:** Nintendo EAD, Nintendo R&D3 and **Paradigm Simulation** — *"a Texas-based
  graphics company"* with prior experience in *"military vehicle and flight simulators."*
- **Dates:** Japan **June 23, 1996**; North America **September 29, 1996**; Europe March 1, 1997.
  One of **three** N64 launch titles in Japan and Europe, and one of **two** in North America.
- **Vehicles:** **hang glider, jet pack ("Rocket Belt") and gyrocopter**, with bonus modes for
  skydiving, a human cannonball, and **"Birdman"** — a bird suit for free exploration of four
  islands, one of them a United States map featuring Mount Rushmore (with Mario's face added).
- **Rendering:** **level of detail, mipmapping, z-buffering, texture filtering and anti-aliasing**
  used to render large environments smoothly on constrained hardware by cutting polygon counts
  while holding visual quality.
- **Progression:** bronze / silver / gold **licences** awarded on time, accuracy and **landing
  softness**. *(A "softness of landing" score is a genuinely lovely, non-punitive metric — it
  rewards gentleness, which is a rare thing to find in a scoring system and directly usable.)*
- **Rating:** **ESRB E for Everyone.**
