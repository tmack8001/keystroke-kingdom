Final Fantasy VII (Square, January 1997), directed by Yoshinori Kitase, was the first
mainline Final Fantasy with 3D character models over pre-rendered 2D backgrounds and
full-motion video — a project that began in 1994 targeting Super Famicom/Nintendo 64 before
moving to PlayStation for CD-ROM capacity.

## The facts

- Developed and published by Square (now Square Enix), released January 1997 in Japan for
  PlayStation. Director Yoshinori Kitase; producer Hironobu Sakaguchi; composer Nobuo
  Uematsu; character design Tetsuya Nomura.
- First mainline Final Fantasy with 3D character models over pre-rendered 2D backgrounds
  and full-motion video. Development began in 1994, originally targeting Super
  Famicom/Nintendo 64 before moving to PlayStation for CD-ROM capacity.
- Director Kitase has stated in period interviews that the move to 3D-rendered characters
  (replacing the earlier "nitoushin"/chibi sprite style) was intended to achieve "a
  completely unified" visual style now that characters would render in real time.

## Gameplay

The player leads a party of up to three characters (from a larger roster) through an
overworld and dungeons rendered as 3D models over pre-rendered 2D backgrounds, triggering
random or scripted encounters that shift to a dedicated battle screen. Combat runs on the
Active Time Battle (ATB) system: each character (and each enemy) has a gauge that fills
over time, and only once a character's gauge is full can the player open its command menu
to choose Attack, Magic, a Materia-granted command, Summon, or Item (or Defend, or change
row) — turns are not simultaneous or strictly sequential, but paced by each combatant's own
fill rate, which real-time pressure the player can typically pause by opening the menu.
Hit Points (HP) track how much damage a character can take before falling; Magic Points
(MP) are spent casting spells or abilities. Materia — equippable orbs slotted into weapons
and armor — is the primary customization system, granting new spells, summonable
monsters, commands, and stat boosts, and is the mechanic FF7 is most closely associated
with. Taking damage also fills a separate Limit gauge; once full, a character's Attack
command is replaced by a unique, character-specific Limit Break — a powerful signature
move (damage, healing, or buffs) unlocked and leveled up through use. The signature
mechanic the game is remembered for is this combination: ATB's real-time-pressured menu
combat paired with the Materia system as the primary way characters gain and mix
abilities, rather than a fixed class system.
