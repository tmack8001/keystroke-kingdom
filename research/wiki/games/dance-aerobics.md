*Dance Aerobics* (Human Entertainment, **26 February 1987** Japan / **March 1989** North
America, NES + **Power Pad**) is the missing link between [[simon-electronic-game]] and the
rhythm genre proper: an aerobics game played by stepping on a 3×4 floor mat in time with music,
nine years before [[parappa-the-rapper]]. Wikipedia places it as a precursor to "a genre that
would later explode into the mainstream gaming market at the tail end of the 1990s."

## In this game

Two things earn it a mention in [[rhythm-games-by-era]] §2 and nothing more:

1. It is the **first foot-input music game**, eleven years before
   [[dance-dance-revolution]] — the "DDR invented dancing on a mat" belief is wrong by a
   decade.
2. **It shipped a practice mode with nothing at stake.** Alongside its graded "Normal Mode"
   classes it carries an **"Aerobics Mode" for continuous practice with no level progression** —
   an escape hatch from grading, in 1987. The genre has been quietly building these for
   children for nearly forty years ([[rhythm-games-by-era]] §8).

**Does not translate to a keyboard** — the input is whole-body. Only the practice-mode idea
transfers. **What to draw:** an NES, a television, and the Power Pad on the floor
([[nes-hardware]], [[console-hardware-silhouettes]]) — never a cabinet.

**[single-source]** for its own facts: only one page describing this game was reachable this
session; the genre-placement claim is separately corroborated.

## The facts

- **Developer:** Human Entertainment. **Publisher:** Bandai (Japan) / Nintendo (North America).
- **Release:** Japan **26 February 1987**; North America **March 1989**, NES.
- **Peripheral:** the **Power Pad**, a 3×4 dance mat (the Family Trainer / Family Fun Fitness
  line), designed for this and its sibling titles.
- **Play:** follow an on-screen instructor by "stepping on the appropriate buttons on the Power
  Pad as music plays."
- **Three modes:** Normal Mode (aerobics classes of increasing difficulty), Pad Antics
  (composition and memory mini-games), and **Aerobics Mode (continuous practice, no level
  progression)**.
