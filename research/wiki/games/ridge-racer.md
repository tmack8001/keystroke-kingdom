Ridge Racer (Namco, October 1993) is the first arcade video game with 3D **texture-mapped**
graphics and Gouraud shading — the moment polygon surfaces stopped being flat colour and started
carrying pictures — and it built its handling model around **drifting**, borrowed from Japanese
mountain-road car culture. Its 1994 PlayStation port was a launch title that helped Sony beat the
Saturn out of the gate.

## The facts

- Developer/publisher: **Namco**. Arcade release **October 1993** (Japan), **November 30 1993**
  (North America).
- Hardware: **Namco System 22**, capable of texture mapping and Gouraud shading. Ridge Racer is
  "notable for being the first arcade video game with 3D texture-mapped graphics."
- **Signature mechanic — drifting:** the handling model was designed around drifting through
  corners, modelled on "a trend among Japanese car enthusiasts" of racing on mountain roads.
- PlayStation: a launch title in Japan (**December 3 1994**), North America (**September 9
  1995**) and Europe (**September 29 1995**), credited with "playing a major role in establishing
  the new system" and giving Sony an early edge over the Sega Saturn.
- **Punishment mechanics:** a **time limit that ends the race when it counts down to zero**, with
  **checkpoints that grant additional time** when passed — the standard arcade coin contract.
