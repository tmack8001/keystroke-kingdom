Grand Theft Auto (DMA Design, November 1997) began life in March 1995 as **"Race'n'Chase"** — a
design document describing "a fun, addictive, fast-paced multiplayer car racing and crashing
game" — and then deliberately **left the racing genre** when the team decided it was more fun to
play the robbers than the cops. Everything the franchise is famous for happened *after* it
stopped being a racing game.

## In this game

**Recommendation: do not use Grand Theft Auto in Keystroke Kingdom — not as a cabinet, not as a
history card, not as an easter egg.** Trevor named GTA as a modern-era anchor for
**Act 7 — Speed / Racing**; the research advises against it on evidence rather than taste
(full argument: `research/racing-games-by-era.md` §9). Three findings:

1. **Ratings.** Grand Theft Auto III is **ESRB M for Mature 17+** with descriptors **Blood,
   Strong Language, Violence** — outside a game built for a child learning to read.
2. **Regulatory record.** In July 2005 the ESRB **retroactively re-rated** GTA: San Andreas
   **AO (Adults Only 18+)** after finding sexually explicit content in fully rendered form on
   shipped discs; in June 2006 Rockstar and Take-Two **settled FTC charges** for failing to
   disclose it, agreeing to a disabling patch, a $24.5 million re-labelling/recall, mandatory
   content-review systems, and civil penalties up to $11,000 per future violation. The
   easter-egg rules require every reference to be "harmless to a child who stumbles on it."
3. **It isn't even the right genre.** Its own history says so (above).

The one GTA contribution worth teaching — **in-car radio stations** — Keystroke Kingdom already
owns upstream: [[out-run]] (Level 21, 1986) is documented as the in-game radio-selection first
"later adopted by Grand Theft Auto." Recommended substitutes for the slot:
**Midnight Club: Street Racing** (2000, ESRB E) or **[[need-for-speed]]: Underground** (2003,
ESRB E). Note that **Forza Horizon is ESRB T**, not a safe drop-in.

## The facts

- Developer **DMA Design**; published by BMG Interactive (Europe) and ASC Games / Take-Two
  Interactive (North America). MS-DOS/Windows **November 28 1997** (EU) / March 24 1998 (NA);
  PlayStation December 12 1997 (EU) / June 30 1998 (NA); Game Boy Color 1999.
- **Origin:** conceived at DMA Design in March 1995 as **Race'n'Chase**, built on Mike Dailly's
  top-down city engine. The original concept document describes "a fun, addictive, fast-paced
  multiplayer car racing and crashing game," with cannonball-run and demolition-derby modes.
  Creative director **Gary Penn**'s summary of the pivot: *"nobody wants to be the cop, it's more
  fun to be bad."*
- Rendering: top-down, three fictional cities (Liberty City, San Andreas, Vice City).
- Feature of genre consequence: multiple **in-game radio stations** with original music — three
  DMA team members composed tracks recorded in the company's own offices.
- Marketing: publicist **Max Clifford** deliberately used the game's violent content as
  promotional fuel before release.
- Commercial: 1 million shipments by November 1998; 6 million by March 2001.
- Ratings record: **GTA III — ESRB M 17+ (Blood, Strong Language, Violence)**. **GTA: San Andreas
  — re-rated AO on July 20 2005**, the first time a major retail release was retroactively
  reclassified; **FTC settlement June 8 2006**.
