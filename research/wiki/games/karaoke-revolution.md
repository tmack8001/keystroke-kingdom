*Karaoke Revolution* (**Harmonix** developed, **Konami** published, **4 November 2003**,
PlayStation 2) is the first console karaoke game of the modern boom. Lyrics **scroll from the
bottom of the screen to the top** beside a "piano roll representation of the relative pitches,"
and a microphone scores the singer — **on pitch, not on words**, so humming or substituting
lyrics is not penalised.

## In this game

This is the title that makes the karaoke case and, on close reading, also limits it. See
[[karaoke-reading-support]] and [[rhythm-games-by-era]] §9.

- **The screen is the prize.** Words on screen, moving, with the current phrase marked, is a
  reading-support device that requires **no input mapping at all** — the only artefact in the
  genre whose value does not depend on a timing window.
- **The input is unusable.** Karaoke's controller is **the human voice**. There is no keyboard
  mapping for singing, and pretending otherwise would be exactly the kind of gimmick the
  cabinet-art rule forbids. A Keystroke Kingdom "karaoke cabinet" is a screen where the words
  light up **as the child types them** — which is **assisted reading with a keyboard**, not
  karaoke. Name it honestly.
- **Curious detail worth keeping:** it scores **pitch rather than words**, which means the game
  is deliberately indifferent to whether you got the lyric right. A reading game would want the
  exact opposite emphasis — and that inversion is itself the design brief.

**Punishment to strip (One Rule):** a **crowd meter** — *"poor singing results in booing and
game over"* — plus gold-record (12,500 points) and platinum (20,000 of a possible 50,000)
tiers. **Booing a child is the wrong emotional register outright**; this is the one entry in
the ledger that fails on tone before it fails on mechanics. Delete the crowd entirely.

**Credits correction:** commonly attributed wholly to Konami. **Harmonix developed** the
2003–2007 entries (Blitz Games and Hijinx Studios did later ones); Konami published all of
them.

**What to draw:** a PlayStation 2, a television, and a microphone on a stand.

## The facts

- **Developer:** Harmonix (2003–2007 entries). **Publisher:** Konami. **Launch: 4 November
  2003**, PlayStation 2; later Xbox, GameCube, Wii, PS3, Xbox 360.
- **Play:** "The words to the song scroll bottom to top" alongside "a piano roll representation
  of the relative pitches"; a **pitch star** gives real-time accuracy feedback.
- **Pitch, not words:** the game "detects pitch rather than analyzing words, allowing humming or
  alternate lyrics without penalty."
- **Scoring:** each song contains 30–50 individually scored phrases; filling a phrase meter to
  green earns points and consecutive greens build a multiplier. Gold record at 12,500 points,
  platinum at 20,000, out of a 50,000 maximum.
- **Failure:** the crowd meter reflects performance — poor singing produces booing and a game
  over.
- **Recordings:** early versions used **cover recordings of karaoke standards**, explicitly
  unlike competitors using original-artist recordings; the 2009 reboot switched to **all master
  tracks**. See [[music-licensing-in-games]].
