*SingStar* (London Studio / Sony, first release **May 2004**, PlayStation 2) is Sony's karaoke
line: two USB microphones, lyrics on screen under "horizontal grey bars, which function similar
to a musical stave," and pitch scored against the original recording. **It has no traditional
fail state — players complete songs regardless of performance** — which makes it one of only a
handful of commercial rhythm games that is nearly One-Rule-compliant as shipped.

## In this game

Cited in [[rhythm-games-by-era]] §7 and §8 for two reasons:

1. **Proof that the genre can ship without failure.** SingStar scores you and then lets you
   finish, every time. Contrast [[karaoke-revolution]], which boos you off, and
   [[guitar-hero]], which ends the song under you. The remaining problem is the score itself,
   not the structure.
2. **"Pass the Mic" is ensemble play with no loser at the individual level** — up to eight
   people in two teams across multiple rounds. The nearest thing in the genre to a
   parent-and-child-take-turns mode, which is the shape a Keystroke Kingdom music cabinet would
   most naturally take.

**On the reading-support question:** SingStar's lyric display is the same artefact as
[[karaoke-revolution]]'s and the same one evaluated in [[karaoke-reading-support]]. As with
every karaoke title, the **input (voice) does not translate to a keyboard at all** — only the
screen does.

**What to draw:** a PlayStation 2, a television, and the **red and blue USB microphones**.

## The facts

- **Developer:** London Studio. **Publisher:** Sony (Sony Interactive Entertainment).
  **First release: May 2004**, PlayStation 2.
- **Play:** lyrics and pitch guidance on screen — "horizontal grey bars, which function similar
  to a musical stave" with the lyrics beneath. A microphone analyses pitch and timing against
  the original track.
- **Failure:** the game evaluates "how accurate their singing is" but **does not feature a
  traditional fail state** — songs can be completed regardless of performance.
- **Modes:** solo, two-player competitive or duet; **"Pass the Mic"** for up to eight players in
  two teams; an early PS2 single-player "Star Maker" career mode, later discontinued.
- **Audio:** early titles used **master recordings** with the vocals removed; later ones used
  **ADRess** digital vocal-suppression, "successfully remov[ing] the vocal tracks from 80% of
  songs." Music videos play behind the lyrics.
- **Hardware:** red and blue USB microphones sharing a single converter, deliberately leaving
  console ports free for a camera.
