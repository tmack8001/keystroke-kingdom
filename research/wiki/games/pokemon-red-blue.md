Pokémon Red and Green (Game Freak, February 27, 1996, Japan), designed and created by
Satoshi Tajiri with character/art direction by Ken Sugimori, took roughly six years to
develop and nearly bankrupted Game Freak before becoming a generation-defining franchise —
recombined and localized as Pokémon Red and Blue for North America in 1998.

## Gameplay

The player explores the Kanto region from an overhead perspective, walking between towns
and routes that unlock as the story progresses. Wild Pokémon appear randomly while walking
through tall grass, caves, or water; the player can weaken one in battle and throw a Poké
Ball to catch it, adding it to their roster (a completed Pokédex of all 151 is a secondary
long-term goal, aided by trading via the Game Link Cable — impossible solo, since Red and
Blue each withhold species the other version has). Combat is turn-based: on each turn the
player chooses to fight with one of up to four known moves, use an item, switch their
active Pokémon, or attempt to flee, with a rock-paper-scissors-style type system (e.g. Water
beats Fire, Grass beats Water) determining move effectiveness; winning battles earns
experience that levels Pokémon up and can trigger evolution into a stronger form. The
primary progress condition is defeating all eight Gym Leaders for their badges, then the
Elite Four and the Champion at the Pokémon League, to complete the game. The signature
mechanic the game is remembered for is "Gotta catch 'em all" made literal: every wild
encounter is a potential permanent addition to the player's own collection, not just an
obstacle to clear.
