Indianapolis 500: The Simulation (Papyrus Design Group / Electronic Arts, 1989, MS-DOS) is widely
regarded as the personal computer's first true auto-racing simulation — a game where you *set the
car up* (tyres, shocks, wings) and the setup genuinely changes how it handles. It is also the
first game made by the organisation that eventually became [[iracing]].

## In this game

The origin point of the simulation branch documented for **Act 7 — Speed / Racing**

lineage: **1989 Indianapolis 500 → [[nascar-racing-1994]] → [[grand-prix-legends]] → [[iracing]]
(2008)**, all traceable to designer **David Kaemmer**. That single thread is the cleanest possible
narrative spine for reaching Trevor's intended F1-simulation terminal point (see [[sim-racing]]).

## The facts

- Developer: **Papyrus Design Group**; publisher **Electronic Arts**. Designed by **David
  Kaemmer** and **Omar Khudari**. Release **1989** for MS-DOS; Amiga version 1990.
- Simulation features: the player can realistically **set up the car**, and those settings affect
  handling. Adjustments can be made freely during practice with immediate effect; during a race,
  most are only possible during pit stops.
- Replay: six camera angles — In-Car, Behind, Track, TV, Sky and Leader/Crash — reviewing the
  previous 20 seconds of racing.
- Lineage: "the first game developed by the organisation that would later become iRacing."
- **Attribution caution:** the "first true auto racing simulation" framing comes from
  genre-history sources, **not** from Wikipedia's own article. Attribute it as "widely regarded
  as," never as a flat fact. Likewise, detailed physics descriptions (tyre contact patches,
  grip-loss modelling, racing-line enforcement) appear in retrospectives rather than in the
  primary encyclopaedic record.
- **Punishment mechanics:** none unusual for a sim of its era — but note that the *entire design
  philosophy* here is fidelity to a difficult real activity, which is the opposite pole from
  Keystroke Kingdom's One Rule.
