NBA Jam (Midway, April 1993), designed by Mark Turmell, set the all-time US arcade-revenue
record (per Guinness World Records) with its exaggerated two-on-two basketball, digitized
NBA players, and Tim Kitzrow's legendary commentary ("Boomshakalaka!").

## In this game

NBA Jam is not an assigned Keystroke Kingdom cabinet, but its hidden-character easter-egg
history is a useful example for the game's broader easter-egg research (alongside
[[konami-code]]).

## The facts

- Developer: Midway — designed by Mark Turmell. Released April 1993.
- Visual style: digitized sprites from video footage of amateur basketball players mapped
  to NBA player likenesses; exaggerated physics (superhuman dunks); the ball catches fire
  during hot streaks.
- Input: joystick + buttons (shoot/block, pass/steal, turbo); two-player positions per
  cabinet.
- Iconic: Tim Kitzrow's commentary ("He's on fire!" "Boomshakalaka!") — scripted by Jon
  Hey, largely improvised by Kitzrow. Individual cabinets earned up to $2,400/week (per
  GamePro, March 1994), independently confirmed by Guinness World Records as the
  all-time US arcade-revenue record.
- **Correction (Easter egg misattribution):** Bill Clinton and Al Gore appeared as hidden
  characters only in **NBA Jam Tournament Edition's home ports**, not this 1993 coin-op.
  The original arcade version's hidden development-team characters ARE a genuine
  arcade-original feature — the two claims are distinct and should not be conflated.
- Technical: Midway T Unit arcade board, written entirely in assembly language.
