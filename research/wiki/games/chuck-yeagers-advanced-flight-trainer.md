Chuck Yeager's Advanced Flight Trainer (**Electronic Arts, 1987**; developed by **Lerner
Research**, designed by **Ned Lerner**) is the great *civilian* flight sim of the 8-bit home-
computer era: **12 real aircraft and 3 experimental designs** to test-pilot, including the
**Bell X-1** in which Yeager himself first exceeded Mach 1 — with Yeager as technical consultant,
his likeness and voice throughout, and his laconic commentary when you pile it in:
*"You sure bought the farm on that one."*

## In this game

**The recommended 1980s station for the flight act's kid-safe path.** It does everything the era's
combat sims do — real aerodynamics per aircraft, multiple viewpoints, a real aviator's name on the
box — with **no combat**. Its modes are test-piloting, formation flying, stunt flying and racing
against a computer opponent. For an act that has to answer "what is the alternative to
dogfighting?", this is the 1987 answer, and it sold a quarter of a million copies making it.

It also gives the act a **real person** to card, in a game that already loves those
([[ada-lovelace]], [[grace-hopper]], [[margaret-hamilton]], [[katherine-johnson]]).

## The facts

- **Publisher** Electronic Arts; **developer** Lerner Research; **designer** Ned Lerner;
  **1987**, across Amstrad CPC, Apple II, Commodore 64, MS-DOS, Mac, MSX and ZX Spectrum.
- **The name is a lawsuit scar.** It shipped as *Chuck Yeager's Advanced Flight **Simulator***
  and was **pulled from shelves and renamed** after a legal dispute with **Microsoft over the
  "Flight Simulator" trademark**. A nice, true, slightly absurd fact — and a precision trap: both
  titles are "correct," for different weeks in 1987.
- **Yeager's role:** technical consultant, likeness and voice. The crash commentary is the
  thing everyone remembers.
- **Content:** 12 real aircraft plus 3 experimental designs, each with **different aerodynamics**;
  **10 viewpoints** inside and outside the aircraft with zoom up to 256×; test-piloting, formation
  flight, stunt flight, and racing a computer opponent. **Not a combat game.**
- **Commercial:** 100,000 copies by December 1987; **Software Publishers Association "Platinum"**
  certification in **May 1988** for sales above 250,000.
- **Punishment mechanics:** you can crash, and Yeager will say something dry about it. That is the
  entire penalty — no lives, no campaign to lose. It is, by accident, one of the gentlest failure
  states in the era (see the ledger in `research/flight-simulators-by-era.md` §9).
