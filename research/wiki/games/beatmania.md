*beatmania* (Konami G.M.D., **10 December 1997**, arcade) is the game that invented the
**falling-note highway** — icons cascading down vertical lanes toward a judgement line near the
bottom of the screen — and gave Konami's entire music-game division its name: **Bemani**. Its
controller is **five plastic keys arranged in a zigzag and coloured like piano keys**, plus a
turntable to the right. It precedes [[dance-dance-revolution]] by nine months, which makes the
common belief that DDR started Konami's rhythm line wrong by a game and a year.

## In this game

Two reasons it matters to [[rhythm-games-by-era]], one encouraging and one disqualifying.

- **Encouraging:** its controller is the **closest physical analogue to a computer keyboard in
  the entire genre** — five keys, in two rows, deliberately laid out like piano keys. If any
  commercial rhythm cabinet was ever nearly a keyboard, it was this one
  ([[rhythm-on-a-keyboard]]).
- **Disqualifying:** it is the origin point of the mechanic Keystroke Kingdom cannot have.
  **The note highway exists for exactly one purpose: to make a deadline legible in advance.**
  Strip the deadline and the display has nothing to show. The turntable does not translate to a
  keyboard gesture either and should be dropped rather than faked.

**Punishment to strip (One Rule):** an audience-satisfaction **groove gauge** — red passing
region on the right, green failing range on the left — that can **end the song early** when it
empties (operator-configurable), plus per-note grading **p-great / great / good / bad / poor**.
Both the meter and the per-note grade are out. If a fill-meter is wanted at all, take
[[taiko-no-tatsujin]]'s version instead, which only ever fills.

**What to draw:** the real cabinet — five zigzag piano-style keys and a turntable.

## The facts

- **Developer:** Konami G.M.D. **Release: 10 December 1997**, arcade.
- **Controller:** "five plastic vertical rectangular keys that are arranged in a zigzag
  pattern," coloured like piano keys (white on the lower row, black above), with a **turntable**
  to the right for scratching.
- **Display:** rectangular icons cascade down vertical bars toward "a horizontal line near the
  bottom of the screen"; the player must hit each as it arrives.
- **Judgement:** p-great (from "perfect great," also called "flashing great"), great, good, bad,
  poor.
- **Groove gauge:** an audience-satisfaction bar with a red passing region and a green failing
  range; the game **can end prematurely** if it empties, depending on machine settings.
- **Bemani:** "The Bemani line of music games from Konami is named after the series, was first
  adopted in the arcade release of beatmania 3rdMix and kept ever since."
