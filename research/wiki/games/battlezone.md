Battlezone (**Atari, November 1980**) is the first-person vector-graphics tank game that is widely
called **the first true 3D first-person arcade game** — wireframe tanks and terrain drawn by a
vector beam on a black-and-white tube with a coloured plastic overlay, viewed through a
**periscope** hood. The US Army later commissioned a variant, **the Bradley Trainer**; lead
designer **Ed Rotberg** agreed to work on it *only* after management promised he would never be
asked to do a military project again.

## In this game

**Documented, recommended AGAINST as a cabinet.** It is a vehicle game, it is era-perfect, and its
vector rendering is a genuinely missing technique in Keystroke Kingdom's vocabulary — but the
vehicle is a **tank**, the verb is **destroy enemy tanks**, and the most interesting thing about it
is a **military trainer**. That is three strikes on register for a game built for a child. If the
vector-graphics era is wanted, **[[lunar-lander-1979]] supplies it** — same year, same Atari, same
technique, and the subject is landing on the Moon instead of shooting.

The Rotberg story is, however, a first-rate **parent-facing** note about conscience in engineering,
adjacent to the hacker-ethic material in `research/hacker-culture.md`.

## The facts

- **Release November 1980**, Atari, Inc. (Sega and Taito distributed in Japan). Designers
  **Ed Rotberg, Owen Rubin and Roger Hector**; programming by **Morgan Hoff** and Rotberg.
  **Date caution:** a widely circulated figure is **June 1, 1980**; Wikipedia's article gives
  **November 1980.** The two do not agree — use "1980" unless a KLOV/Strong record settles it.
  *(arcade-museum.com returned **HTTP 403** to fetch this pass and could not arbitrate.)*
- **Rendering:** **wireframe vector** 3D on a black-and-white monitor with a **coloured overlay**.
  *"Considered to be the first true 3D arcade game that has a first-person perspective"* and *"the
  first big 3D success in the video game industry."*
- **Cabinet:** a **periscope viewfinder** that restricted the view, borrowed from submarine-themed
  arcade machines; a later cabinet dropped it for visibility.
- **The Bradley Trainer:** Atari built a customised variant for **US Army gunner training on
  Bradley Fighting Vehicles**. **Only two units were produced** — one delivered to the Army
  (presumed lost), one in a private collection. Several Atari developers, **Ed Rotberg** most
  notably, **initially refused to work on it** on anti-military grounds; he agreed only on the
  condition he would never be assigned a military project again.
- **Sales:** approximately **15,000 cabinets**.
