Age of Empires (Ensemble Studios, October 15, 1997) was one of the first history-themed
(rather than sci-fi/fantasy) real-time strategy games, built on the in-house "Genie" engine
and named 1998 "Computer Strategy Game of the Year" by the Academy of Interactive Arts &
Sciences.

## The facts

- Developed by Ensemble Studios, published by Microsoft. Released October 15, 1997 — first
  major Ensemble release, built on their in-house "Genie" engine (programmer Matt
  Pritchard). Design credits: Bruce Shelley, Rick Goodman, Tony Goodman (art), Dave
  Pottinger (AI).
- One of the first history-themed (rather than sci-fi/fantasy) RTS games. Named 1998
  "Computer Strategy Game of the Year" by the Academy of Interactive Arts & Sciences.

## Gameplay

The player starts each match with a Town Center and a handful of villagers, controlling
one of twelve ancient civilizations (Egyptians, Greeks, Babylonians, Shang Chinese, and
others) in real time. Villagers are the economic engine: they cut wood, mine gold and
stone, and hunt/forage/farm/fish for food, and can also construct and repair buildings.
Accumulating enough resources lets the player "age up" through four historical stages —
Stone, Tool, Bronze, and Iron Age — each unlocking new buildings, units, and technologies
on the game's tech tree (military upgrades, economic upgrades, religious upgrades,
infrastructure upgrades). Resources funneled into barracks, archery ranges, and stables
train military units — infantry, archers, cavalry, siege weapons — in a rock-paper-scissors
matchup web (e.g. spearmen countering cavalry) that rewards scouting the opponent and
reacting. Victory is most commonly achieved by defeating all opponents, though building
and defending a Wonder for a set span (2,000 in-game years, roughly 10 real-time minutes)
is an alternate win condition. The signature mechanic the game is remembered for is
pairing a real-time economy-building loop with historical (rather than sci-fi/fantasy)
civilizations and a four-Age tech-tree structure — establishing the template Age of
Empires II and the broader historical-RTS genre would refine.
