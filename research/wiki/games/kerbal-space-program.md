Kerbal Space Program (**Squad**, out of beta **27 April 2015**) is a spaceflight simulation game
with a **realistic orbital physics engine** — real manoeuvres like **Hohmann transfer** and
**bi-elliptic transfer** orbits work because the physics are actually modelled — wrapped in
cheerful green cartoon astronauts who take failure extremely well. **ESRB E.**

## In this game

**The recommended modern station of the space arc's upper half.** It is the rare title that is
simultaneously (a) genuinely educational about orbital mechanics, (b) **ESRB E**, and (c) tonally
correct for a child — its failures are comic, not tragic, which is the exact register the One Rule
demands. It is the orbital counterpart to what [[pilotwings]] is in the atmosphere: a curriculum
disguised as a toy.

## The facts

- **Developer/publisher:** Squad. Full release **27 April 2015**, after a long public early-access
  period; Windows, macOS, Linux, PlayStation 4 and Xbox One.
- **Physics:** a realistic orbital physics engine supporting real orbital manoeuvres including
  **Hohmann transfer orbits** and **bi-elliptic transfer orbits**.
- **Rating:** **ESRB E (Everyone)**.
- **Educational standing:** in **2022** Boeing sent Kerbal character dolls to the **International
  Space Station** in acknowledgement of the game's contribution to **STEM education**.
  *(NASA endorsement specifically was **[unverified]** in this pass — searches surfaced the Boeing/
  ISS item but no NASA endorsement document. Do not claim NASA endorses it.)*
- **Punishment mechanics:** rockets explode, constantly and hilariously, and the game's own
  personality treats this as the expected outcome of experimenting. There is no permadeath
  requirement in the default sandbox mode. **This is the friendliest failure model in the whole
  vehicle-games survey** and is worth studying rather than stripping.
