F1 25 (Codemasters / EA Sports, **May 30 2025**, PS5 / Xbox Series X|S / PC) is the current
entry in the official Formula One game series Codemasters has produced since 2009. It is the
recommended **terminal station of Act 7** for a reason that is pleasantly unusual: the most
technically serious racing simulation in the arc is also, by rating, **the most
age-appropriate game in it** — **ESRB E for Everyone**.

## In this game

**The last station of Act 7 — Speed / Racing** (`research/racing-games-by-era.md` §11, §15).
Trevor's instinct was to end the act on a modern F1 simulation; the research confirms it twice
over.

1. **It is historically the right ending.** The genre forks in 1989 into arcade racers and
   simulations ([[indianapolis-500-the-simulation]]), and the simulation branch terminates in
   software real teams and drivers use. F1 25 is the licensed, current end of the line that
   began with [[formula-one-grand-prix]] (Geoff Crammond, 1991) — the first authentic simulation
   of the F1 World Championship. Act 7 can draw a single straight line from
   [[monaco-gp]] (1979, a cabinet with no processor in it) to F1 25 (2025, circuits measured
   by laser). **Both are Grand Prix cars.** The act opens and closes on the same sport, 46 years
   apart, and the only thing that changed is how much computer sits behind it.
2. **The rating is clean, and verified on the record.** **E for Everyone**; descriptors
   **Mild Language** and **Use of Alcohol**; interactive elements **Users Interact** and
   **In-Game Purchases**. Two caveats worth a parent's eye, quoted from ESRB's own summary:
   the mild profanity is *"damn"* and *"hell"*, and the alcohol reference is **champagne on the
   podium** — which is a real thing about the sport rather than a thing about the game. (Act 7's
   own celebrations should note this and simply not do it — see
   [[mario-kart]], whose SNES podium has the same champagne beat.) The **In-Game Purchases**
   flag is a storefront, not content, and is irrelevant to a station Keystroke Kingdom is
   merely *depicting*.

## The facts

- Developer **Codemasters**, publisher **EA Sports**. **Released May 30 2025** for
  **PlayStation 5, Xbox Series X|S and PC**.
- Codemasters has been the official Formula 1 game developer **since 2009**; **EA acquired
  Codemasters in a ~$1.2bn deal completed February 2021**.
- **LiDAR-scanned circuits — five, new for this entry: Bahrain, Miami, Melbourne, Suzuka and
  Imola**, joining Silverstone. The scan data was captured **during actual Grand Prix
  weekends**, and captures "tiny asphalt surface details," **kerb heights** and **distance to
  barriers**. Vegetation is placed from the scan too — **Suzuka's cherry blossoms and
  Melbourne's parkland are the real trees, in the real places**.
- **Reverse track layouts for the first time in an official F1 game**: Silverstone, Zandvoort
  and the Red Bull Ring (Austria), with **DRS zones, pit-lane entries, AI training and marshal
  placement all reworked** for the reversed flow — not mirrored copies.
- **Path tracing on PC** — indirect lighting from light bouncing off surfaces; Codemasters
  describes it as "a big step up in terms of visual fidelity." Senior Creative Director
  **Lee Mather** on the scanning: *"LiDAR tracks are a win across the board for us."*
- **ESRB: E for Everyone.** Descriptors **Mild Language, Use of Alcohol**. Interactive
  elements **Users Interact, In-Game Purchases**. Modes named on the record: Career, League
  Racing, F1 World, Grand Prix.
- The wider series is consistently E-rated: **F1 23** and **F1 24** are also **E for Everyone
  (Mild Language)**.

## The cabinet build (Act 7 track spec)

**Render tier: `modern`.** `gradient`, `bloom`, `particles` — and the only tier the kit permits
a WebGL backend to serve. This station is where the act's whole graphic-quality ladder cashes
out, and it should be **unmistakably more beautiful than everything before it**, exactly the way
product.md §5 describes the main curriculum's arc.

**Rendering technique.** Physically-based materials, real-time global illumination (path
tracing on PC), high dynamic range, volumetric spray in the wet, heat shimmer, reflections that
carry the whole environment across a carbon-fibre bodyshell, and geometry derived from
**laser-measured reality** rather than modelled by eye. For a canvas renderer this is out of
reach as photorealism, and chasing it is the wrong move — `racing-games-by-era.md` §13 already
recommends the honest alternative: **a telemetry-trace aesthetic**. Draw what the *engineers*
see. Line graphs, data channels, a delta bar, a racing-line trace on a scanned track outline,
a tyre-temperature gradient. That is genuinely what distinguishes a simulation from an arcade
racer ([[sim-racing]]), it is reproducible with `gradient` and `particles`, and it looks like
nothing else in the act.

**Road / track geometry.** Real circuits, measured. The thing to render is **the racing line on
a surveyed track map** — elevation changes that are actually there, kerbs at their actual
height, barriers at their actual distance. And the act has a free gift here: **reverse layouts**
are a documented F1 25 feature, which means a station that runs its own track **backwards** is
era-accurate rather than a gimmick.

**The signature visual moment.** An F1 car at night or in the wet, **spray lit from behind by
the car following**, with a **telemetry overlay** running beside it. Or, quieter and better for
this game: the **LiDAR point cloud of a circuit resolving into the circuit** — a real thing
that really happens in the production pipeline, and a perfect closing image for an act that
began with a machine made of 100 logic chips.

**Completion / celebration state.** The podium — and this is where the act has to make a
choice. Real F1's podium is **placement, a trophy and champagne**: three things Act 7 has
already removed everywhere else. The honest reframe is the one the sport itself provides:
the **cool-down lap** and the **parc fermé** moment where the driver climbs out and the team
mobs them. Nobody is ranked in that picture; a group of people are simply pleased. Pair it
with the [[daytona-usa]] victory lap — *go round once more, slowly, with everyone watching* —
and the act's final celebration is both era-true and One-Rule-native.

**Punishment mechanics to strip.**

| What the game does | Why it violates the One Rule | Reframe |
|---|---|---|
| Championship **points and standings** | The entire sport is a ranking | No standings. The reward is the lap you drove |
| **Penalties** — time penalties, drive-throughs, grid drops for track-limit violations | A rules system that punishes | Track limits are a *guide*; going wide costs nothing |
| **Damage and retirement** — a race can simply end | Failure state | Nothing retires. The car always continues |
| **Tyre wear and fuel** as depleting resources | Attrition; the car gets worse the longer you play | If modelled at all, purely as a **visual** (colour change on a tyre graphic), never as a cost |
| Qualifying setting grid position | Same gate [[pole-position]] had in 1982 | Never gate. Qualifying is a warm-up you cannot fail |
| **Champagne on the podium** (ESRB's own Use of Alcohol descriptor) | Wrong content for a child's game | The celebration is the team, the cool-down lap, the crowd — no bottle |
| **In-Game Purchases** | Monetisation | Trivially inapplicable; noted only so nothing about it is ever depicted |

**Cabinet art notes.** No cabinet — a 2025 console/PC title. If Act 7 gives this station an
object, the faithful one is **a modern direct-drive wheel-and-pedal rig**, the consumer end of
the force-feedback lineage `racing-games-by-era.md` §11 traces. **Do not reproduce real team
liveries, sponsor marks or the F1 logo** — the same trademark judgement
`research/automotive-vocabulary.md` reached about car brands applies with more force here,
since this is an actively licensed property. Draw **a** Formula car, not **a** team's car.

**Era-appropriate palette.** Broadcast Formula 1: **deep asphalt grey-blue** rather than black,
**white and red kerbing**, **acid-green run-off** (the real colour of modern track paint),
**pure-white line markings**, and a car in one saturated hue with **carbon black** under it and
**hot-white brake glow** at the discs. The UI layer — the telemetry aesthetic above — runs on
**near-black with a single cyan or lime data accent and fine light-grey type**. Everything
blooms slightly; nothing is flat. Held beside [[monaco-gp]]'s one white ink on pure black, this
is the picture of the whole act.

## Corrections from citation pass (2026-09-13)

1. **"A modern F1 game is ESRB E with only Mild Language."** True of **F1 23** and **F1 24**.
   **F1 25 adds a second descriptor: `Use of Alcohol`** — podium champagne. Still E, still the
   most age-appropriate terminal station available, but the descriptor list changed between
   entries and the earlier research's flat "E (Mild Language)" would now be out of date. **State
   the year and the descriptors together.**
2. **This page is deliberately perishable.** It documents the *current* entry in an annual
   series (precedent: `research/wiki/tech/ev-makers-2026.md`). A future F1 26/27 will need its
   own ratings check — **do not assume the E carries forward**, which is exactly the mistake
   corrected in item 1.
3. **F1 25's reverse layouts are real and first-of-kind for the official series.** Worth stating
   precisely: they are **reworked**, not mirrored — DRS zones, pit entries, AI and marshals were
   all re-authored. An Act 7 "run it backwards" idea is therefore era-accurate, not a liberty.
