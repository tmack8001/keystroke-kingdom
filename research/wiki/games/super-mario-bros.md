Super Mario Bros (Nintendo, September 13, 1985), directed and designed by Shigeru Miyamoto
with Takashi Tezuka, is the NES side-scrolling platformer widely credited — alongside the
NES itself — with reviving the video game industry after the North American market crash of
1983.

## In this game

Super Mario Bros is the **Level 9 cabinet** in Act 2 (The Computer Wing), teaching W and O.
It occupies the center of the room — "arguably the single most important game ever made."
Its full NES palette (up to 52 colors available) and smooth parallax side-scrolling mark a
deliberate visual step up from [[tetris]]'s minimalism. See `.claude/rules/levels.md` Level
9 and the [[nes-hardware]] concept page.

## The facts

- Directed/designed by Shigeru Miyamoto, co-designed with Takashi Tezuka, programmed by
  Toshihiko Nakago. Released in Japan September 13, 1985, for the Famicom/NES.
- The "saved the industry" framing is the accepted historical narrative, specifically
  referring to the North American video game crash of 1983 — Super Mario Bros. and the NES
  are widely credited with reviving the market.
