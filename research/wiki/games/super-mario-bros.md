Super Mario Bros (Nintendo, September 13, 1985), directed and designed by Shigeru Miyamoto
with Takashi Tezuka, is the NES side-scrolling platformer widely credited — alongside the
NES itself — with reviving the video game industry after the North American market crash of
1983.

## In this game

Super Mario Bros is the **Level 9 cabinet** in Act 2 (The Computer Wing), teaching W and O.
It occupies the center of the room — "arguably the single most important game ever made."
Its full NES palette (up to 52 colors available) and smooth parallax side-scrolling mark a
deliberate visual step up from [[tetris]]'s minimalism. See `.claude/rules/levels.md` Level
9 and the [[nes-hardware]] concept page.

## The facts

- Directed/designed by Shigeru Miyamoto, co-designed with Takashi Tezuka, programmed by
  Toshihiko Nakago. Released in Japan September 13, 1985, for the Famicom/NES.
- The "saved the industry" framing is the accepted historical narrative, specifically
  referring to the North American video game crash of 1983 — Super Mario Bros. and the NES
  are widely credited with reviving the market.

## Gameplay

The player runs and jumps Mario (or Luigi) rightward through side-scrolling stages,
crossing gaps and stomping on enemies like Goombas and Koopa Troopas to defeat them —
jump height scales with how long the button is held, and a running start extends jump
distance. Three power-ups transform Mario mid-run: the Super Mushroom grows him to
"Super Mario" (able to break bricks and survive one extra hit), the Fire Flower lets him
throw bouncing fireballs, and the Starman grants ~10 seconds of invincibility that defeats
enemies on contact. Each of a stage's three sub-levels ends at a flagpole — touching it
slides Mario down and completes the level, with height-on-the-pole awarding bonus points
(up to 5,000) — while the fourth, castle-set sub-level replaces the flagpole with an axe
that drops a (usually decoy) Bowser into lava below. The whole game spans 8 worlds × 4
levels toward rescuing Princess Peach from Bowser in the Mushroom Kingdom. The signature
mechanic the game is remembered for is this power-up transformation loop (small → Super →
Fire, lost instantly on taking a hit) layered onto pure run-and-jump platforming — the
template nearly every side-scrolling platformer since has iterated on.
