*Taiko no Tatsujin* (Namco / Bandai Namco, **21 February 2001**, arcade) is played on a
**simulated taiko drum struck with bachi sticks** and uses only **two inputs**: a red **don**
note (hit the drum face) and a blue **ka** note (hit the rim). It is the gentlest game in the
rhythm canon by a clear margin, and it contains the single most directly adoptable idea in this
entire research area: **"missing a note does not result in off-key sounds or interruptions to
the background track."**

## In this game

Two findings put this page near the top of [[rhythm-games-by-era]]'s recommendations.

1. **Two inputs, two hands.** Don and ka are left-hand and right-hand. That is **Level 1 —
   J and F** with a drum skin on it. Of every game in the genre, this is the one whose input
   maps onto Keystroke Kingdom's curriculum with no distortion at all
   ([[rhythm-on-a-keyboard]]).
2. **Namco decided in 2001 that the song should not punish you.** In [[guitar-hero]] a missed
   note makes an ugly sound; in [[rock-band]] a failing player's instrument is **muted out of
   the mix in front of their friends**. Taiko does neither — the music simply continues. This is
   the exact audio analogue of *the letter stays on screen and waits*, and it is the
   proof-of-existence that failure and music can be decoupled.

**Punishment to strip (One Rule) — the mildest entry in the ledger:** a **spirit gauge** that
must be **filled past a target** to clear a song. Note the direction: it is a **fill** meter, not
a drain meter, so it is already half-inverted relative to [[beatmania]]'s and
[[dance-dance-revolution]]'s life bars. **Delete only the clear-threshold** and keep the fill as
a plain progress bar, and this mechanic is compliant.

**What to draw:** the real cabinet, with its drum and bachi sticks.

**History-card candidate** (proposal only, not built): moment word `drum` — *"Taiko no Tatsujin
let you miss a note and the song kept playing anyway. The music never scolded you."*

## The facts

- **Developer / publisher:** Namco (now Bandai Namco Entertainment). **Debut: 21 February
  2001**, arcade.
- **Controller:** simulated taiko drums "that register hits when struck with drumsticks
  (bachi)." Console versions use buttons, touchscreen drums, or a separately sold drum
  peripheral.
- **Notes:** red **don** (strike the face) and blue **ka** (strike the rim), plus rapid-hit
  bars, balloon notes and bonus markers.
- **Clear condition:** the **spirit gauge** must be filled beyond a target through accuracy by
  the end of the song.
- **The decoupling:** "Unlike other rhythm games, missing a note does not result in off-key
  sounds or interruptions to the background track."
- **Difficulties:** Easy, Normal, Hard, and **Oni** (released in English as "Extreme"); some
  entries add a fifth, **Ura Oni**.
- **Reach:** **over 10 million console copies sold as of 2019**, across two decades — one of
  only two rhythm franchises to come through the genre's 2009–2011 collapse intact (the other
  being [[just-dance]]).
