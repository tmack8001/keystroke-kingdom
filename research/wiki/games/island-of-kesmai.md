Island of Kesmai (CompuServe, launched December 1985), created by John Taylor and
Kelton Flinn — who had written the six-player prototype "Dungeons of Kesmai" together
at the University of Virginia in 1980 and founded the Kesmai company in 1982 — is a
persistent, ASCII/pseudo-graphical, overhead-view multiplayer dungeon crawl commonly
cited as a direct forerunner of modern MMORPGs including EverQuest. It is the
strongest candidate found this session for a memory combining **ASCII/overhead
display**, **genuine persistent multiplayer**, and a **dialup-era commercial online
service** — but its cooperative-party social structure was not a formal named
guild/clan system (that arrived with its 1996 graphical sequel, [[legends-of-kesmai]]).

**Strongest ASCII+multiplayer+dialup match; weakest on the specific "clans you

is the only one that is simultaneously: true ASCII/roguelike pseudo-graphics on an
overhead grid (closest text-mode kin to [[nethack]] and [[mangband]], not a sprite/tile
game), a genuinely shared persistent world (not solo-with-spectators, unlike NetHack),
and reachable from home over a dial-up modem to a commercial service in the right era
(1985, predating even Trevor's likely play window, but the game ran for over a decade).
Where it falls short of the brief: Wikipedia and a period fetch of a retrospective
piece describe strong **cooperative party** culture (players banding together for
quests requiring multiple characters, a "conference room" for strategizing) but not a
formal, named guild/clan/faction system — that specific mechanic is documented for
the 1996 graphical sequel [[legends-of-kesmai]] instead. If Trevor's memory
specifically features **joining a named clan/guild**, Legends of Kesmai (2D sprites,
not ASCII) or a graphical MMORPG like [[neverwinter-nights-1991]] or Meridian 59 is
the stronger match on that single cue — see the ranked comparison page for the full

## The facts

- **Origin:** John Taylor and Kelton Flinn, University of Virginia classmates, wrote
  "Dungeons of Kesmai" in summer 1980 — a six-player game inspired by Dungeons &
  Dragons, using Roguelike ASCII graphics. They founded the Kesmai company in 1982.
- **Launch:** Island of Kesmai, an enhanced version, launched on CompuServe in 1985.
- **World scale:** a persistent fantasy world of "five diverse lands encompassing
  62,000 hexes and over 2,500 creatures," supporting up to 100 simultaneous players.
- **Display:** the screen was divided into quadrants — a status window (character
  state), a pseudo-graphical overhead view of the character's immediate surroundings,
  a text area for environment descriptions, and a command line for player orders.
  Characters were rendered as ASCII symbols (`>` the player, `A` monsters, `B` other
  players, `$` treasure, plus wall/door glyphs) — the same visual grammar family as
  [[roguelike]] games, but multiplayer and persistent rather than single-player.
- **Access method — CompuServe, not raw telnet:** players logged into CompuServe and
  selected Island of Kesmai from its menu; dedicated third-party front-end clients
  (IoKTerm, TheGuide, KPlus, requiring COM/IP redirector software) existed to improve
  the interface, distinct from typing a bare `telnet` command at a terminal prompt.
  CompuServe access cost $6/hour at 300 baud or $12/hour at 1200 baud in this era —
  meaningfully different economics from a flat-rate telnet BBS/MUD connection.
- **Cooperative, not formally clan-based:** sources describe players forming
  adventuring parties, using a shared "conference room" to strategize, and quests
  "deliberately designed to require the cooperation of several characters" — genuine
  social/cooperative multiplayer, but not documented as a named guild/clan/faction
  system in Island of Kesmai itself.
- **Lineage forward:** Wikipedia credits Island of Kesmai's questing systems as "a
  significant aspect of the wildly popular future MMORPGs such as EverQuest and World
  of Warcraft" — directly supporting the "reminded him of a really early version of
  EverQuest" cue in the brief. Its 1996 graphical sequel is [[legends-of-kesmai]],
  which added the documented guild system. Both were retired commercially in 2000
  after Electronic Arts acquired Kesmai in 1999.
