The Commodore 64, launched at CES in January 1982 at $595, holds the Guinness World
Record for best-selling desktop computer of all time — with total unit-sales estimates
ranging widely across sources, from a "credible modern estimate" of 12.5 million to
Commodore's and its founder's own claims of 17–30 million.

## The facts

- **January 1982:** the Commodore 64 debuts at CES at a $595 launch price.
- Guinness World Records credits it as the best-selling desktop computer, citing a
  "credible modern estimate" of 12.5 million units, while noting Commodore's own claim
  of 17 million and founder Jack Tramiel's claim of 22–30 million.
- The Computer History Museum states sales "totaled close to 17 million units, making it
  the best-selling single personal computer model of all time."
- Verdict: accurate. "Best-selling computer of all time" matches the actual Guinness
  World Records title. The exact unit count is genuinely contested (12.5M–30M range) —
  the card sidesteps this by not citing a specific number, the right call. No change
  needed.
